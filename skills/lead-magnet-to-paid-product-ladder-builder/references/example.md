# Example Output: Lead Magnet to Paid Product Ladder Builder

Two complete walkthroughs: a freelance video editor newsletter building a ladder from zero, and a productivity YouTuber auditing a broken two-product setup.

---

## Example 1: Freelance Video Editor Newsletter — Building From Zero

### Input

```
I don't have any digital products yet. Here's my situation:

Niche/content: I run a newsletter called "Cut & Dry" for freelance video editors trying to
go full-time. 6,000 subscribers, weekly issue, mostly about client work and pricing.

Audience: Freelance video editors, mostly 1-3 years in, still doing a mix of platform gigs
(Fiverr, Upwork) and direct clients. Very engaged in reply threads whenever I write about
pricing or scope creep.

Biggest pain point: They constantly undercharge and let scope creep eat their margins. Almost
every reply thread about a botched client relationship traces back to no contract or a vague
one.

What I can realistically produce: Writing, templates, spreadsheets. No video production
capacity right now, no community platform set up.

Run the Niche & Monetization Diagnostic, then build me the full ladder.
```

---

### Output: Monetization Gap Diagnosis

**Monetization Gap Diagnosis: Cut & Dry**

**Audience snapshot:** 6,000 newsletter subscribers, freelance video editors 1-3 years in, most engaged in reply threads about pricing and scope creep.

**Core audience pain point:** They undercharge and get scope-crept because they don't have (or don't enforce) real contracts and rate logic.

---

**What's Missing**

You have zero products. Every reply thread you get about pricing and contracts is a direct signal of demand you're not capturing — people are telling you exactly what they'd pay for, in public, for free. Right now that engagement just evaporates.

**Root Cause**

This isn't a "I don't know what to build" problem — you clearly know the pain point. It's a "I haven't built anything yet" problem, likely because pricing/contracts feels like a small, unglamorous topic compared to a full course. That instinct is wrong: it's exactly narrow enough to be a great lead magnet and tripwire.

**Recommended Starting Point**

Build the full ladder from Tool 2, starting with the lead magnet. Given your pain point (contracts and scope creep) and your production capacity (writing, templates, spreadsheets — no video), this ladder should be entirely document-based. That's a strength here, not a limitation — it means you can build all four tiers without new skills or tools.

---

### Output: Four-Tier Value Ladder

**Value Ladder: Cut & Dry**

**Core pain point this ladder solves:** Freelance video editors undercharge and get scope-crept because they don't have real contracts or defensible rate logic.

---

**Tier 1: Lead Magnet (Free)**

**Product concept:** The Scope Creep Firewall — a one-page pre-project checklist

**Format:** Checklist (PDF, 1 page)

**The 10-minute promise:** Before their next client call, they fill in project scope, revision limits, and a kill-switch clause in plain language. They walk away with one paragraph they can paste directly into a client email that sets revision limits before work starts.

**Why this tier exists:** Removes "does this newsletter writer actually know contracts, or just complain about bad clients like everyone else." The checklist proves the advice is specific and usable, not general sympathy.

**What happens if you skip this tier:** You'd be asking cold subscribers to pay for a full contract template with zero proof it's better than what they'd find free on Google.

---

**Tier 2: Tripwire Offer ($7–$27)**

**Product concept:** The Editor's Contract Kit — a fill-in-the-blank client contract + rate calculator spreadsheet

**Price:** $19 — high enough to filter for people who are serious about fixing this, low enough that it's an easy "yes" for someone who just used the free checklist and felt the gap

**Format:** One Word/Google Docs contract template with annotated clauses, one Google Sheets rate calculator

**The obvious next step:** The free checklist gives them language for one conversation. It doesn't give them an actual signable contract or a way to calculate what to charge in the first place. This tier finishes both.

**Why this tier exists:** Removes "will paying this newsletter writer for something actually be worth it." $19 is low enough that saying yes doesn't require deep trust — it just requires believing the free checklist was useful, which they already know.

**What happens if you skip this tier:** You'd ask a first-time buyer to jump from $0 straight to a $79+ flagship, which is a much bigger leap than most first-time buyers make.

---

**Tier 3: Mid-Tier Flagship ($47–$150)**

**Product concept:** The Full-Time Freelancer Toolkit — complete client management system (contract kit + rate calculator + client intake form + scope-change invoice template + a written pricing playbook)

**Price:** $79 — priced as "replaces a $150+/hr consulting call," positioned as the complete system rather than one document

**Format:** Template + toolkit bundle (5 components), delivered as a Google Drive folder they duplicate

**The obvious next step:** Tripwire buyers now have a contract and know how to calculate rates for one project type. This tier gives them the system for their whole business — intake, mid-project scope changes, and the pricing logic behind all of it.

**Why this tier exists:** Removes "is this just fragments, or does this person actually have a complete method." This is your main revenue tier — proven buyer at this point, meaningful price, full solution to the core pain point of undercharging and scope creep across their whole freelance practice.

**What happens if you skip this tier:** Tripwire buyers who want more have nowhere to go except a $300+ premium ask, so most of them just stop buying from you.

---

**Tier 4: Premium Offer ($200+)**

**Product concept:** Rate Review — a 45-minute 1:1 async video teardown of their actual pricing, current contract, and one real client scenario

**Price:** $249

**Format:** They submit their current rates/contract + one scenario via a form; you record a personalized video response within 5 business days

**The obvious next step:** Flagship buyers now have the system, but many will hesitate on whether they're applying it correctly to their specific niche (motion graphics vs. YouTube long-form vs. corporate) and client mix. This tier gets them personal application instead of a general template.

**Why this tier exists:** Removes "I have the framework but I don't know if I'm using it right for MY situation." Sells direct access and judgment, not new information — they already own the Toolkit.

**What happens if you skip this tier:** You cap revenue per customer at $79 and lose your highest-intent buyers — the ones who'd happily pay more for personal attention — to a coach who does offer it.

## Example 2: Productivity YouTuber — Auditing a Broken Ladder

### Input

```
I already sell some things but I don't think I have a real ladder. Here's what I have:

Product 1: A free "Weekly Planning Template" (Notion) — collected on my website, no email
follow-up sequence after signup
Product 2: "The Deep Work System" — a full course, $297, video modules + workbook

My audience's core pain point: They know what they should be doing (deep work, planning)
but can't stick with any system for more than 2 weeks.

Run the Ladder Audit & Gap Finder.
```

---

### Output: Ladder Audit Report

**Ladder Audit: Productivity YouTuber**

**Current Ladder (as it exists today)**

| Tier | Product | Price | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Lead Magnet | Weekly Planning Template (Notion) | Free | Exists, but no follow-up sequence |
| 2 — Tripwire | — | — | MISSING |
| 3 — Flagship | — | — | MISSING |
| 4 — Premium | The Deep Work System | $297 | Exists, mismatched position |

---

**Diagnosis**

**Problem: Missing rung between free and $297**

There is nothing between "free template" and "$297 course." Someone who downloads the planning template and likes it has exactly one paid option, and it's a $297 jump from zero — no tier building trust in between. This is the single biggest reason the course underperforms relative to your audience size.

**Fix:** Build a tripwire ($7-27) and ideally a flagship ($47-150) between them. Given the pain point (can't stick with systems, not "doesn't know the theory"), a strong tripwire is a short accountability-focused product — e.g., a 14-Day Deep Work Restart kit with daily check-in prompts, not more theory. That directly targets "can't stick with it" rather than repeating what the free template already covers.

**Problem: Price jump too big**

Free to $297 is not a 4-8x jump, it's effectively infinite from the buyer's perspective — there's no reference point for what $297 buys relative to the free thing. This isn't really a "big price jump between tiers" so much as an absent staircase, but it's worth naming as its own issue: even after you add a tripwire, check that tripwire-to-flagship and flagship-to-premium gaps stay in the 4-8x range, not 15-20x.

**Fix:** Once tripwire and flagship exist, target roughly: Free → $17 → $79 → $297. That keeps each jump reasonable and repositions $297 correctly as the premium tier rather than the only paid option.

**Problem: Disconnected lead magnet**

The Weekly Planning Template solves "I don't have a system," but your stated core pain point is "I have a system but can't stick with it for more than 2 weeks." The free product and the audience's real problem aren't the same problem — so downloading the template doesn't actually build a case for why someone needs the Deep Work System next. It builds a case for a different, adjacent product.

**Fix:** Either reposition the lead magnet's framing (add a short "why systems fail after week 2" section that bridges into the real pain point) or plan the new tripwire to be the thing that explicitly picks up where the template's honeymoon period ends — the second option is stronger and doubles as your Tier 2 concept above.

**Problem: No bridge mechanism**

You said there's no email follow-up after signup. Even with the right products in place, if nothing ever asks the downloader to take the next step, the ladder doesn't function — it's just two products sitting near each other.

**Fix:** Build at minimum a 2-email sequence: one immediately after download (use Tool 3 to draft it once the tripwire product exists), one 4-5 days later re-surfacing the offer with a specific "still haven't stuck with it?" angle tied to the real pain point.

---

**Priority Order**

1. **Fix the disconnected lead magnet framing and build the tripwire product** — highest impact, moderate effort. This single move (a $17-27 "why it falls apart after week 2" product) creates the missing bridge in both directions: it gives the free template somewhere to go, and it gives the $297 course a warmed-up buyer instead of a cold one.
2. **Draft a bridge email sequence** — use confirmed audience needs and recipient permissions. The free template did not collect emails, so there is no existing contact list to send to. Propose an optional signup path; do not claim immediate conversions or send without authorization.
3. **Build the flagship tier ($47-150)** — meaningful impact, higher effort. Do this after 1 and 2 are live and you can see whether tripwire buyers are asking for more before committing to a new build.
