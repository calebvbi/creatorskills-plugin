# Newsletter Sponsorship Pitch Kit

Build an offer, evidence-based media kit, prospect research plan, pitch and sponsored-copy draft. Work with the creator's actual audience and capacity. This workflow does not sell a placement or send a message.

## Intake

Collect newsletter topic, intended reader, subscriber count/date, publication cadence, delivered-message counts, measured opens/clicks with definitions, approved issue samples and existing sponsor commitments. Ask which placement formats the creator can support. If metrics are missing, leave them unknown rather than estimating the newsletter's performance from a benchmark.

## Media kit

Include positioning, confirmed audience description, dated metrics, sample issue link, actual prior campaigns if any, available placement options and verified contact details. Distinguish self-reported audience interests from measured surveys. No invented income, buying intent, advertiser history or industry percentile. An open is not proof of individual reading; subscribers are not guaranteed delivered impressions.

## Price and scope

Use the creator's actual prior terms, costs and preferences. If they want a CPM scenario, define the billable unit and assumed CPM explicitly, calculate units/1,000×CPM and label it a negotiation scenario. There is no universal rate table. Ask which rights, revisions, approval process, disclosures and reporting are included. Explain how an assumed price or sell-through change affects gross receipts without forecasting demand.

## Prospect research

Create a table with brand/product, audience-fit hypothesis, dated observed sponsorship evidence, actual contact source and open questions. Inspect sources before claiming a brand spends on sponsorships or sponsored a peer. If access is unavailable, provide research candidates marked unverified, not a fake ranked advertiser list. Do not scrape or expose personal contact information unnecessarily.

## Pitch

Write a short email with a verified relevance point, supplied audience evidence, one proposed placement and a clear next step. Do not claim familiarity, product use, previous research or campaign results that did not happen. Use placeholders for missing names and contacts. Do not say anything is attached unless it is.

## Follow-up and negotiation

Draft only the follow-ups requested. Confirm recipient permission and suppression, and obtain scoped sending authorization before execution. Offer a smaller scope or alternative placement instead of promising conversion. A declined or unanswered pitch is not evidence that the price is wrong.

## Sponsored copy

Use the approved product brief, actual offer terms and the creator's real experience. Separate factual product claims, personal opinions and unsupported assertions. Include appropriate disclosure after checking current requirements. Verify links, codes, trial length, eligibility and price before publishing. Do not imply the product worked for the creator without supplied experience.

## Report

Return the kit, proposed scope and rate assumptions, research table, unsent pitch/follow-up, copy draft if requested, and missing-evidence list. Post-campaign reporting uses actual delivered/click/conversion counts with definitions and attribution limits; it does not invent success to improve renewal chances.
