# Content Idea Brainstormer — adapted worked example

## Supplied brief

“I make videos for beginner home bakers. I post once a week and have a small kitchen. I can demonstrate measuring ingredients, reading a yeast packet, and keeping a baking log. I have not run comparison experiments or supplied audience analytics. Give me 10 ideas.”

## Example output

Each title is a draft. Audience-fit reasoning is a hypothesis, not a view or conversion forecast.

| Type | Title | Specific angle | Why it may fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tutorial | Your First Baking Log: What to Write Down | Build a log for recipe, flour, temperature, timing and observations. | Beginners can use the artifact on their next bake. |
| Walkthrough | Read the Yeast Packet Before You Start | Demonstrate the actual packet's directions without claiming every yeast type works the same way. | Turns a concrete supplied asset into a useful lesson. |
| Checklist | A Small-Kitchen Bread Setup | Show the creator's real workspace and label optional equipment. | Fits the creator's available location and constraints. |
| Process | Plan a Flour Comparison Without Changing Everything Else | Design a future comparison and identify variables. Do not claim results. | Explains how to make the next experiment easier to interpret. |
| Beginner question | What Does This Recipe Mean by “Rest”? | Choose a specific recipe and verify its directions before explaining. | Addresses a question beginners may have; validate interest. |
| Visual demonstration | Label Your Ingredients Before Mixing | Film ingredient preparation and a simple checklist. | Can be produced with the stated demonstration capabilities. |
| Mistake prevention | Check the Units Before You Measure | Compare the units printed on the actual recipe and measuring tools. | Makes a practical check visible without invented failure stories. |
| Behind the scenes | How I Prepare One Baking Video in a Small Kitchen | Show the creator's actual setup and production sequence. | Uses real process instead of invented expertise or outcomes. |
| Community prompt | What Would You Put in a Baking Log? | Share a draft log and invite specific additions. | Can collect useful questions; response volume is unknown. |
| Follow-up plan | One Change for the Next Batch | Choose a future change from observed notes and describe the plan. | Creates continuity once the creator has real observations. |

**Start with Your First Baking Log.** It produces a useful artifact with the equipment and knowledge supplied. This recommendation is based on production feasibility and audience fit, not a prediction of reach.

**Before recording:** verify recipe-specific explanations, use actual footage, and leave experimental outcomes unknown until observed. Do not manufacture a personal failure, income history, subscriber transformation or scientific result to make a title more dramatic.

## Transfer to another niche

For a creator who only says “I teach beginner personal finance and have 200 subscribers,” ask for the specific task they want to teach, or propose a clearly labeled fictional budget walkthrough. Do not invent their income, savings, debt, investing results or life story. If they want a personal-story angle, request the actual experience first.
