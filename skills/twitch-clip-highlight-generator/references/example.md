# Twitch Clip and Highlight Generator: practical bounded workflows

## Example 1: frequent streamer with little editing experience

**Supplied input:** The creator streams five or six days per week, mainly Valorant and Minecraft, wants TikTok growth, has no editing experience and cannot manually clip while streaming. Session length, capture software, available editing time, account access and budget are unknown.

### Proposed workflow

1. **Confirm the source.** Ask whether recordings or VODs are available and whether the creator has permission to reuse all included content. A title or stream URL alone does not mean its footage was read.
2. **Choose a candidate-detection method.** If a tool supports this platform and source type, automated suggestions may reduce manual searching. Verify current official features, plan limits, pricing, recording access and retention before recommending or connecting a named service. No free tier or automatic capability is assumed.
3. **Review one suggested moment.** Watch it with enough surrounding context to avoid changing the meaning. Confirm actual start/end points from the footage. Suggestions are candidates, not published clips.
4. **Make one simple edit.** Use a vertical layout appropriate to the chosen platform, readable captions checked against the audio, and a clear beginning/end. Current export requirements need verification. Keep the original recording.
5. **Inspect the export.** Check crop, audio, caption accuracy and rights. Choose whether to publish only after review. Tool setup, paid services and posting require the creator's authorization.

**Useful first deliverable without tools:** A clipping checklist and a log with source, verified in/out times, reason to keep, rights concerns and edit notes. Timing fields stay blank until footage is inspected.

**Effort:** Unknown until one candidate is processed. Time the first edit, then choose a sustainable weekly clip target. No daily 45-minute availability, existing OBS/Streamlabs setup or growth percentage is inferred.

## Example 2: manual review with a short time budget

**Supplied input:** A creator has a three-hour VOD and 30 minutes to find potential clips. No markers exist.

Watching the entire three-hour source at 2× takes 90 minutes before pauses. A 30-minute session at 2× covers at most 60 minutes of source, so it cannot establish that the whole VOD was reviewed.

### Review plan

- Choose and record a 60-minute source interval at most; allow less source coverage if pausing or taking notes.
- Record which intervals were actually inspected. Label the result a partial review.
- Mark candidate moments with source-verified boundaries and a short reason to consider them.
- Keep unreviewed footage and rejected candidates. Do not delete source files or unselected material.
- Continue in later sessions if the creator wants complete coverage.

**Candidate log:** source interval reviewed / candidate in / candidate out / context summary / caption check / status. All timestamps are unknown until inspection. A sample log structure is not evidence that footage was viewed.

## Example 3: creator already has markers

**Supplied input:** The creator has three markers from a stream but has not supplied the corresponding clips. They want a repeatable edit checklist.

### Edit checklist

1. Open the actual source around each marker and find a complete moment. A marker alone does not establish its end time or narrative context.
2. Choose framing that keeps the important action visible. Preview the crop for the intended platform; do not assume a universal overlay-safe area.
3. Add captions only after checking the speech. Do not add reactions or quotations the source does not contain.
4. Export a review copy using requirements verified for the current platform and editor. Do not claim a file was rendered unless an export occurred.
5. Review and decide which copy to publish. Keep the original and record the exact published asset only after an authorized, successful action.

### Measurement plan

Track each clip's date, format, source interval and defined platform measures. Compare like-for-like windows where possible. A clip's views or completion rate do not guarantee channel growth or explain algorithm behavior. Use observations to choose the next editing experiment.

No tool was connected, VOD accessed, clip rendered, source deleted or post published in these planning examples.
