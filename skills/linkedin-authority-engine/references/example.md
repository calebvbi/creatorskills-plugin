# LinkedIn Authority Engine - Worked Examples

## Example 1 - Freelance Email Strategist

### Creator Input

```text
I help creator-led SaaS brands improve newsletter click-through rates.

Audience:
- founders
- heads of growth
- newsletter operators

Offer:
- newsletter audits
- retainer strategy work

Goal on LinkedIn:
- better inbound leads
- more replies from operators, not just other freelancers

Tone:
- direct
- practical
- skeptical of growth cliches

Raw material:
- 2 audit case studies
- notes from a failed welcome sequence
- recurring mistakes I see in CTA sections
- a YouTube video I recorded about newsletter retention
```

### Example Output Snapshot

## 1. Authority Positioning

- **Audience:** Founders, heads of growth, and newsletter operators at creator-led SaaS companies
- **Core problem:** Their newsletters get opens but not enough clicks, replies, or downstream action
- **What you want to be known for:** Sharp newsletter strategy rooted in real retention and conversion behavior
- **Offer or outcome:** Audit work and retainer strategy
- **Authority line:** I help creator-led SaaS newsletters turn attention into clicks, replies, and revenue without writing fluffier emails.
- **Proof assets to use:** Two audit turnarounds, failed welcome-sequence lesson, CTA teardown notes, retention video transcript

## 2. Content Pillars

| Pillar | What it covers | Why it works on LinkedIn | Best CTA angle |
|--------|----------------|--------------------------|----------------|
| Audit Proof | Before-and-after lessons from newsletter teardowns | Founders trust specific fixes more than theory | Offer to share the checklist |
| CTA Mechanics | Why readers do or do not click | Tactical breakdowns create saves and comments | Ask what CTA they are using now |
| Operator Perspective | Opinions on newsletter strategy mistakes | Sharp takes cut through generic marketing advice | Invite objections or counterexamples |
| Offer Bridge | What an audit actually reveals | Soft-sell content helps leads self-qualify | Offer a teardown example or filter |

## 3. Idea Bank

1. **Your newsletter probably does not have a writing problem. It has a decision problem.**
   - Pillar: CTA Mechanics
   - Source material: recurring CTA mistakes
   - Goal: reframe the audience's diagnosis
   - CTA direction: ask which CTA type they default to

2. **The audit did not uncover weak copy. It uncovered a weak promise.**
   - Pillar: Audit Proof
   - Source material: case study 1
   - Goal: show authority through a real fix
   - CTA direction: offer to share the before-and-after structure

3. **Why most welcome sequences feel busy and still do nothing**
   - Pillar: Operator Perspective
   - Source material: failed sequence notes
   - Goal: spark comments from operators
   - CTA direction: ask what their welcome sequence is supposed to do

## 4. Two-Week LinkedIn Sprint

### Week 1

**Post 1**
- Goal: establish a sharper point of view
- Pillar: Operator Perspective
- Hook options:
  1. Most newsletter problems are not writing problems.
  2. A better subject line will not fix a weak newsletter strategy.
  3. If your newsletter gets opens and no clicks, the copy is not the first thing I would blame.
- Draft:

Most newsletter problems are not writing problems.

They are decision problems.

I see this in audits all the time.

The team thinks the issue is:
"we need stronger copy"

What is actually happening:
- the email is trying to do 4 jobs at once
- the CTA is vague
- the reader has no clue what matters most

So the copy gets blamed for a strategy failure.

You can write a sharper sentence.
That helps a little.

But if the email has no single job, better writing just makes the confusion easier to read.

The first question I ask in an audit is simple:

What is this email supposed to make the reader do next?

If the answer is fuzzy, everything downstream gets weaker.

Curious - when a newsletter underperforms, what do you usually check first?

- CTA: invite operators to share their first diagnostic step
- First comment: Mine is always "what is the single next action?" because it exposes half the problem immediately.
- Follow-up prompt: Happy to share the 5-part audit filter I use if that would be useful.

**Post 2**
- Goal: show proof through a case-study lesson
- Pillar: Audit Proof
- Hook options:
  1. The audit did not fix the copy. It fixed the promise.
  2. We changed one promise and the clicks followed.
  3. The newsletter was polished. The pitch was muddy.
- Draft:

The audit did not fix the copy.

It fixed the promise.

A founder sent me a newsletter that looked completely fine on the surface.

Good subject line.
Clean writing.
Solid open rate.

But click-through was soft for weeks.

The real problem was not the writing quality.

The email kept asking readers to care about a result the business had not made concrete enough.

We rewrote the promise around one sharper outcome.
Not "improve your workflow."
Not "save time."

A specific result.
For a specific reader.
In language the buyer would actually repeat.

Same list.
Same brand.
Same general offer.

Different promise.

Clicks improved because the reader could finally tell why this mattered now.

That is why I do not start with "make the copy punchier."

I start with:
what exactly are we asking the reader to believe?

What kind of promise gets the best response in your world - speed, certainty, savings, status, or something else?

- CTA: ask which promise type their market responds to
- First comment: A vague promise can hide inside polished copy for months.
- Follow-up prompt: If helpful, I can show the before-and-after promise structure we used.

### Week 2

**Post 4 idea**
- Goal: conversation starter
- Pillar: CTA Mechanics
- Angle: why "learn more" is usually a signal that the writer has not decided what the reader should do

**Post 5 idea**
- Goal: authority plus relatability
- Pillar: Personal Signal
- Angle: what the failed welcome sequence taught you about trying to teach too much too early

**Post 6 idea**
- Goal: soft-sell bridge
- Pillar: Offer Bridge
- Angle: the 3 things you can diagnose in the first 10 minutes of a newsletter audit

## 5. Lead Follow-Up Prompts

- **Warm comment reply:** Happy to share the audit filter I use before I touch the copy. Want me to send it here?
- **DM opener:** Saw your comment on the CTA post. If you want, send me the current CTA and the job of the email - I can tell you where I'd look first.
- **Soft-sell transition:** If this is happening across the whole sequence, that is usually when an audit is worth it.
- **Not-now nurture reply:** No rush. If you want, I can send a simpler checklist you can use on the next send.

## Example 2 - Video Consultant For B2B Creators

### Creator Input

```text
I help B2B creators turn webinar recordings into better YouTube videos and LinkedIn clips.

Audience:
- founders
- consultants
- small media teams

Offer:
- content strategy workshops
- repurposing retainers

Goal:
- more workshop inquiries

Raw material:
- client call notes
- one webinar transcript
- 5 mistakes I keep seeing in repurposed clips
```

### Example Output Snapshot

## 1. Authority Positioning

- **Authority line:** I help B2B creators stop posting chopped-up webinar fragments and start turning long-form expertise into content people will actually finish.

## 2. Sample Pillars

| Pillar | What it covers | Best CTA angle |
|--------|----------------|----------------|
| Repurposing Mistakes | Why most webinar clips die on contact | Ask what part of repurposing feels slowest |
| Editing Decisions | What to cut, keep, and reframe | Offer to share the cut criteria |
| Workshop Lessons | Patterns from team sessions | Invite readers to compare workflow bottlenecks |
| Offer Bridge | When content chaos is actually a systems problem | Offer a workshop outline |

## 3. Sample Post Concept

- **Angle:** Your repurposed clip is failing before the content even starts.
- **Pillar:** Editing Decisions
- **Goal:** teach a sharp lesson and create workshop demand
- **CTA direction:** ask whether the team picks clips by "best moment" or by "best opening 3 seconds"

## 4. Sample Soft-Sell Follow-Up

- **Comment reply:** If you want, I can share the exact clip-selection rule we use before editing starts.
- **DM opener:** If your team is doing this from webinars, send over one example. I can point out where the clip loses the viewer.
- **Offer bridge:** If this keeps happening across the whole content pipeline, it is usually a workshop problem before it is an editing problem.
