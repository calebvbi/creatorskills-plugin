## Concept 1: The Center-Face Reaction (Recommended)

**Why this works for vertical:** This is a reaction-driven storytime — the face IS the content. A strong, legible expression in the safe zone will outperform any graphic or text-heavy layout for this format.

### Composition
- **Subject placement:** Face fills 55% of frame height, centered horizontally, eyes landing around the 35% height mark — clear of both the notch-crop zone and the caption zone.
- **Grid test:** At 200x200px, a single exaggerated disgusted/appalled expression is still instantly readable. Passes.

### Text Overlay
- **Copy:** "AGAIN?!"
- **Placement:** Small text badge just above the head, inside the safe zone — doesn't compete with the expression.
- **Style:** Bold rounded sans, white with black outline, slight upward tilt for energy.

### Color & Contrast
- **Palette:** Warm orange (#FF6B35) background wash, black (#1A1A1A) text outline, white text fill.
- **Contrast note:** Orange stands out against the blue/gray tones common in office-comedy content, so it reads distinctly in a "for you" feed dominated by similar creators.

### Image Generation Prompt
```
Vertical 9:16 composition, 1080x1920, TikTok cover frame. Centered
portrait of a person with an exaggerated appalled/disgusted expression,
face filling roughly 55% of frame height, eyes positioned around 35%
height from top. Warm orange (#FF6B35) background wash. Small bold
white text badge "AGAIN?!" with black outline positioned just above the
head, inside the vertical safe zone (center 70% width, 10%-65% height).
Leave the right-edge vertical strip (icon stack area) and bottom 20%
(caption zone) empty/uncluttered for platform UI overlays.
```

### Series Consistency Element
Not applicable — one-off video.

## Concept 2: The Object Hero

**Why this works for vertical:** A single unmistakable object — the fish, mid-microwave — can carry the joke without needing the creator's face at all, which works well as a scroll-stopping cold open before the reaction plays.

### Composition
- **Subject placement:** A single fish illustration centered in the safe zone, isolated on a flat background, no clutter.
- **Grid test:** A bold fish silhouette against a flat color reads clearly even shrunk to a grid thumbnail. Passes.

### Text Overlay
- **Copy:** "NOT AGAIN"
- **Placement:** Directly below the object, inside the safe zone.
- **Style:** Bold condensed sans, black text, no outline needed against the light background.

### Color & Contrast
- **Palette:** Soft yellow background (#FFE8A3), black object silhouette, black text.
- **Contrast note:** Lower-energy than Concept 1 — works better as a "cold open" cover that gets explained by the caption rather than the expression itself.

### Image Generation Prompt
```
Vertical 9:16 composition, 1080x1920, TikTok cover frame. Bold black
silhouette illustration of a fish on a plate, centered in the vertical
safe zone (10%-65% height, center 70% width), against a soft yellow
(#FFE8A3) flat background. Bold black condensed sans text "NOT AGAIN"
directly below the illustration, inside the safe zone. Leave the
right-edge icon stack area and bottom caption zone empty for platform
UI. Minimal, high-contrast, no gradient noise.
```

### Series Consistency Element
Not applicable — one-off video.

## My Pick: Concept 1

**Why:** For face-forward storytime content, the expression is the hook — TikTok viewers scroll fastest through reaction-style content, so a legible face beats an object graphic for scroll-stop rate here.

**Grid Preview Note:** As a one-off, this doesn't need to hold up as part of a 9-video grid pattern — it just needs to win the single scroll decision.

## Example 2: Educational Series (YouTube Shorts, Recurring)

### Creator Context

- **Channel:** MoneyMoves101 on YouTube Shorts

- **Video topic:** "3 signs you're bad with money" (part of an ongoing "signs" series)
- **Face on screen:** No — text and graphics only
- **Series or one-off:** Series — needs a repeatable visual system
- **Brand colors:** Navy (#1B2A4A) and gold (#D4AF37), established from prior videos

### Input Given

```
Platform: YouTube Shorts
Topic: "3 signs you're bad with money" - part of an ongoing series
Face on screen: no, text/graphics only
Series: yes, need it to match my existing navy and gold videos
```

## Concept 1: The Big Word Stack (Recommended)

**Why this works for vertical:** Listicle hooks ("3 signs...") are built for the word-stack format — vertical scrolling already trains the eye to read top-to-bottom, so a stacked headline mirrors the scroll motion instead of fighting it.

### Composition
- **Subject placement:** No face — three stacked words centered in the safe zone, one per line.
- **Grid test:** Large stacked text in navy/gold still reads as "part of the money series" even shrunk to a grid thumbnail, because the color pairing is now a recognized signal.

### Text Overlay
- **Copy:** "BAD / WITH / MONEY?" (one word per line, 3 lines)
- **Placement:** Vertically centered in the safe zone, occupying the middle 40% of frame height.
- **Style:** Bold gold serif-adjacent display font, navy drop shadow, huge scale relative to frame.

### Color & Contrast
- **Palette:** Navy background (#1B2A4A), gold text (#D4AF37), thin gold rule line as a divider accent.
- **Contrast note:** Gold-on-navy is high contrast and now functions as brand recognition across the whole series — a viewer who's seen one video in the grid recognizes the next instantly.

### Image Generation Prompt
```
Vertical 9:16 composition, 1080x1920, YouTube Shorts cover frame. Solid
navy (#1B2A4A) background. Bold gold (#D4AF37) display text stacked in
three lines, one word per line — "BAD" / "WITH" / "MONEY?" — vertically
centered occupying the middle 40% of frame height, large scale, subtle
navy drop shadow for depth. Thin gold rule line beneath the stack as a
divider accent. Small recurring gold corner badge (numbered "part of
a series" mark) in the top-left corner, inside the safe zone. Leave the
right-edge icon stack area and bottom caption zone empty for platform
UI. Clean, high-contrast, minimal.
```

### Series Consistency Element
A small numbered gold corner badge (e.g., a subtle "05" mark for the fifth video in the "signs" series) in the top-left of the safe zone, present on every cover in the series — repeated across every video so the profile grid reads as one ongoing show, not scattered one-offs.

## Concept 2: The Split Moment

**Why this works for vertical:** "Signs you're bad with money" implies a before/after or problem/consequence structure — a split panel format can tease the payoff without giving it away.

### Composition
- **Subject placement:** Top panel (within the 10%-65% safe zone) shows a simple icon of a maxed-out credit card; bottom of that same band shows a shocked-expression emoji-style graphic reacting to it.
- **Grid test:** Two stacked icons at grid size are legible as "problem + reaction" but less distinctive than the word stack at a glance.

### Text Overlay
- **Copy:** "SIGN #1"
- **Placement:** Small label at the top of the safe zone.
- **Style:** Bold gold sans, navy background chip.

### Color & Contrast
- **Palette:** Navy (#1B2A4A) and gold (#D4AF37), matching series colors.
- **Contrast note:** Consistent with the established palette but relies more on icon recognition than the word stack's instant legibility.

### Image Generation Prompt
```
Vertical 9:16 composition, 1080x1920, YouTube Shorts cover frame. Navy
(#1B2A4A) background split into two stacked panels within the safe zone
(10%-65% height): top panel shows a simple gold-line icon of a
maxed-out credit card, bottom panel shows a simple shocked-expression
graphic reacting to it. Small gold text label "SIGN #1" in a navy chip
at the top of the safe zone. Leave the right-edge icon stack area and
bottom caption zone empty for platform UI. Clean, minimal, consistent
navy/gold series palette.
```

### Series Consistency Element
Same numbered gold corner badge system as Concept 1, so either concept can be used interchangeably within the series without breaking the visual pattern.

---

## My Pick: Concept 1

**Why:** The word-stack format's brand recognition compounds across the series — every new "signs" video reinforces the navy/gold pattern, and the format is faster to read than the split-panel icon approach.

**Grid Preview Note:** With the numbered corner badge, a grid of 9 recent "signs" covers would show a consistent navy/gold word-stack pattern with only the words and numbers changing — instantly recognizable as one ongoing series rather than 9 disconnected videos.
