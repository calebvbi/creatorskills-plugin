# Podcast Guest Booking System: useful prep with limited evidence

## Supplied sample brief

This fictional exercise supplies a show named The Case Files, 45-minute interviews and 8,000 listeners per episode. The host wants to invite Dr. Maya Chen, described by the host as a forensic pathologist and author of What the Body Knows. The angle is communicating uncertainty in court. No book excerpt, guest biography, contact address or browsing tool is available. The host has not said they read the book.

## Ideal guest profile

- Practitioner able to explain how uncertainty is communicated to a non-specialist audience.
- Willing to discuss general methods and limitations without disclosing private case information.
- Fit to confirm through an introductory conversation; expertise and availability are not independently verified here.

## Outreach drafts

### A: direct invitation

**Subject:** Invitation to The Case Files: explaining forensic uncertainty

Hi Dr. Chen,

I host The Case Files, a 45-minute podcast with about 8,000 listeners per episode. I'd like to invite you to discuss how experts explain uncertainty in forensic evidence to a courtroom audience.

Would that topic fit your work and interests? If so, I can send a short proposed outline and coordinate a time that works for you.

[Host name]

### B: audience-first invitation

**Subject:** A conversation about what forensic conclusions can tell us

Hi Dr. Chen,

The Case Files explores cases with listeners who want to understand the process behind the conclusions. I'm planning a conversation about explaining uncertainty without making the science harder to follow.

Would you be open to discussing that topic in a 45-minute interview? Our show reaches about 8,000 listeners per episode. I'd welcome your suggestions for an angle that fits your expertise.

[Host name]

### C: book-led invitation without invented familiarity

**Subject:** Interview invitation related to What the Body Knows

Hi Dr. Chen,

I'm reaching out about an interview for The Case Files. I understand you wrote What the Body Knows, and I'd like to explore whether a discussion about communicating forensic uncertainty would fit your work.

I can share a proposed outline for a 45-minute conversation. Is there an angle you would prefer to discuss?

[Host name]

**Choice:** A is the least dependent on unverified book details. Confirm the guest's professional identity and contact address before sending. These drafts make no claim that the host read a particular chapter.

## Follow-up drafts

1. After an appropriate interval chosen by the host: “Following up on the invitation below. Would this topic fit your current interests? Happy to send a brief outline.”
2. If another follow-up is warranted: “I can adapt the proposed angle to the part of your work you'd most like to discuss. Let me know if a short outline would help.”
3. Final note: “I'll close the loop here. Thank you for considering the invitation; you're welcome to reach out if a future conversation would suit.”

Do not automate or send these without authorization for the recipient and schedule. No contact or booking has occurred.

## Pre-show prep kit

### Open questions

- How would you describe the part of your work most relevant to this topic?
- What does a listener need to understand before interpreting a forensic conclusion?
- Can you offer an example you are permitted to discuss?
- Where is uncertainty easiest to miscommunicate?
- What should we avoid oversimplifying?
- Which sources would you recommend listeners read afterward?

### Intro draft

“Today we're discussing how forensic uncertainty is explained. My guest is Dr. Maya Chen, a forensic pathologist and author of What the Body Knows.”

The identity description is supplied by the host and needs guest confirmation before recording. Add no career duration or specific testimony without evidence.

### Recording plan

Opening and definitions → a guest-approved example → communication choices → limitations → resources. These are proposed segments, not extracted timestamps.

### Guest check

Ask the guest to approve their short biography, pronunciation and topics they cannot discuss. Obtain any sources needed for specific factual claims. Leave recording date, contact details and links unresolved until confirmed.
