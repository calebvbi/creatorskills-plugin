# Podcast Episode Planner: one measurable review-request experiment

## Supplied brief

The creator wants a 30-minute solo episode for indie podcasters, roughly three to twelve months into their shows, who want more reviews. The stated goal is a tactic listeners can try this week. No host personal story, review data, platform research or measured tactic effectiveness is supplied. “First 100 reviews” is an audience aspiration, not a result this episode can promise.

## Proposed episode

**Working title:** Ask for Podcast Reviews: One Small Experiment

**Premise:** Help listeners make a clear, optional invitation and evaluate what happens without assuming why past asks did or did not work.

**Duration:** A 30-minute planning allocation, not verified audio or extracted timestamps.

| Segment | Allocated time | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Opening | 2 minutes | Set a practical, limited goal |
| Review the current invitation | 6 minutes | Identify what is known and unknown |
| Draft a clear invitation | 8 minutes | Produce usable wording |
| Plan one comparison | 8 minutes | Separate hypotheses from results |
| This week's action | 4 minutes | Choose a small test |
| Close | 2 minutes | State limits and next step |

Total allocation: 30 minutes. Do a read-through of the finished script before assigning exact edit marks.

## Opening draft

“If you want more reviews for your podcast, start with one thing you can inspect: the invitation you're giving listeners. This episode will help you draft one clear ask and choose a small test. It won't promise a particular number of reviews or assume why your current approach hasn't produced them.”

## Segment 1: inspect the current invitation

Ask listeners to write down the wording, where it appears and which platform it names. Their existing results may be unknown. Avoid diagnosing timing, wording or listener motivation without data. Review counts alone cannot show which part of an invitation caused a response.

Before giving platform instructions, verify the current official review flow. Do not claim review counts control a platform's recommendation algorithm.

## Segment 2: draft the invitation

**Solo-show draft:** “If you'd like to share your thoughts about the show, you can leave an honest review on [confirmed platform]. [Verified brief instructions]. Thank you for listening.”

**Interview-show draft:** “If this conversation gave you something to think about, you're welcome to leave an honest review on [confirmed platform]. [Verified brief instructions].”

The invitation asks for honest feedback, not a positive rating. Include “I read every review” only if the host confirms that it is true; finding two review examples does not establish it. No host personal story is needed.

## Segment 3: plan a comparison

Mid-episode versus outro placement is a possible experiment, not an established winner. Choose one variable and a defined observation window. Track which episodes used each invitation, comparable exposure if available, and review counts with their limits. Audience changes, episode differences and delayed reviews can confound the result.

If there is too little data, report that uncertainty instead of choosing a winner. Do not invent a conversion multiplier or cite research that has not been accessed.

## Segment 4: this week's action

Draft one invitation and verify the chosen platform's instructions. The creator can decide where to place it in a future episode. If considering direct outreach, first decide whether it is appropriate for the relationship and obtain authorization for any tool-assisted send. A planning request does not send messages.

**Optional unsent message:** “Thanks for listening to the show. If you'd like to leave an honest review, here's the verified route: [link]. No pressure.”

## Closing draft

“Your next step is a clear invitation you can test. If the result changes, record what else changed too. That gives you a basis for the next decision, without pretending one tactic guarantees your first hundred reviews.”

## Recording preparation

Confirm the actual review flow and links. Use real, approved reviews only if the creator wants examples. Write and time the final script; the outline allocations are estimates. No episode was recorded, published or promoted, and no outreach was sent.
