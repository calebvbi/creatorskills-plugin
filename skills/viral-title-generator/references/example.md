# Worked Examples — Viral Title Generator in Action

## Example 1: Tech Channel — Camera Gear Switch

### Creator Input

```
Topic: I switched from a DSLR to a mirrorless camera after 6 years
Platform: YouTube

The most surprising thing: I almost returned the mirrorless camera
in the first week because the autofocus felt worse — then a firmware
update fixed it, and now I'll never go back to DSLR.
```

### Output

```
VIRAL TITLES — Switching from DSLR to Mirrorless

1. "I Almost Returned My Mirrorless Camera (Here's Why I Didn't)" —
   Formula: Curiosity Gap — Clickbait risk: Low
2. "3 Things That Almost Made Me Return My Mirrorless Camera" —
   Formula: Power Number — Clickbait risk: Low
3. "I Was Embarrassed to Admit I Almost Went Back to DSLR" —
   Formula: Emotional Trigger — Clickbait risk: Medium
4. "I Used a Mirrorless Camera for 30 Days After 6 Years on DSLR" —
   Formula: Challenge/Experiment — Clickbait risk: Low
5. "5 Reasons Mirrorless Beat My DSLR (After I Almost Gave Up)" —
   Formula: Listicle — Clickbait risk: Low
6. "How to Fix Mirrorless Autofocus Lag Without Buying New Gear" —
   Formula: How-To — Clickbait risk: Low
7. "Stop Blaming Your Mirrorless Camera — It's Probably This Setting" —
   Formula: Warning/Stakes — Clickbait risk: Medium
8. "Why I Almost Gave Up on Mirrorless in the First Week" —
   Formula: Curiosity Gap — Clickbait risk: Low
9. "The Firmware Update That Changed My Mind About Mirrorless" —
   Formula: Power Number (implicit) — Clickbait risk: Low
10. "6 Years on DSLR, Then I Almost Quit Mirrorless in a Week" —
    Formula: Emotional Trigger — Clickbait risk: Medium

RECOMMENDED FOR TESTING:
- Option 1 (curiosity gap) — tests: whether withholding the "why I
  didn't return it" payoff drives more clicks than stating a fix
  upfront.
- Option 6 (how-to) — tests: whether framing this as solvable-problem
  content pulls a different, more search-driven audience than the
  personal-story angle.

WATCH OUT FOR: Option 7 implies a broader "you're probably doing this
wrong" warning. If your video is really about your own specific fix
rather than a common mistake other people are making, soften it to
"The Setting That Fixed My Mirrorless Autofocus" — same hook, no
overreach.
```

## Example 2: Personal Finance Channel — Shorts Cutdown

### Creator Input

```
I have a YouTube video about paying off $40K in debt in 18 months
and I'm also cutting a Shorts version of the best moment: the exact
month I realized I could actually hit the goal early.

Give me titles for both, with a note on how the winning YouTube
formula should be rephrased for Shorts.
```

### Output

```
VIRAL TITLES — Paying Off $40K in Debt in 18 Months (YouTube)

1. "I Paid Off $40K in Debt in 18 Months — Here's the Month Everything
   Changed" — Formula: Curiosity Gap — Clickbait risk: Low
2. "$40,000 in Debt, Gone in 18 Months — 3 Numbers That Made It
   Possible" — Formula: Power Number — Clickbait risk: Low
3. "I Cried When I Realized I'd Pay Off My Debt Early" — Formula:
   Emotional Trigger — Clickbait risk: Medium
4. "I Tracked Every Dollar for 18 Months to Kill $40K in Debt" —
   Formula: Challenge/Experiment — Clickbait risk: Low
5. "5 Moves That Paid Off $40K in Debt Faster Than Expected" —
   Formula: Listicle — Clickbait risk: Low
6. "How to Pay Off Debt Fast Without a Six-Figure Income" — Formula:
   How-To — Clickbait risk: Low
7. "Stop Making Minimum Payments — It's Costing You Years" — Formula:
   Warning/Stakes — Clickbait risk: Medium
8. "The Month I Knew I'd Beat My $40K Debt Payoff Goal Early" —
   Formula: Curiosity Gap — Clickbait risk: Low
9. "18 Months, $40,000, and One Spreadsheet That Changed Everything" —
   Formula: Power Number — Clickbait risk: Low
10. "I Almost Gave Up on My Debt Payoff Goal at Month 10" — Formula:
    Emotional Trigger — Clickbait risk: Low

RECOMMENDED FOR TESTING:
- Option 1 (curiosity gap) — tests: whether the "moment everything
  changed" hook pulls harder than a results-first framing.
- Option 6 (how-to) — tests: whether search-driven, income-agnostic
  framing brings in a broader audience than the personal-story
  numbers angle.

WATCH OUT FOR: Option 7 makes a blanket claim about minimum payments
"costing you years" — only use it if your video actually addresses
this as a general lesson, not just your personal experience. Otherwise
soften to "Why I Stopped Making Minimum Payments."

---

SHORTS CUTDOWN — "The Month I Knew"

For the Shorts version, the winning YouTube formula (curiosity gap)
translates directly, but tighten it for a feed scroll: drop the
setup words and lead with the moment itself.

Shorts title: "The exact month I knew I'd pay off $40K early"

Shorts titles read more like spoken text than a formal headline —
cut "Here's" and similar framing words that work in a YouTube title
bar but read as dead weight in a vertical feed where every word
competes for a half-second of attention.
```

## Why These Batches Work

Both examples avoid the trap of generating 10 titles that are really one title reworded nine times — each option comes from a genuinely different trigger, so the creator can learn something from testing them against each other instead of just picking whichever sounds catchiest. High-risk titles are flagged with a specific, usable softer alternative rather than a vague warning, and the testing recommendation always states the actual hypothesis being tested, not just "these two seem good."
