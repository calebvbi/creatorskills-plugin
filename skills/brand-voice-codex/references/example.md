#### Sentence Patterns

**Pattern 1:** "So [company] wants [$price] for a [reductive description of the product]. [One-word reaction.]"
*What makes it his:* Opens reviews by stripping the product down to its most basic function and then reacting to the price. The one-word sentence is the punchline. Sets the skeptical-but-fair tone immediately.

**Pattern 2:** "[Positive statement]. But [specific criticism]. [Dry aside.]"
*What makes it his:* The three-beat structure — give credit, take it away with specifics, then land a joke. Almost every paragraph in his reviews follows this rhythm.

**Pattern 3:** "[Brand] gives you [what the competitor offers]. [Brand 2] gives you [what competitor 2 offers]. [Product being reviewed] gives you [less] and says '[marketing quote].' Cool."
*What makes it his:* Parallel structure that builds a comparison ladder, then drops the product at the bottom with its own marketing language used against it. The trailing "Cool." is his signature dismissal.

**Pattern 4:** "I've been [doing X] for [time period] and [strong opinion]."
*What makes it his:* Establishes credibility through time spent, then delivers the verdict in the same sentence. No buildup, no hedging.

**Pattern 5:** "[Something] is [positive adjective]. [Something else] is [positive adjective]. [The one bad thing] is [direct criticism] and [that ruins everything / that's my only complaint]."
*What makes it his:* Stacks positives quickly, then drives the criticism home by contrast. The positives feel earned because he didn't linger on them.

**Pattern 6:** "Yes, [acknowledge something obvious]. No, [refuse to elaborate]. [Humorous deflection.]"
*What makes it his:* Used on Instagram and in asides. The yes/no parallel structure sets up the joke. Compact, conversational, slightly combative.

#### Sentence Patterns

**Pattern 1:** "Okay so [topic reframe that acknowledges what the audience is thinking]. And honestly, [personal admission that validates their feeling]. [Pivot to why it's different than they think.]"
*What makes it hers:* Opens from the audience's perspective, not her own. The "honestly" signals she's about to be vulnerable, which earns trust before she gives advice.

**Pattern 2:** "Not [abstract version], not '[cliche advice]' — [the real, specific thing]."
*What makes it hers:* Double negative construction that strips away what she ISN'T doing before revealing what she is. Creates contrast that makes her advice feel concrete and different.

**Pattern 3:** "[Short declarative statement about what to do]. Seriously. [Reason why, grounded in values not math.]"
*What makes it hers:* Permission-giving structure. The standalone "Seriously." sells the conviction. The reasoning is always emotional/practical, not purely mathematical.

**Pattern 4:** "Was it [the 'right' approach]? Probably not. [Acknowledge the critics.] But you know what? [What actually happened.]"
*What makes it hers:* Preemptively disarms criticism, then redirects to real results. The "but you know what?" pivot is her signature move from theory to practice.

**Pattern 5:** "If you [did the hard thing], I'm proud of you. If you [didn't], I'm [still proud/that's okay] because [reframe of what still counts as progress]."
*What makes it hers:* Double encouragement structure. The second half is the real move — validating people who feel like they fell short. She never leaves someone behind.

**Pattern 6:** "I don't need [fancy thing]. I need [plain, practical thing]."
*What makes it hers:* Cuts through feature marketing with what actually matters to a real person. Used for product recommendations and life advice alike.
