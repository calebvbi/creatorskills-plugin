# Worked Examples: Explicit Revenue Scenarios

These synthetic fixtures teach arithmetic and planning. They are not forecasts, market benchmarks or CreatorSkills financial results. No sales, growth, audience conversion or deal acceptance is guaranteed. Do not relabel a scenario "expected" without evidence supporting its assumptions.

## Scenario 1: Video creator

Suppose a creator explicitly asks to model these monthly assumptions: 250,000 monetized-period views at $3.60 RPM; two sponsor deals at $1,500; 50 digital-product sales at $19; ten course sales at $49; $450 affiliate receipts and $300 newsletter sponsorship receipts. The inputs are hypothetical and must be replaced with real observations or creator-selected assumptions.

| Stream | Calculation | Scenario gross receipts |
|---|---|---:|
| Video revenue | 250,000 / 1,000 × $3.60 | $900 |
| Sponsorships | 2 × $1,500 | $3,000 |
| Digital products | 50 × $19 | $950 |
| Course | 10 × $49 | $490 |
| Affiliates | Assumed receipts | $450 |
| Newsletter sponsor | Assumed receipts | $300 |
| Total | Sum | $6,090 |

Costs, fees, refunds and taxes are absent; net income is unknown. At 180,000 views and the same RPM, video revenue would be $648, not $900. If no sponsor deal closes, the scenario falls by $3,000. This sensitivity does not establish the likelihood of either outcome.

Plan: validate one stream with existing demand evidence before investing in several launches. Record production capacity, service obligations and the creator's actual costs. Choose a review point and a stopping condition for each experiment. Do not assume that future revenue buys back time or that an email list will grow to a specific size.

## Scenario 2: Podcast creator

Assume 12,000 downloads per episode, four episodes per month, two sold ad slots per episode and $35 CPM for every slot. Gross sponsorship revenue is 12 × 4 × 2 × $35 = $3,360. It is not $3,800 net. If only half the slots sell at that price, gross revenue is $1,680. Confirm the measurement window and billable download definition before using the calculation commercially.

Assume 8,000 newsletter subscribers, four sold issues and a hypothetical $70 per thousand subscriber price. Gross newsletter revenue is 8 × 4 × $70 = $2,240. This is an asking-price scenario; subscribers are not delivered impressions or guaranteed readers.

Assume 170 paying podcast members at $7: gross membership receipts are $1,190. Without actual fees/refunds, do not call this $850 net. Three $250 consultations produce $750 gross if sold and delivered. Other product and affiliate streams stay unknown rather than being filled to meet a target.

The four specified gross streams sum to $7,540 under these assumptions. Their costs, capacity, sell-through and demand remain unresolved. Model a zero-new-sales case and the creator's existing obligations before choosing an experiment.

## Reusable planning worksheet

For each stream record observed baseline, proposed offer, required work, assumption source, units, price, gross receipts, costs, net contribution if calculable and downside. Keep market evidence separate from editable scenario inputs. Review which assumption mattered most after real observations arrive. No blanket claim that email converts several times better than social is required.
