# Sponsor Deal Calculator and Pitch

Produce a rate scenario, draft pitch, media-kit outline and negotiation checklist using the creator's actual evidence. Prices are proposed terms, not a claim of guaranteed deal value or future performance.

## 1. Establish scope and evidence

Collect platform, deliverable, average performance with reporting window and definition, audience evidence, prior deal terms if shareable, production time/cost, rights, revisions, deadlines and objectives. Ask whether the brand is identified and whether product use or research has actually happened. Do not invent demographics, testimonials, attachments or hands-on experience.

## 2. Choose a pricing approach

Compare a cost/capacity-based quote, relevant actual prior deals and, if useful, a CPM scenario. Current market comparisons require dated sources and comparable scopes. Do not present a fixed platform table or niche multiplier as an industry standard.

For a CPM scenario: defined expected billable units / 1,000 × assumed CPM. Label both inputs as observations or assumptions. Multipliers and negotiating bands are optional user-selected assumptions; they do not prove fair market value. Explain which rights and deliverables the result covers. Historical average views are not guaranteed future views.

A walk-away price depends on cost, time, rights, risk and the creator's preferences. Do not mechanically set it to 70% of a model midpoint. Separate usage rights, paid amplification, exclusivity, raw assets, revisions and rush delivery; obtain agreement on each scope and price rather than imposing universal premiums.

## 3. Check the math

Show formulas, units and rounding. Engagement rates need a defined denominator; views and followers produce different measures. For packages, sum component prices and state the actual discount. Separate cash compensation from gifted products and uncertain affiliate commissions. Do not call product value equivalent to cash income.

## 4. Draft the pitch

Use a relevant, verified opening; actual audience metrics; one specific proposed integration; optional substantiated prior-work result; and a simple next step. Keep it concise and in the creator's voice. Describe a proposed product test as a proposal until it is performed. Disclose paid relationships as appropriate under verified current requirements.

If evidence is missing, omit the claim or use a visible placeholder. A prior partnership does not prove it exceeded KPIs. Say a media kit is attached only when an actual attachment is present. Do not send the pitch from a drafting request.

## 5. Outline the media kit

Include positioning, supplied audience metrics with dates/definitions, approved work samples, relevant rights-safe brand references, proposed packages and confirmed contact details. Do not add category averages or top-percentile rankings without evidence. Preserve unknowns rather than creating plausible statistics.

## 6. Prepare negotiation options

Offer scope reductions or alternate deliverables when a budget is lower; show the math. Flag vague usage rights, exclusivity, payment conditions, open-ended revisions and performance guarantees. Avoid predicting that a brand will counter or accept. Contract and disclosure requirements may require current official guidance or professional review.

Return rate assumptions and calculation, proposed scope, unsent email, media-kit outline, open questions and a next action. No contract, invoice, rights transfer or external communication is completed without separate authorization and observed tools.
