# Worked Examples: Interpreting Without Inventing Causes

## Fixture A: Overlapping periods

Creator input: October 1–7 has 1,000 views and 50 link clicks from 2 uploads; October 5–11 has 1,500 views and 45 clicks from 4 uploads. A title changed on October 5.

Output: click/view ratio fell from 5% to 3%, down 2 percentage points or 40% relative. Views rose 50%, clicks fell 10%, uploads doubled. October 5–7 overlaps; the earlier window includes post-change observations. These totals cannot establish whether the title improved conversion. Link clicks per view are not impression CTR, and no purchase conversion was supplied.

Next action: get daily video-level exposure and click data, plus any other changes. Use a supported controlled title comparison if available, or carefully comparable non-overlapping periods with explicit limitations. Hold other changes stable where feasible. Do not report the title as the cause.

## Fixture B: Newsletter denominator

Synthetic input: 2,000 delivered messages, 800 measured unique opens, 60 unique clickers and 6 confirmed orders attributed under the creator's seven-day rule. Campaign cost and order margin are unknown.

Output: measured open rate is 40%, click-through rate per delivered message is 3%, click-to-open rate is 7.5%, and observed orders per unique clicker are 10%. These measures use different denominators. Tracking privacy effects and attribution limits still apply. Six attributed orders do not prove six incremental orders or positive ROI.

Next experiment: test one call-to-action variation with a suitable comparison, delivered-message click rate as the primary measure and unsubscribe rate as a guardrail. Record costs and margin before evaluating profit. No email has been sent by this example.
