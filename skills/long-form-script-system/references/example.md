# Long-Form Script System: review draft without invented testing

## Creator input

> I'm reviewing a phone I own. I paid $499. I like the camera, performance feels solid, and the display seems less appealing to me than the phone I used before. I want a review outline with sample narration. I haven't collected battery tests or comparison photos. Do not invent those.

## Evidence ledger

| Item | Evidence | Use in script |
|---|---|---|
| Price paid | Creator statement | “I paid $499” |
| Camera opinion | Creator statement | Personal impression, not measured superiority |
| Performance opinion | Creator statement | Subjective experience, no benchmark |
| Display opinion | Creator statement | Subjective comparison only |
| Battery, dimensions, charging and chipset | Not supplied | Verify specs or leave out |
| Duration of ownership | Not supplied | Do not invent |
| Comparisons and photographs | Not supplied | Plan actual capture before asserting results |

## Three opening options

1. “I paid $499 for this phone. The camera is the part I like most; the display is the part I'm less enthusiastic about.”
2. “The camera, performance and display leave me with different impressions. Here's where each one stands for me.”
3. “A phone review is more useful when opinions and measurements are kept separate. Here's what I can say from using this one, and what I still need to test.”

## Script plan and sample narration

No source recording exists. The sections below are an outline and narration draft, not timestamped footage or a full-length finished script. Set a target length with the creator, then expand only with useful supported material.

### 1. Context

“I paid $499 for this phone. I'm sharing my own impressions of its camera, performance and display. I haven't run a battery benchmark or prepared controlled comparison photographs, so I won't give you those results today.”

**Visual plan:** Record the actual device and the creator using it. Add the actual model name once supplied; do not generate a misleading product-test image.

### 2. Camera

“The camera is the part I've liked most. That's my impression from using it. To make the review more useful, I'd like to show the photos behind that opinion.”

**Evidence to collect:** Ask the creator for their own photos and what they noticed in each. Use descriptions such as “I prefer this result” where appropriate. Reserve comparative performance claims for actual matched tests.

**Transition:** “The camera is one reason I enjoy using it. The next question is how the rest of the phone feels in daily use.”

### 3. Performance

“Performance has felt solid to me. I don't have benchmark scores here, so I'm describing how it feels rather than ranking its hardware.”

**Follow-up needed:** Ask which real tasks informed that impression. Include only the tasks the creator confirms. Do not assume gaming, travel or a three-week test period.

**Visual plan:** Film an actual relevant task if the creator wants a demonstration. A shot suggestion is not evidence that the task was performed.

### 4. Display

“The display hasn't appealed to me as much as the phone I used before. I'd want to explain which part matters to me rather than treating my preference as a universal verdict.”

**Follow-up needed:** What did the creator notice: readability, color, size, brightness, or something else? Confirm both model names and any specifications before a technical comparison.

### 5. What remains untested

“I don't have measured battery or charging results to share. If those are decisive for you, this review doesn't settle them.”

**Optional test plan:** Record the actual settings, tasks, start/end charge and elapsed time for a repeatable usage test. Describe its limits; a single personal test is not a universal battery-life figure.

### 6. Conclusion

“My current view is that I like the camera, performance feels solid, and the display is the compromise for me. Whether that fits you depends on what you care about most.”

**CTA:** “Which part would you want me to examine more closely?”

## Retention and production notes

- Open with the actual tradeoff rather than an unsupported “best phone” claim.
- Use the evidence ledger to decide what can be said now and what needs recording or checking.
- Keep transitions brief; do not pad to a target duration using invented experiences.
- Count spoken words after the creator supplies the missing details. Report the assumed words-per-minute rate and label duration estimated until a read-through is timed.
- No audio, footage, graphs or published video were produced by this text draft.
