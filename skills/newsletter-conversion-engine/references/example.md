# Newsletter Conversion Engine — Worked Examples

These fictional briefs demonstrate source-grounded drafts. They are not CreatorSkills customer results. Proposed topics and copy are labeled; confirm links, offer details, consent and scheduling before sending. No email has been sent.

## Example 1: A tech creator repurposes a video

### Supplied source

- The creator covers tech reviews and productivity and sends a weekly newsletter.
- They published a video titled “I Tried 12 Note-Taking Apps So You Don't Have To.”
- They tested Notion, Obsidian, Apple Notes, Bear, Logseq, Capacities, Craft, Evernote, OneNote, Google Keep, Simplenote and Upnote for 30 days each. The source does not say whether those periods overlapped.
- Their conclusion: Apple Notes was their choice for most people; Obsidian for power users. They thought Notion was overrated specifically for note-taking.
- They sell a course called Digital Declutter for $79. Course contents, outcomes, completion time and refund terms are not supplied.
- No personal origin story, measured performance improvement, customer feedback or other video titles are supplied.

### Newsletter draft

**Subject options:**
1. My picks after testing 12 note-taking apps
2. Apple Notes or Obsidian? Here's my take
3. Twelve apps, two recommendations

**Preview:** My note-taking comparison, in a shorter format.

Hey [first name],

I tested 12 note-taking apps for 30 days each. My choice for most people was Apple Notes; for power users, Obsidian.

I also came away thinking Notion was overrated for note-taking specifically. That's my conclusion from this comparison, not a verdict on every use of Notion.

The full lineup was Notion, Obsidian, Apple Notes, Bear, Logseq, Capacities, Craft, Evernote, OneNote, Google Keep, Simplenote and Upnote.

If you're choosing between them, the video is a place to start: [verified video URL].

What do you need your notes app to do? That question can help you decide which parts of a comparison matter to you.

[Name]

**Source map:** The test duration, app names and opinions come from the brief. The final question is proposed framing. The draft does not invent a year-long sequential test, migration history, software capabilities, speed measurements or audience percentages.

### Five-email welcome sequence: proposed drafts

This is an editorial sequence, not an observed conversion funnel. Send intervals and enrollment consent still need configuration.

**Email 1 — Welcome**

Subject: Welcome to the tech and productivity newsletter

Hey [first name],

Welcome. I cover tech reviews and productivity, and send this newsletter weekly.

To start, here's my comparison of 12 note-taking apps: [verified video URL]. My picks were Apple Notes for most people and Obsidian for power users.

Which tool are you trying to choose right now? You can reply with the task you need it to handle.

[Name]

**Email 2 — A question before another app**

Subject: What should your notes app help you do?

Hey [first name],

Before comparing another tool, try writing down one task you need it to handle. It might be capturing a thought, finding a note later or connecting related ideas.

Then use that task to guide your comparison. This is a suggested exercise; it doesn't require changing your setup or deleting anything.

[Name]

**Email 3 — The comparison**

Subject: Two picks from the note-taking test

Hey [first name],

In my 12-app comparison, Apple Notes was my choice for most people and Obsidian for power users.

Those are recommendations from this test. Your requirements may differ. If you'd like the full comparison, the video is here: [verified video URL].

[Name]

**Email 4 — A review you can try**

Subject: Choose one part of your digital setup to review

Hey [first name],

Here's a proposed exercise: pick one area of your digital setup and list what you use it for, what feels confusing and what you want to understand better.

Start with a review. Keep backups and account access intact; this isn't an instruction to delete files, remove tools or change security settings.

[Name]

**Email 5 — An optional course**

Subject: Digital Declutter is $79

Hey [first name],

I also offer a course called Digital Declutter for $79.

If you're interested, review the confirmed course contents and terms here before deciding: [verified course URL].

[Name]

**Before using this sequence:** Insert verified URLs and sender details. Do not add course modules, urgency, testimonials, time savings, personal history or results unless the creator supplies evidence. Emails 2 and 4 are proposed exercises, not claims that the creator tested them. Sending, subscriber management and performance measurement require the user's chosen email platform and explicit authorization.

## Example 2: A creator starts a newsletter

### Supplied source

The creator has 20,000 Instagram followers, posts daily about mindset, habits and personal growth, and has no newsletter yet. They have not chosen an email platform or launched a coaching offer. No subscriber results, testimonials or personal stories are supplied.

### Proposed lead magnet

**Working title:** Five prompts for planning your next week

**Landing-page draft:**

Set aside a little time to reflect on what you want to focus on. This proposed five-part email series offers one planning prompt per message. It does not promise a personal transformation or a result within five days.

[Get the prompts — activate only after the series, signup flow and consent notice are ready]

**Prompt sequence:**
1. What would you like to focus on next week?
2. What is making that task harder to start?
3. What is one smaller step you could choose?
4. What support or information would help?
5. What would you like to review afterward?

These prompts are newly proposed content. Their effectiveness and completion rate have not been measured.

### First newsletter draft

**Subject:** A question for your next weekly plan

**Preview:** Try choosing one thing to focus on.

Hey [first name],

Here's a prompt you can try before planning your week: “What would I like to focus on?”

Write one answer. Then consider a small next step and what might get in the way. You can revise the answer as your circumstances change; it doesn't need to become a rule for your whole week.

This is a suggested planning exercise, not a promise that you'll get more done. Keep what helps and adapt the rest.

[Name]

### Four proposed follow-up issues

| Topic | Subject draft | Evidence needed before adding a personal story |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing a manageable next step | Make the next step smaller | A real example supplied by the creator |
| Reviewing a plan | What would you change next week? | Actual observations, not invented productivity gains |
| Curating useful reading | One idea to consider | The source text and an accurate attribution |
| Expanding an Instagram post | A closer look at this idea | The exact post; reach and popularity claims need metrics |

### Platform selection and measurement

Compare current official plan limits, export options, consent controls and automation features before selecting a provider. No current vendor price or subscriber limit is assumed here.

After launch, record delivered emails, unique clicks, unsubscribes and any purchases with a defined attribution window. Report denominators and uncertainty. A sequence's structure alone is not proof of improved conversion.
