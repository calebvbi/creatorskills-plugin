# Membership Tier Review and Refresh

Produce a current-state audit, proposed tiers, fulfillment plan and voluntary transition plan. Never alter memberships, prices, entitlements or recurring charges as part of this planning workflow.

## Gather the current arrangement

Collect platform, tiers, prices, member counts, billing periods, included benefits, delivery time/cost, cancellation definitions and recent trends. Ask which commitments existing customers were promised. Record missing data instead of inferring member motivations from inactivity. Ask for redacted totals, not customer lists.

Calculate gross tier receipts as price × members, with the actual billing basis stated. Separate annual receipts, monthly equivalent revenue, fees, refunds and net contribution. Member share and revenue share are different measures. Check that tier counts sum to total members and that rates use consistent periods.

## Audit each tier

Review value clarity, actual benefit use, fulfillment cost, customer feedback and fit with the creator's capacity. A small tier is not automatically dead; it may serve a distinct need. Assign keep, clarify, test, redesign or investigate, with supporting evidence. Do not declare an industry conversion or churn benchmark without a dated, compatible source.

## Design proposed options

For each proposed tier state price as a creator-selected asking price, exact benefits, cadence, service limits, capacity and expected workload. Discuss tradeoffs in access, time and cost. Do not promise that most members will choose the middle tier or that annual billing will reduce churn by a fixed percentage.

Create optional participation scenarios only when useful. Show the assumed member distribution and all arithmetic. Label each as hypothetical; include no-change and downside cases. Unknown uptake, churn or costs must stay unknown. Scenarios do not justify changing existing customers' terms.

## Preserve existing commitments

Inventory current entitlements and any price-lock, lifetime or ongoing benefit promises. Keep existing arrangements in place unless a supported, explicitly agreed transition is authorized. Never assume silence means consent to higher charges or new terms. Verify the current platform's exact capabilities and requirements from official documentation before planning a migration.

A proposed transition should include existing-member options, an explicit opt-in path, confirmed dates, plain-language benefits and price differences, support contact and a remedy for any benefit that cannot be maintained. That remedy is a creator decision requiring review, not something this skill invents. Do not claim a migration or grandfathering mechanism exists until verified.

## Build a fulfillment and retention plan

Create a repeating calendar sized to available hours. Name each benefit's owner, input, delivery date and capacity. Write a welcome draft explaining how to use the benefits. Invite cancellation feedback without pressure. A win-back draft is optional and must respect recipient permissions and suppression; it is not sent automatically.

For a churn hypothesis, inspect cohort age, benefit use, pricing, acquisition sources and cancellation comments. Early cancellations might relate to onboarding, expectations or fit; they do not establish one cause. Pick one measurable improvement rather than asserting a universal remedy.

## Deliver and review

Return the audit, proposed tiers, workload estimate with assumptions, scenario table if requested, existing-customer plan, unsent announcement and measurement plan. Track cohort retention, voluntary opt-ins, benefit use, support burden and contribution with consistent definitions. Choose an observation window before interpreting changes.

State which platform behavior remains unverified. No posts, emails, billing changes or customer migrations have occurred without separate authorization and observed tool results.
