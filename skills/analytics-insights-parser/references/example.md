# Worked Examples: Findings and Testable Hypotheses

## Example 1: One video

Supplied synthetic fixture: a 9:40 home-improvement video about budget waterproofing has 14,200 views, 3.4% impression CTR, 3:52 average view duration, 210,000 impressions and traffic shares Browse 24%, Suggested 19%, Search 31%, External 26%. The creator has 22K subscribers. No comparable baseline or retention curve is supplied.

Calculation: 3:52 is 232 seconds; 9:40 is 580 seconds; 232/580 = 40% average percentage viewed. Browse and Suggested sum to 43%. The traffic shares total 100%. These facts do not identify a bottleneck. Total views and impression CTR may cover different eligible events, so do not force their product to equal all views.

Next useful evidence: comparable recent videos by source and age, plus this video's retention curve. Candidate hypotheses are a packaging mismatch, an early viewing drop or different distribution. None is established by the supplied aggregate values.

Five ideas to test from the supplied budget-waterproofing topic:

| Draft idea | Basis | Evidence needed before scripting |
|---|---|---|
| Budget waterproofing: choosing a first step | Supplied topic | Verified process and limitations |
| Compare two waterproofing approaches | Adjacent format hypothesis | Actual comparison material |
| Questions to ask before a basement repair | Audience-question hypothesis | Real questions or sourced guidance |
| What changed after a repair | Follow-up hypothesis | Creator's documented experience |
| A budget breakdown with assumptions | Budget angle | Actual costs and scope |

Do not claim any approach is proven to increase views. First action: obtain the retention curve and two comparable video records before recommending a re-edit.

## Example 2: Overlapping weeks

Supplied fixture: Week A, October 1–7, has 1,000 views, 50 link clicks and 2 uploads. Week B, October 5–11, has 1,500 views, 45 link clicks and 4 uploads. A title changed on October 5. The creator asks whether it improved conversion.

Observed click/view ratios are 5% and 3%, a decline of 2 percentage points or 40% relative. Views increased 50%; clicks decreased 10%; upload count doubled. These are link clicks per view, not impression CTR or purchase conversion.

October 5–7 appears in both windows. The weeks are not independent pre/post cohorts, Week A includes days after the change, and output volume differs. We cannot infer that the title caused either change. Ask for daily, video-level clicks/views and title exposure, plus impression CTR if packaging is the question. Next test: compare one variable using a supported randomized title test or clearly separated, comparable periods, recording audience/source changes and uncertainty. Do not pool these two weeks as independent evidence.
