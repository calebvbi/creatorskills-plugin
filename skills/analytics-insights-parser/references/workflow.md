# Analytics Insights Parser

Turn supplied creator analytics into a ranked diagnosis, five grounded content experiments and one immediate action. Accept tables, screenshots, exports or typed figures. Say which material was actually read. Never imply account access from a URL alone.

## 1. Validate the evidence

Record platform, content format, reporting dates/timezone, video age, metric definitions and denominators. Distinguish views, unique viewers, impressions, clicks, average view duration and average percentage viewed. Ask for the smallest missing fact that changes the decision. With only CTR and flat views, list packaging as one hypothesis, not an established cause.

Check arithmetic, missing rows, rounding, overlapping windows and changes in upload count. Do not pool overlapping cohorts. A percentage without its count may hide a tiny sample. Compare similar traffic sources, formats, lengths and observation windows. Mark incomplete data instead of inventing a benchmark.

## 2. Rank hypotheses

Use these diagnostic questions rather than universal thresholds:

- Distribution: did measured impressions change, within comparable traffic sources?
- Packaging: did impression CTR change for comparable audiences and exposure windows?
- Viewing: where does the supplied retention curve change, and does the relevant script or edit support a plausible explanation?
- Audience fit: do returning-viewer and subscriber measures support the proposed content strategy?
- Output volume: could aggregate changes be explained by more or fewer uploads?

Use the creator's own relevant baseline when available. An external benchmark needs a source, date, population and matching definition; omit it otherwise. Do not infer an algorithm decision from a threshold. Average percentage viewed does not tell you how many viewers reached a particular timestamp.

For each issue, provide observed evidence, hypothesis, competing explanations, confidence limited by the sample and the next measurement. Rank by likely decision value and effort without fabricating money or reach lost.

## 3. Compute useful comparisons

For a rate, show numerator/denominator. For changes, distinguish percentage points from relative percent. A rise from 3% to 4% is +1 percentage point and about +33.3% relative. That is an observation, not proof a title change caused it.

Calculate aggregate rates from summed compatible counts, not an unweighted average of unequal rates. Do not divide all views by impressions and call it impression CTR unless the platform definitions match. Separate view totals from impression-attributed views.

A what-if estimate may hold impressions fixed and vary CTR if requested. Label it a scenario, list the assumption and state that changes in audience or distribution could invalidate it. Never present the scenario as expected growth.

## 4. Produce five content experiments

Each idea must reference a supplied topic, format, audience question or measured pattern. Separate evidence from creative extrapolation. With one video, offer adjacent hypotheses rather than claiming a proven format. Do not invent the creator's personal experience, testing history or results in proposed titles.

For each experiment include working title, source observation, one proposed variable, required source material and success measure. Keep the number or format requested by the user; five is the default.

## 5. Deliver a short report

1. Evidence and important missing inputs.
2. Ranked hypotheses with confidence and competing explanations.
3. What appears to be working, limited to the observed sample.
4. Five content experiments.
5. One immediate action, measurement window and guardrail.

On follow-up, compare the same definitions and exposure windows. Report improvement or regression without attributing it to an intervention unless the test design supports that claim. If windows overlap or several things changed, say why attribution remains unresolved and propose a cleaner test. Do not publish, edit or delete content from an analytics request.
