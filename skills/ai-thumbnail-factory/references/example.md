## Example 1: Tech Review Channel

### Creator Context

- **Channel:** TechDaily with Marcus
- **Subscribers:** 52,000
- **Niche:** Consumer tech reviews and comparisons
- **Typical views:** 20,000-60,000 per video
- **Audience:** 22-38 year olds, mostly men, interested in gadgets, work-from-home setups, and getting the best value
- **Brand style:** Clean, dark backgrounds, blue accents, Marcus appears in thumbnails
- **Brand colors:** Dark navy (#1a1a2e), electric blue (#0099ff), white (#ffffff)

### Input Given

```
I'm making a video comparing the new Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra
vs iPhone 17 Pro Max — camera, battery, performance, and value.
Title I'm considering: "Galaxy S26 Ultra vs iPhone 17 Pro Max — The HONEST Comparison"
I use my face in thumbnails. My brand colors are navy and blue.
```

### Full AI Output

---

**Hook Analysis:** The core emotion here is resolution — your audience is actively deciding between these two phones and wants someone they trust to settle it. The scroll-stop factor is the direct confrontation: two flagship phones, head to head, with a clear winner promised. This should feel like the final verdict.

---

#### Concept 1: The Versus Split (Recommended)

**Why this archetype:** This is THE format for head-to-head comparisons. Your audience expects it, and it immediately communicates what the video delivers. The key is executing it better than every other tech channel doing the same comparison.

**Visual Layout**
- **Composition:** Classic 50/50 split. Left side: Galaxy S26 Ultra held at a slight angle, showing the screen. Right side: iPhone 17 Pro Max in the same pose. A bold dividing line runs down the center with "VS" overlaid.
- **Primary element:** Both phones, shot at similar angles so neither looks favored
- **Secondary element:** Marcus's face at the bottom center, overlapping both sides, looking directly at camera with a "let's settle this" confident expression
- **Background:** Left side has a subtle Samsung-blue gradient (#1428a0 to #0a1628). Right side has an Apple-style warm gray gradient (#3a3a3c to #1c1c1e).

**Text Overlay**
- **Copy:** VS (center, large)
- **Font style:** Bebas Neue or Impact, ultra bold, with a bright white glow effect
- **Placement:** Dead center of the frame, overlapping the dividing line
- **Color:** White (#ffffff) with electric blue outer glow (#0099ff)

**Color Palette**
- **Primary:** #1428a0 (Samsung blue) — left background
- **Secondary:** #3a3a3c (Apple gray) — right background
- **Accent:** #0099ff (Marcus's brand blue) — VS text glow and dividing line

**Emotional Target**
- **First impression:** "This is the comparison that'll help me decide."
- **Scroll-stop factor:** Two recognizable flagship phones in direct confrontation. If the viewer is even slightly interested in either phone, they can't scroll past without knowing the verdict.

**Image Generation Prompt**
```
A YouTube thumbnail background image in a clean, professional tech review style.
Split composition: the left half has a deep blue gradient background (#1428a0 fading
to dark navy), the right half has a dark gray gradient background (#3a3a3c fading to
near-black). A thin bright blue vertical line divides the two halves down the center.
Both sides have subtle light rays emanating from the center. The overall mood is
premium, high-stakes, and authoritative. 16:9 aspect ratio, landscape orientation.
Do not include any text, phones, people, watermarks, or logos.
```

**Canva/Manual Execution Notes**
Find high-resolution press images of both phones from Samsung and Apple press kits. Place each phone on its respective side at matching angles. Cut out Marcus from a photo where he's looking confident and directly at camera, place him bottom-center overlapping both halves. Add the "VS" text in Bebas Neue at 200pt+ with a blue outer glow effect.

---

#### Concept 2: The Bold Statement

**Why this archetype:** Instead of the expected versus format, this leads with the provocative verdict — forcing viewers who disagree to click and argue, and those who agree to click and confirm. Controversy drives clicks.

**Visual Layout**
- **Composition:** Full-width image of both phones lying flat on a dark surface, slightly overlapping. Bold text takes up the top 50% of the frame.
- **Primary element:** The text statement
- **Secondary element:** Both phones beneath the text, with one clearly positioned "on top" of the other (visually suggesting a winner)
- **Background:** Dark navy (#1a1a2e) with subtle light falloff from the center

**Text Overlay**
- **Copy:** IT'S NOT EVEN CLOSE
- **Font style:** Montserrat Black, all caps, with a slight perspective tilt for dynamism
- **Placement:** Top half of the frame, centered
- **Color:** White (#ffffff) with a thin dark navy outline for depth

**Color Palette**
- **Primary:** #1a1a2e (dark navy) — background
- **Secondary:** #ffffff (white) — text
- **Accent:** #0099ff (electric blue) — subtle underline or highlight on the text

**Emotional Target**
- **First impression:** "Wait — which one won? And by THAT much?"
- **Scroll-stop factor:** The bold claim provokes a reaction regardless of which phone the viewer prefers. Samsung fans want to see their phone win. iPhone fans want to see if it lost. Everyone clicks.

**Image Generation Prompt**
```
A YouTube thumbnail image of two premium smartphones lying flat on a dark navy
surface, slightly overlapping each other at an angle. The phones are generic (no
visible branding) with glowing screens. Dramatic overhead lighting creates sharp
reflections on the glass surfaces. The mood is premium and decisive. Dark, moody
lighting with a single soft spotlight from above. 16:9 aspect ratio, landscape
orientation. Do not include any text, watermarks, or logos.
```

**Canva/Manual Execution Notes**
Use the generated image as the base. Replace the generic phones with actual product images of the S26 Ultra and iPhone 17 Pro Max. Add "IT'S NOT EVEN CLOSE" in Montserrat Black across the top half. Optional: add a subtle blue line under the text for brand consistency.

---

#### Concept 3: The Reaction Face

**Why this archetype:** Marcus's face builds trust and brand recognition with returning viewers, and the shocked expression creates curiosity about what surprised him during testing. This works especially well if the test results were genuinely unexpected.

**Visual Layout**
- **Composition:** Marcus on the right side (55% of frame), face close-up showing genuine surprise — raised eyebrows, slightly open mouth. Left side shows both phones with one crossed out with a red X.
- **Primary element:** Marcus's surprised face
- **Secondary element:** The two phones on the left, one with a red X overlay
- **Background:** Solid electric blue (#0099ff) to bright navy (#1a1a2e) gradient

**Text Overlay**
- **Copy:** ACTUALLY WORSE??
- **Font style:** Impact, all caps, heavy weight
- **Placement:** Upper left, above the phones
- **Color:** Yellow (#ffd700) with a dark outline — maximum contrast against the blue background

**Emotional Target**
- **First impression:** "One of these phones is actually BAD? Which one??"
- **Scroll-stop factor:** The combination of Marcus's surprise plus a crossed-out flagship phone creates double curiosity. The viewer needs to know which phone failed and what test exposed it.

**Image Generation Prompt**
```
A YouTube thumbnail background with a smooth gradient from electric blue (#0099ff)
on the left to dark navy (#1a1a2e) on the right. Clean and slightly glossy look,
with a very subtle radial light burst from the upper left corner. No objects, no
text, no people. 16:9 aspect ratio, landscape orientation.
```

**Canva/Manual Execution Notes**
Use the gradient background. Cut out Marcus from a photo with a surprised/shocked expression and place him on the right. Find product images of both phones, place them on the left side vertically stacked. Add a red X graphic over one phone (don't reveal the "loser" — let the video do that). Add "ACTUALLY WORSE??" in yellow Impact font upper-left.

---

### My Pick: Concept 1

**Why:** For a 52K-subscriber tech channel, the Versus Split is the right balance of searchability and click-through rate. People actively searching "S26 Ultra vs iPhone 17" expect this format and will click the most polished version of it. Marcus's face at the bottom adds personal brand equity without sacrificing the product-first approach his audience comes for.

**A/B Test Suggestion:** Test Concept 1 against Concept 2 for the first 24 hours. If Concept 2's bold claim drives a higher CTR in the first 2 hours, it's catching attention in browse feeds. If Concept 1 leads, your search traffic (people looking for this specific comparison) is driving views — and that's the right audience for a comparison video.

## Example 2: Cooking Channel

### Creator Context

- **Channel:** Simply Nia
- **Subscribers:** 14,500
- **Niche:** Quick, healthy recipes for people who hate cooking
- **Typical views:** 5,000-12,000 per video
- **Audience:** 23-35 year olds, mostly women, busy professionals who want to eat better but find cooking intimidating
- **Brand style:** Bright, airy, clean white backgrounds with pops of color from the food itself
- **Brand colors:** Warm white (#faf8f5), sage green (#7a9e7e), soft coral (#e8847c)

### Input Given

```
New video: 5 meals I eat every week that take under 15 minutes.
These are genuinely the meals I make on repeat — nothing fancy.
Title: "5 Lazy Girl Meals That Changed How I Eat (Under 15 Min)"
I don't use my face in thumbnails. Clean and bright style.
```

### Full AI Output

---

**Hook Analysis:** The core emotion is aspiration mixed with relief — your audience wants to eat better but feels overwhelmed by cooking. "Lazy girl meals" gives them permission to keep it simple, and "changed how I eat" promises transformation without effort. The scroll-stop factor is showing food that looks amazing but clearly simple — the visual proof that "easy" doesn't mean "sad desk lunch."

---

#### Concept 1: The Countdown Number + Food Grid (Recommended)

**Why this archetype:** A bold "5" anchors the frame while showing all five meals creates a visual menu the viewer wants to explore. For recipe content, showing the actual food is the strongest possible hook — people eat with their eyes first.

**Visual Layout**
- **Composition:** Large "5" on the left side (30% of frame). The remaining 70% is a clean grid of the 5 meals — two on top, two in the middle, one centered at bottom, each in a simple white bowl on a light surface. Small gaps between each photo create a tiled look.
- **Primary element:** The 5 food photos arranged as a visual grid
- **Secondary element:** The bold number "5" anchoring the left side
- **Background:** Warm white (#faf8f5) consistent with Nia's brand

**Text Overlay**
- **Copy:** 5 (large number) + EVERY WEEK (small subtitle)
- **Font style:** The "5" in a bold rounded sans-serif (Nunito Black or similar). "EVERY WEEK" in a lighter weight beneath it.
- **Placement:** Left side, vertically centered
- **Color:** Sage green (#7a9e7e) for the "5", soft coral (#e8847c) for "EVERY WEEK"

**Color Palette**
- **Primary:** #faf8f5 (warm white) — background and overall feel
- **Secondary:** #7a9e7e (sage green) — number and accents
- **Accent:** #e8847c (soft coral) — secondary text and subtle frame borders

**Emotional Target**
- **First impression:** "Those look so good AND simple — I could actually make those."
- **Scroll-stop factor:** Five distinct, colorful meals displayed together creates visual richness that stands out in a feed. The viewer's eye bounces between dishes trying to identify each one — that engagement buys the 3 seconds needed to earn a click.

**Image Generation Prompt**
```
A YouTube thumbnail image showing a flat lay overhead shot of 5 different healthy
meals in simple white ceramic bowls arranged in a grid pattern on a warm white linen
surface. The meals should look homemade but appetizing: a colorful grain bowl, a
simple pasta with vegetables, a fresh salad with protein, a stir-fry with rice, and
a soup with bread on the side. Bright, airy, natural lighting from the left. The
style is minimal, clean, and inviting — like a food magazine shot. 16:9 aspect ratio,
landscape orientation. Do not include any text, hands, utensils, or watermarks.
```

**Canva/Manual Execution Notes**
Photograph the actual 5 meals from directly overhead on a white or linen surface with natural window light. Arrange the 5 photos in a grid in Canva using the grid layout feature. Add the "5" in Nunito Black on the left side in sage green. Add "EVERY WEEK" in a smaller size beneath it in coral.

---

#### Concept 2: The Before/After Split

**Why this archetype:** This plays on the "changed how I eat" promise in the title. Showing the sad reality of what Nia's audience currently eats vs. what they COULD be eating in the same amount of time creates an irresistible visual argument.

**Visual Layout**
- **Composition:** Left 45%: a sad, gray-toned image of stereotypical lazy meals — takeout containers, a sad microwave dinner, cereal for dinner. Right 55%: a bright, warm, gorgeous spread of the 5 meals from the video — colorful, fresh, steaming. A clean white dividing line separates the two sides.
- **Primary element:** The contrast between the two food realities
- **Secondary element:** A simple curved arrow from left to right suggesting transformation
- **Background:** Desaturated/cool on the left, warm and bright on the right

**Text Overlay**
- **Copy:** SAME TIME (positioned at the bottom center, bridging both sides)
- **Font style:** Montserrat Bold, clean and modern
- **Placement:** Bottom center, overlapping the dividing line
- **Color:** White (#ffffff) with a subtle sage green shadow

**Color Palette**
- **Primary:** Desaturated gray-blue (#c5c8cc) — "before" side
- **Secondary:** Warm white (#faf8f5) — "after" side
- **Accent:** #7a9e7e (sage green) — arrow and text shadow

**Emotional Target**
- **First impression:** "I'm literally eating the left side right now... but the right side takes the same amount of time??"
- **Scroll-stop factor:** The personal recognition of the "before" side creates an emotional jolt. The viewer sees their own current reality and an achievable alternative in the same frame.

**Image Generation Prompt**
```
A YouTube thumbnail split image. Left side: an overhead photo of a sad, messy desk
with a half-eaten microwave meal in a black plastic tray, a cereal box, and a
crumpled napkin on a gray surface — desaturated, dim lighting, looks depressing.
Right side: an overhead photo of a beautiful, colorful healthy meal in a white bowl
on a bright white surface — fresh vegetables, grains, vibrant colors, natural warm
lighting. A thin white vertical line divides the two sides. 16:9 aspect ratio,
landscape orientation. Do not include any text, people, or watermarks.
```

**Canva/Manual Execution Notes**
Photograph or find stock images representing both sides. Apply a desaturation filter and slightly blue color grade to the "before" side. Keep the "after" side warm and bright. Add a thin white dividing line and a curved arrow pointing left to right. Place "SAME TIME" in white Montserrat Bold at the bottom center.

---

#### Concept 3: The Aspirational Outcome

**Why this archetype:** Instead of showing the process or comparing, this concept simply shows the single most beautiful meal from the video and lets it speak for itself. The visual argument is: "This is what a 15-minute meal looks like." The simplicity stands out in a feed full of cluttered cooking thumbnails.

**Visual Layout**
- **Composition:** A single, stunning overhead shot of the most photogenic meal from the 5, centered in the frame. Lots of white space around it. Clean, minimal, editorial.
- **Primary element:** One gorgeous meal in a white bowl
- **Secondary element:** A few scattered fresh ingredients around the bowl (a lemon wedge, fresh herbs, a drizzle of olive oil mid-pour) to add life and movement
- **Background:** Clean white surface with natural shadows

**Text Overlay**
- **Copy:** 15 MIN
- **Font style:** Thin, elegant sans-serif (Quicksand or Josefin Sans) in all caps
- **Placement:** Upper right corner, clean and minimal
- **Color:** Sage green (#7a9e7e)

**Color Palette**
- **Primary:** #faf8f5 (warm white) — background
- **Secondary:** Natural food colors — greens, oranges, reds from the ingredients
- **Accent:** #7a9e7e (sage green) — text only

**Emotional Target**
- **First impression:** "That looks like it came from a restaurant, and she made it in 15 minutes?"
- **Scroll-stop factor:** The deliberate minimalism stands out in a feed full of busy, text-heavy cooking thumbnails. One beautiful meal with one simple time stamp. The elegance itself is the differentiator.

**Image Generation Prompt**
```
A YouTube thumbnail image showing a single beautiful healthy grain bowl photographed
from directly overhead on a clean white marble surface. The bowl is white ceramic and
contains colorful fresh ingredients — quinoa, roasted sweet potato, avocado slices,
cherry tomatoes, microgreens, and a drizzle of tahini. A few fresh herb leaves and a
lemon wedge are scattered artfully beside the bowl. Bright, airy natural lighting
from the left. Editorial food photography style — clean, minimal, appetizing. 16:9
aspect ratio, landscape orientation. Do not include any text, hands, or watermarks.
```

**Canva/Manual Execution Notes**
This concept works best with a real photo of the actual meal from the video — authenticity matters for a "meals I eat every week" video. Shoot overhead with natural light on a white surface. In Canva, add "15 MIN" in Quicksand or a similar elegant font. Keep the overall design sparse — resist adding more elements.

---

### My Pick: Concept 1

**Why:** For a 14.5K-subscriber channel, discoverability matters more than brand mystique. The food grid with the bold "5" communicates maximum value — viewers see five distinct meals and a clear number promise. This format performs consistently well for recipe compilation videos because it gives the viewer a visual table of contents that makes them want to see each recipe in detail.

**A/B Test Suggestion:** Test Concept 1 against Concept 3 for the first 48 hours. Concept 1 should win on raw CTR (it communicates more value), but if Concept 3's minimalist approach outperforms, it means Nia's audience responds to editorial aesthetics — and she should lean into that for future thumbnails.
