# Worked Example — Audience Growth Stall Audit

## Creator Context

- **Platform:** YouTube
- **Channel:** Personal finance for millennials (budgeting, investing, side income)
- **Subscribers:** 28,400
- **Active for:** 2 years
- **Posting cadence:** Weekly — every Tuesday for 18 months, then dropped to bi-weekly 3 months ago
- **Target audience:** 25–35 year-olds earning $45–$90k who feel behind on savings

**90-day trend data:**
- Net new subscribers: +110 in month 1, +62 in month 2, +38 in month 3
- Impressions: down 31% vs. prior 90-day period
- CTR: 3.8% (was 5.2% six months ago)
- Average view duration: 42% (was 48%)

**Last 10 videos (title + views at 30 days):**
1. "I Saved $10,000 in 12 Months on a $52k Salary" — 41,200 views
2. "The 3 Accounts You Need Before You Invest" — 18,700 views
3. "Why Your Budget Always Breaks by Day 15" — 14,300 views
4. "Roth IRA Explained in 7 Minutes" — 9,800 views
5. "My Honest Review of YNAB After 6 Months" — 8,100 views
6. "How I Think About Spending Money (No Guilt Method)" — 6,400 views
7. "Index Funds vs. ETFs — What's the Difference?" — 5,900 views
8. "Q&A: Your Biggest Money Questions Answered" — 4,200 views
9. "The Sinking Fund System I Use for Every Big Expense" — 3,900 views
10. "January Budget Recap + Net Worth Update" — 3,100 views

**What changed when growth slowed:**
- Dropped from weekly to bi-weekly posting 3 months ago
- Started covering more "foundational" finance topics (index funds, ETFs) to fill out the catalog
- Thumbnails shifted from personal/story-driven to more "explainer" style

---

## Diagnosis

**Your primary growth leak is: CTR + Content-Audience Misalignment**

The data tells a clear story. Your #1 video ("I Saved $10,000 in 12 Months on a $52k Salary") is pulling 4x the views of everything else on the list. That's not a fluke — it's your audience telling you exactly what they want: personal, specific, relatable money stories. Your last 8 videos have moved away from that toward generic finance education content (Roth IRA Explained, Index Funds vs. ETFs), and the numbers reflect it.

**Three data points that prove the diagnosis:**

1. CTR dropped from 5.2% to 3.8% — a 27% decline. This means the thumbnails and titles aren't resonating before the click happens. Your story-based titles ("I Saved $10,000...") outperform your explainer titles ("Roth IRA Explained in 7 Minutes") by a factor of 4–5x in views.

2. The performance cliff is clean. Videos 1–3 are your story-based content. Videos 4–10 are explainers and evergreen fundamentals. The drop-off is not gradual — it's a step change between your two content modes.

3. View duration also slipped (48% → 42%). Your audience watches longer when they're invested in YOUR story. When you switch to teaching general concepts, they disengage faster.

The posting frequency drop to bi-weekly is a secondary factor — it reduces your algorithm exposure and slows subscriber velocity — but it's not the root cause. Even if you went back to weekly, you'd be posting more of the content that isn't converting.

**What not to do:**
- Don't chase the explainer format harder thinking SEO will rescue it. Foundational topics are covered by channels with 500k+ subscribers who outrank you.
- Don't change your niche. Your niche is working — your execution of it drifted.
- Don't rethink your posting schedule first. Fix the content direction, then restore frequency.

## 30-Day Recovery Protocol

### Week 1: Reset your content signal

**Action:** Post one video in your proven format — personal story, specific dollar amounts, relatable scenario. Use your highest-performing video as the template, not the inspiration.

**Title formula:** "[I/We] [did specific thing] on [specific income/timeframe]" or "Why [relatable mistake] is costing you [specific amount]"

**Thumbnail formula:** Your face + a number or text overlay with the key stat from the video. No stock art, no abstract graphics.

**Goal:** Get one piece of content back in your CTR sweet spot (4.5%+) before the week ends. This resets the algorithm's content model for your channel.

---

### Week 2: Audit your last 12 months

**Action:** Pull CTR and 30-day view data for every video from the past year. Sort them by CTR, not views. Identify the top 5 and bottom 5. Write down exactly what the top 5 have in common (topic type, title structure, thumbnail style, video length).

**Output:** A one-page "content playbook" for your channel — what you post, how you title it, how you thumbnail it. This is your creative brief for the next 90 days.

---

### Week 3: Post your second recovery video

**Action:** Apply the playbook from Week 2. If your top performers are "I paid off X debt in Y months" style, make another one. If your best titles include a specific dollar amount, put one in the title.

**Don't:** Start a series, pivot to shorts, or add a new format. One variable at a time.

---

### Week 4: Restore posting frequency

**Action:** Return to weekly publishing. Now that your content direction is dialed in, volume helps. More surface area = more impressions = more subscribers.

**Watch:** CTR (target: 4.5%+), 30-day views on new videos, and subscriber net adds per week. If your Week 3 video hits within 20% of your Week 1 recovery video, the playbook is working.

---

## Metrics to Watch

1. **CTR** — Your primary diagnostic signal. Target: back to 4.5%+ within 30 days.
2. **30-day views on new videos** — Compare to your story-based content baseline, not your recent explainers.
3. **Net new subscribers per week** — Should rebound within 3–4 weeks of consistent story-based content.

**Note:** You don't need to delete or unlist your explainer videos. Leave them — they may still get search traffic. Just don't make more of them as your primary format until your CTR recovers.
