# Email Subject Line Optimizer — Worked Examples

---

## Example 1: Newsletter About Productivity Tips

### Creator Input

```
My newsletter this week is about the "time blocking" productivity method.
I'm sharing 3 specific blocks I use every day as a full-time creator, plus
a free template readers can download. My audience is part-time creators
trying to get more done without burning out. Tone is friendly and practical.
```

### Generated Output

---

**Subject Line Options**

**1. Curiosity Gap**
`The 3 blocks I protect no matter what`

**2. Benefit-Driven**
`Get 3 more focused hours out of every workday`

**3. Intrigue / Specificity**
`My exact daily schedule (+ free template inside)`

**4. Personal / Direct Address**
`If your days feel scattered, try this`

**5. Question-Based**
`What if your best work happened before noon?`

---

**My Recommendation**
Go with **Option 3** — "My exact daily schedule (+ free template inside)." It's specific, it calls out the free resource, and "exact" signals real, actionable content rather than generic advice. The word "inside" pulls readers in without overpromising.

---

**Optional: A/B Test Pair**
Test **Option 3** vs **Option 1**. Option 3 sells the tangible asset (template); Option 1 sells the personal angle (curiosity about what the creator protects). The winner will tell you whether your audience responds more to concrete resources or personal storytelling.

---

### Why This Works

**Option 1 (Curiosity Gap)** withholds the specific blocks — readers can't know what they are without opening. The phrase "no matter what" adds stakes without being dramatic.

**Option 2 (Benefit-Driven)** leads with the outcome ("3 more focused hours") and puts a number on it, which performs consistently well for productivity content. It's honest — the email actually delivers techniques that create focus time.

**Option 3 (Intrigue / Specificity)** is used instead of Urgency because this email has no deadline or scarcity. "My exact daily schedule" is specific and personal; "(+ free template inside)" adds a second reason to open. This is why the skill switches to Intrigue/Specificity when urgency doesn't apply.

**Option 4 (Personal)** speaks directly to the frustration the audience already feels. "Scattered days" is a precise pain point for part-time creators, and "try this" is an invitation rather than a command.

**Option 5 (Question)** uses an aspirational angle. The question isn't obvious — it reframes when productive work happens, which is counterintuitive enough to create genuine curiosity.

## Example 2: Launch Announcement Email

### Creator Input

```
I'm launching my new course "YouTube Script System" on Friday. Doors are
open for 5 days only. The course teaches creators how to write engaging
scripts 3x faster using a modular template system. Early bird price is
$97 (regular $147). My list knows me from my YouTube channel about
content creation. Tone is confident but not pushy.
```

### Generated Output

---

**Subject Line Options**

**1. Curiosity Gap**
`The template I use for every video script`

**2. Benefit-Driven**
`Write better scripts 3x faster — doors open now`

**3. Urgency / Scarcity**
`YouTube Script System is live (5 days only)`

**4. Personal / Direct Address**
`You asked how I script videos — here's the full system`

**5. Question-Based**
`Spending hours on scripts that don't convert?`

---

**My Recommendation**
Use **Option 4** — "You asked how I script videos — here's the full system." Launch emails from creators succeed when they feel like a natural continuation of the relationship. This option references prior conversations (or the impression of one), which lowers the guard of readers who are skeptical of sales emails. It also describes the product accurately without feeling like a pitch.

---

**Optional: A/B Test Pair**
Test **Option 4** vs **Option 3**. Option 4 relies on relationship and personal credibility; Option 3 uses a direct announcement with a clear deadline. The winner tells you whether your audience buys on trust or responds to explicit time constraints.

---

### Why This Works

**Option 1 (Curiosity Gap)** works for a launch because it leads with value (the template) rather than the sale. Readers who are interested in scripting will open based on the template alone — then encounter the course offer inside. This is a softer entry point for readers who are skeptical of launch emails.

**Option 2 (Benefit-Driven)** names the core promise of the course ("3x faster") and announces the launch in plain language. It's direct and honest — the right fit for audiences who appreciate efficiency over warmth.

**Option 3 (Urgency / Scarcity)** works here because the 5-day window is real. The name of the course in the subject line also helps — subscribers who've heard the creator mention it before will recognize it immediately. Urgency is earned, not manufactured.

**Option 4 (Personal)** frames the launch email as a response to audience demand, which reframes the sale as service. "You asked" creates a sense of obligation fulfilled. "Full system" signals completeness and value. This archetype performs especially well with engaged, loyal audiences.

**Option 5 (Question)** opens with the pain point the course solves. It's a good choice if the creator's list is newer or less engaged — it hooks readers on the problem before introducing the solution.

---

## Key Takeaways

**One input, five angles.** Each subject line is built for a different reader mindset. Some of your subscribers are driven by curiosity, others by practical outcomes, others by relationship. Having five options means you can pick the one that fits both your email and your audience's current mood.

**Specificity beats cleverness.** "Write better scripts 3x faster" outperforms "The secret to better scripts" because it tells the reader exactly what to expect. Vague subject lines feel like bait; specific ones feel like value.

**Match the archetype to the email type.** Relationship newsletters tend to perform best with Personal or Curiosity options. Launch emails tend to perform best with Benefit-Driven or Urgency options. The skill's recommendation accounts for this — but your knowledge of your audience is the final call.

**Test when you can.** A/B subject line testing is the fastest way to learn what your specific audience responds to. Even one test per month builds meaningful data over time.
