## Example 1: Cooking Channel Expanding to TikTok and Newsletters

### Creator Profile

- **Channel:** Everyday Eats
- **Primary platform:** YouTube (200K subscribers)
- **Content type:** 10-15 minute recipe videos (film once weekly, edit with a freelance editor)
- **Niche:** Weeknight dinner recipes for busy families
- **Audience:** 70% women, 28-45 years old, primarily US/Canada/UK
- **Current posting:** 1 YouTube video/week, sporadic Instagram posts (35K followers, mostly food photos)
- **Goal:** Expand to TikTok and start a newsletter. Wants to build a direct audience off YouTube.
- **Capacity:** 15 hours/week, plus a freelance video editor

### Content Extraction Summary

Strong position. 200K YouTube subscribers in cooking means an established audience that already trusts the creator's taste and instruction style. The content is inherently visual and repurposable — cooking translates well to short-form video. The audience demographic (28-45 women, families) is active on Instagram, Pinterest, and increasingly on TikTok's food side. The newsletter goal is smart — it builds an owned audience that survives algorithm changes.

Current weakness: Instagram is underutilized (35K followers with 200K YouTube subscribers suggests inconsistent posting and no clear Instagram strategy). No TikTok presence means starting from zero but the content library is massive.

## Example 2: Business Coach Adding YouTube and Twitter

### Creator Profile

- **Channel:** Coach Marcus
- **Primary platform:** LinkedIn (50K followers)
- **Content type:** Text posts (daily), occasional LinkedIn Lives, one LinkedIn article per month
- **Niche:** Sales coaching for B2B SaaS founders and sales teams
- **Audience:** 80% male, 30-50 years old, founders and VP-level sales leaders, primarily US
- **Current posting:** 5 LinkedIn posts/week, 1 LinkedIn Live/month
- **Goal:** Add YouTube for long-form content and Twitter for reach. Wants to build a personal brand beyond LinkedIn and drive sales for his $5,000 coaching program.
- **Capacity:** 12 hours/week, solo (no team, considering hiring a part-time VA)

### Content Extraction Summary

Marcus has strong credentials (50K LinkedIn followers in B2B sales is a serious authority position) but is platform-dependent. If LinkedIn changes its algorithm or deprioritizes his content type, his business pipeline dries up. Smart to diversify.

YouTube makes sense for long-form coaching content — "how to close enterprise deals" or "cold email teardowns" are search-heavy topics with sustained traffic. Twitter is a natural extension of the LinkedIn voice (professional, opinionated, direct) and can cross-pollinate with the LinkedIn audience.

Key risk: 12 hours/week solo is tight for LinkedIn + YouTube + Twitter. YouTube has the highest startup cost (filming, editing, learning the platform). He needs to prioritize ruthlessly or hire help.

## Example 3: Gaming Streamer Growing on YouTube Shorts and TikTok

### Creator Profile

- **Channel:** Nyx (gaming handle)
- **Primary platform:** Twitch (10K followers, avg 80-120 concurrent viewers)
- **Content type:** Live streams (Valorant, variety gaming), 4-5 streams per week, 3-4 hours each
- **Niche:** FPS gaming with commentary, humor-driven, competitive but not full esports
- **Audience:** 85% male, 16-28 years old, US/EU, very online
- **Current posting:** Twitch streams only. No YouTube, no TikTok, no clips being posted anywhere.
- **Goal:** Grow on YouTube Shorts and TikTok to drive Twitch viewership and build a sustainable audience. Wants to eventually do YouTube full-time.
- **Capacity:** Streams 15-20 hours/week already. Has about 5 additional hours for content repurposing. No team, no editor.

### Content Extraction Summary

Nyx is sitting on a goldmine of unclipped content. 4-5 streams per week at 3-4 hours each generates 15-20 hours of raw footage every week — and none of it is being repurposed. In gaming, clips are the primary growth driver. The best gaming creators on TikTok and YouTube Shorts are pulling from stream highlights, and the audience (16-28 male, very online) is exactly the demographic that lives on those platforms.

The constraint is real: 5 hours per week for repurposing with no editor. The strategy needs to be high-volume but low-production-cost. Clipping and posting must be as efficient as possible.

#### Content Format per Platform

**Twitch (maintain):**
- Keep the 4-5 streams/week cadence
- Add one change: use a stream deck button or OBS hotkey to timestamp "clip-worthy moments" during stream. This saves hours during the clipping process.
- Consider running shorter "clip farming" streams (1-2 hours) focused on high-action gameplay specifically designed to generate short-form content

**YouTube Shorts (new):**
- 5-7 Shorts per week, clipped from streams
- Format: 15-45 seconds, vertical crop (or zoom into the gameplay with face cam overlay), captions on screen
- Content types: insane plays, funny moments, rage clips, clutch rounds, fails
- Use a free/cheap clipping tool (like Medal, Outplayed, or manual OBS replay buffer) to capture moments during stream

**TikTok (new):**
- Cross-post YouTube Shorts content (re-export without watermark)
- Same clips, same format — but add trending sounds when it fits (gaming + trending audio does well on TikTok)
- 5-7 per week (same clips as Shorts, just cross-posted)
- TikTok-specific: occasional "reacting to clips" or "storytime while gaming" format for variety

**YouTube Long-Form (new, low frequency):**
- 2 videos per month: "Best of [Month]" compilations (10-15 minutes of best stream moments)
- Compilation editing is faster than scripted content — it's just sequencing existing clips
- Gradually transition to scripted content (game reviews, guides, commentary) as the channel grows

**Twitter/X (new):**
- Post 1-2 clips per day (same clips going to Shorts/TikTok, just as native Twitter video)
- Tweet reactions to game updates, patch notes, competitive results
- Engage with other gaming creators — reply game is huge in gaming Twitter
- 5-10 tweets per day (clips, commentary, replies, memes)

#### Clipping Workflow

This is the production bottleneck, so it needs to be optimized:

```
DURING STREAM:
  → Hit "clip" hotkey whenever something noteworthy happens
  → Aim for 5-10 clip markers per 3-hour stream

POST-STREAM (batch 2x per week):
  → Review clip markers (30 min per session)
  → Select top 5-7 clips for the week
  → Edit each clip: trim, crop to vertical, add captions (5-8 min per clip)
  → Export for YouTube Shorts (no watermark)
  → Export for TikTok (can add trending sound)
  → Schedule posts across platforms

MONTHLY (2-3 hours):
  → Compile best clips into a YouTube long-form highlight reel
  → Add intro, transitions, and outro
  → Upload with searchable title ("Best Valorant Clips [Month] 2026")
```

### Why This Strategy Works for Nyx

The entire strategy is built around one insight: Nyx is already generating 15-20 hours of content per week but capturing zero value from it after the stream ends. The clips already exist inside those streams — the only work is extracting and distributing them.

Cross-posting the same clips to YouTube Shorts, TikTok, and Twitter means one editing session produces content for three platforms. The YouTube long-form compilations repackage that same content again. Nyx creates one piece of content (the stream) and distributes it five ways.

The 5-hour weekly budget is tight but doable because the production workflow is clipping, not creating from scratch. If Nyx can eventually hire a clip editor ($200-400/month for gaming editors on Fiverr or Discord), the time drops to 1-2 hours per week for scheduling and strategy while maintaining the same output volume.
