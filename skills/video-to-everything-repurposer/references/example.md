## 1. Short video draft

“I spent eight months building a project-management tool because I thought it was a good idea. After launch, it had zero users.

The lesson I took from that was to talk to potential customers before building. My suggested target is 50 conversations, but that number isn't a guarantee of demand.

The important question is what those conversations reveal about the problem and how people handle it today.

Before you commit to building, write down who you need to speak with and what you still need to learn.”

## Start with the problem

An idea can feel clear to its creator while the intended audience handles the problem differently. An editorial starting question is: “How do you handle this today?” The aim is to understand the person's situation before explaining a proposed solution.

## Keep the evidence separate from the interpretation

A conversation can produce useful observations and still leave important questions unanswered. Record what the person actually said, what you think it means and what you still need to test. These are suggestions for applying the lesson, not additional claims from the original excerpt.

## Choose the next learning step
