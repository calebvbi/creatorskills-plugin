## Example 1: Responding to a Vague Brand Brief

### Creator Input

```
I got an email from a skincare brand called GlowLab. They said:

"Hi! We love your content and want to work with you on some UGC for our
new Vitamin C serum launch. We're looking for authentic TikTok content that
feels native and drives conversions. We need it ASAP — can you deliver by
next Friday? Let us know your rate."

That's the whole brief. I've never worked with them before. I make skincare
content on TikTok (12K followers) and I've done 3 paid UGC deals at
$200–$350 per video. My rate for a single 30-sec video is normally $300,
with 2 revision rounds and 90-day usage rights.

I already own their Vitamin C serum — I've been using it for 2 months and
genuinely like it. My setup is iPhone 15, ring light, CapCut.

Help me turn this into a proper response brief that locks in scope and
protects me from the usual surprises.
```

## 3. Creative Direction

### Hook (First 0–3 Seconds)
- **Primary hook:** Hold the Vitamin C bottle close to camera. Text overlay: "I stopped buying $60 serums when I found this."
- **Backup hook:** "POV: your skincare routine takes 3 minutes and actually works."

### Key Messages (Must Include)
1. Product name: GlowLab Vitamin C serum
2. Personal result: "I've used this for 2 months — my dark spots have definitely faded"
3. Price/value angle: Effective serum without the luxury price tag
4. Usage: Part of a simple morning routine

### What NOT to Include
- No "before/after" medical claims ("cleared my acne in 3 days")
- No competitor comparisons
- No unverifiable clinical language

### Visual Style
- **Setting:** Clean bathroom counter, natural daylight or ring light
- **Product shots:** Bottle visible in opening shot and at least once mid-video
- **Creator appearance:** Casual, no-makeup or minimal makeup to show skin texture naturally
- **Color palette:** Bright, clean, minimal background clutter

### Audio
- **Voiceover:** Yes — natural, conversational tone
- **Music:** Trending TikTok audio or subtle ambient track (no copyrighted music without clearance)
- **Captions:** Required — large font, on-screen within first 2 seconds

## 4. Usage Rights & Licensing

- **Usage period:** 90 days from delivery date
- **Usage channels:** Paid social (TikTok, Instagram, Facebook), organic social, email marketing
- **Exclusivity:** None — creator free to work with other skincare brands
- **Raw footage:** Not included — delivered assets only
- **Whitelisting / spark ads:** Not included — available as add-on for $100

## 5. Approval Process

- **Concept due:** [2 business days from brief approval] — written concept + hook options
- **Feedback turnaround:** Brand commits to 48-hour feedback on all drafts
- **Revision rounds:** 2 rounds included in base rate
- **Additional revisions:** $75 per round beyond 2
- **Final delivery format:** Google Drive link with uncompressed files + device-ready compressed versions

## 6. Pricing & Payment

- **Total project fee:** $450
- **Payment terms:** 50% upfront ($225) upon brief approval, 50% ($225) upon final delivery
- **Rush fee:** Included — next Friday delivery is aggressive but feasible
- **Additional deliverables:** $150 per extra video; $50 per extra hook variation

## 7. Creator Notes & Open Questions

1. **Product confirmation:** Please confirm the exact product name and SKU for the launch. I'll also need to confirm the current price point for accuracy.
2. **Brand examples:** To nail the creative on round one, could you share 1–2 examples of UGC you've run that performed well?
3. **Usage intent:** You mentioned "drives conversions" — are you planning to run these as paid ads (whitelisting) or organic posts? This affects licensing.
4. **Approval contact:** Who will be the final approver on my drafts? Please confirm name and email.

## What These Examples Show

1. **The response brief turns ambiguity into protection.** GlowLab's email had no specs, no usage scope, no revision limit, and no approval process. The response brief fills all of that in with professional language the brand will accept.

2. **The self-directing brief leads with proof, not promises.** Instead of "I have a great idea," it opens with "my organic video about your product hit 250K views." That is what makes a brand say yes.

3. **Both briefs name specific numbers.** Deliverables, lengths, due dates, revision rounds, usage periods, and fees — all specific. No "灵活" or "we'll figure it out."

4. **The scope protects the creator.** Neither brief includes raw footage, whitelisting, or exclusivity as free. Those are add-ons with clear pricing.

5. **The next step is one reply away.** Both briefs end with a single action: reply to approve. No committee meetings, no "let's hop on a call to discuss" (unless the creator wants to offer that as an option).

6. **Red flags are handled before they appear.** The response brief asks for brand examples, confirms the approver, and defines usage intent. The self-directing brief doesn't wait for the brand to ask about whitelisting — it names the price upfront.
