# Newsletter Subscriber Growth Engine — adapted worked example

## Supplied brief

“I have 310 newsletter subscribers. I post creator workflow tips and want more relevant subscribers. I have not supplied acquisition sources, signup page visits, conversion rates, referral history, unsubscribes or reader feedback. I can spend two hours this week. Do not contact anyone.”

## Growth audit

**Observed:** 310 subscribers and a two-hour time budget. **Unknown:** which sources bring subscribers, how many people reach the signup page, why they join, whether they stay and what has already been tried.

The count alone cannot identify a content or distribution problem. Working hypotheses include insufficient qualified visits, an unclear signup promise or a mismatch between expectations and content. None is established yet. Export dated source and signup data if available; do not invent an existing referral or cross-promotion history.

## Five lead-magnet options

| Format | Specific idea | Audience-fit hypothesis | Proposed effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Checklist | A Five-Step Pre-Publish Review | Strong if readers struggle with final checks; validate with reader replies. | 1 hour, estimated |
| Template | A One-Page Creator Brief | Moderate until the audience's planning needs are known. | 1 hour, estimated |
| Worksheet | Map One Video into Three Drafts | Strong if repurposing is a stated reader problem. | 2 hours, estimated |
| Example | One Annotated Workflow Walkthrough | Moderate; choose a workflow readers actually request. | 2 hours, estimated |
| Prompt guide | How to Supply Better Source Material | Uncertain; validate whether input preparation causes friction. | 1 hour, estimated |

These are fit hypotheses and effort estimates, not conversion predictions. Start with the pre-publish checklist if it matches recent reader questions; otherwise ask readers which task is difficult before building it.

## Social-to-newsletter draft

“If final checks keep delaying your next post, I'm drafting a short pre-publish checklist. My newsletter covers practical creator workflows. Subscribe here if that is useful to you: [SIGNUP LINK].”

This is proposed copy. Verify that the signup page accurately describes what subscribers receive. Choose a currently supported profile, post or story link placement; do not assume a retired platform feature exists. The copy has not been posted.

## Cross-promotion draft

“Hi [name], I write a newsletter about creator workflows. Would an exchange of useful resources fit your readers? I can share a sample issue and audience information before we decide.”

No partner has been researched or contacted. Supply real, dated subscriber and engagement data before including numbers in a pitch. Do not imply an exchange is agreed.

## Welcome email draft

Subject: Your creator workflow checklist

“Thanks for joining. Here is the checklist you requested: [CHECKLIST LINK]. You'll receive [CONFIRMED CADENCE] about [CONFIRMED TOPICS]. What is one publishing task you want to make easier?”

Use the promised asset only when it exists. A reply question invites conversation; it does not guarantee inbox placement. Follow the email provider's current configuration and deliverability guidance.

## One-week measurement plan

Spend the first hour checking the signup promise and any existing source data. Use the second hour to draft the chosen resource and one promotion. Record the start date, qualified page visits, signups and source tags using tools already available and authorized. Compare only compatible, non-overlapping periods and explain small-sample uncertainty. Subscriber gains remain unknown; no numerical growth target is predicted from the subscriber count alone.
