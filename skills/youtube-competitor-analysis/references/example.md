# Worked Example: A Small, Explicit Competitor Dataset

All names and numbers here are synthetic teaching data. No real channel was researched, and this sample is not a current market report.

## Input fixture

A creator, Budget Workshop, reports 8,200 subscribers and 3,000 average views at 30 days. Their goal is to find a useful topic to test. They supply four fictional comparison videos, each measured at 30 days:

| Channel | Video/topic | Views | Likes | Upload date |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Sample A | First emergency fund | 8,000 | 240 | September 1 |
| Sample A | Budget setup walkthrough | 4,000 | 100 | September 8 |
| Sample B | Compare two savings methods | 6,000 | 180 | September 2 |
| Sample B | Monthly spending review | 3,000 | 90 | September 9 |

No thumbnails, comments, search data, impressions, CTR or retention are supplied. Subscriber counts and eligibility are not being audited.

## Analysis

Like/view ratios are 3%, 2.5%, 3% and 3%. These describe this fixture only. The emergency-fund video has the most views in this four-video sample, but topic, channel and distribution could explain the difference. It does not prove a winning format.

Title patterns in the sample are a beginner task, a walkthrough, a comparison and a review. A larger, comparable observed sample would be needed to estimate prevalence. Thumbnail patterns and comment sentiment remain unknown.

Potential content-gap hypothesis: a beginner emergency-fund decision guide might fit the creator's audience. Confirm whether the creator has covered it and whether actual audience questions support it. Absence from four rows does not establish no competitor coverage or high search demand.

## Test plan

Propose one video answering a documented audience question, using the creator's own verified examples. Record title, format, source evidence and publication date. Compare at the same age with relevant creator baselines, including source mix and viewing metrics. Do not promise extra subscribers, infer competitor burnout or invent personal reputations.

## Research checklist for a live use case

Record every source URL, access date, sample-selection rule and metric definition. Separate observations from estimates and hypotheses. If live access fails, supply the research table and ask for relevant records instead of pretending research occurred.
