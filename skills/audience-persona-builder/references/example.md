# Worked Examples — Audience Persona Builder in Action

## Example 1: Gaming Streamer — 28K Followers, Variety Streamer with Strategy Focus

### Interview Transcript (Condensed)

**Q: What's your content about?**
I'm a Twitch streamer and YouTube channel — mostly strategy and city-builder games. Factorio, Cities Skylines, Satisfactory, some Civilization. I do long let's-play series on Twitch and edited "tips and tricks" videos on YouTube. My angle is efficiency — I'm the "optimal build order" guy.

**Q: What platforms are you on, and which is primary?**
Twitch is primary with about 28K followers. YouTube is secondary with 14K subs. I stream 4 nights a week and upload 2 edited videos per week to YouTube.

**Q: Roughly how large is your audience?**
Twitch averages 180-250 concurrent viewers. YouTube videos get 8-15K views in the first week.

**Q: Who do you think your core viewer is?**
Mostly guys, probably 22-35. Software engineers, IT workers, data people. The type who finds spreadsheets fun. Analytics say 78% male, biggest age group is 25-34. Mostly US, Canada, UK, Germany, and the Nordics.

**Q: Is there a secondary audience?**
I've noticed a smaller group of college students who are into the games but more casually. They watch the YouTube videos but don't catch streams because of the schedule. And there's a tiny group of actual urban planners and logistics people who watch Cities Skylines stuff — they leave the most detailed comments.

**Q: What content gets the most engagement?**
My "optimization guide" videos on YouTube do the best. Things like "Factorio: Perfect Smelting Array Setup" or "Satisfactory: Most Efficient Power Grid." Comments are always people sharing their own builds, debating ratios, asking follow-up questions about edge cases. On Twitch, the most active chat moments are when I'm solving a bottleneck live and chat is trying to figure out the solution before I do.

**Q: What underperforms?**
Tier lists and "what I'm playing this month" style vlogs. My audience doesn't care about my opinions on other games — they want the deep tactical content. Also tried a few "first impressions" videos on new releases and they got half the views of my guide content.

**Q: Why do people watch your content?**
They want to get better at these games without spending 100 hours figuring out the meta themselves. My viewers want efficiency in real life too — they'd rather watch a 15-minute guide than spend 3 hours experimenting. A lot of them have limited gaming time (full-time jobs, some have kids) and want to maximize the fun-per-hour.

**Q: What does your audience want to achieve?**
They want to master the games they play. Not just beat them — optimize them. The goal isn't "finish Factorio," it's "launch the rocket in under 8 hours with a clean bus layout." They're completionists and optimizers by nature.

**Q: What frustrates your audience?**
Bad game wikis and outdated guides. They complain in comments about finding info from 3 patches ago that doesn't work anymore. Also frustrated by "beginner" content when they're intermediate-to-advanced — they want the nerd-level breakdown, not another "here's how to place a belt."

**Q: When and how does your audience consume your content?**
Twitch streams are weeknight evenings, 7-11 PM. YouTube is split — some watch during lunch breaks at work, others save videos for their own gaming sessions and follow along. I get a lot of "watching this on my second monitor while I play" comments.

**Q: What other creators does your audience follow?**
Nilaus (Factorio), TotalXclipse (Satisfactory), City Planner Plays (Cities Skylines), and broader strategy people like Potato McWhiskey and quill18. Some overlap with tech YouTubers like LTT and MKBHD.

**Q: Does your audience spend money in your niche?**
They buy every DLC on day one. They have gaming PCs, multiple monitors, mechanical keyboards. Some spend on Patreon for creators they follow. They don't seem to buy merch or physical goods much, but they'll pay for utility — like save files, mod packs, or blueprint books. A few people have literally asked if I'd sell my Factorio blueprints.

**Q: How does your audience talk about you?**
"This guy's builds are insane." "Found this channel and lost 3 hours." "The engineering spreadsheet energy is strong." One comment that stuck with me: "You're like a university lecture on logistics but actually entertaining."

**Q: What do people say when they share your content?**
Usually something like "if you play Factorio you need to see this" or "this is the most efficient setup I've found." Utility framing — they share it because it's useful, not because it's funny or dramatic.

**Q: Is there anything your audience asks for that you haven't made yet?**
Blueprint downloads, written guides to go alongside videos, and a Discord where people can share builds. Also a lot of requests for Dyson Sphere Program content, which I haven't covered yet.

## Example 2: Personal Finance Creator — 42K Subscribers, Millennial Professional Audience

### Interview Transcript (Condensed)

**Q: What's your content about?**
Personal finance for people who make decent money but feel like they're behind. Not "how to save $5 on groceries" — more like "you make $75K and still feel broke, here's why and how to fix it." YouTube channel, 42K subscribers. I do a mix of evergreen advice videos and market commentary when something relevant happens.

**Q: Platforms and size?**
YouTube is primary, 42K subs. I also have an Instagram with 18K followers where I post carousel breakdowns. YouTube videos get 15-35K views typically. Carousels get 3-8K reach.

**Q: Who is your core viewer?**
Women 27-35 are my biggest group — about 58% of my audience is female according to analytics. Mostly in the US, some UK and Canada. They have professional jobs — marketing, HR, project management, nursing, teaching. They make $55-85K which is solid but they live in expensive cities and feel like their money disappears. A lot of them are the first in their family to make this kind of money and didn't grow up learning about investing.

**Q: Secondary audience?**
There's a male audience, maybe 25-33, similar income bracket. They tend to be more interested in the investing content than the budgeting content. Also getting some older viewers (40+) in the comments who say they wish this existed when they were younger — they share it with their kids.

**Q: What content gets the most engagement?**
"I make $X and here's how I spend it" breakdowns. Anything where I show real numbers — my actual budget, my actual investment portfolio, my actual savings rate. The comments section goes wild because people start sharing their own numbers. My video "I Make $78K and Here's Every Dollar" has 180K views and 1,400 comments. Also "mistakes I made" content does well — "3 Money Mistakes I Made in My 20s" had people writing paragraphs in the comments about their own mistakes.

**Q: What underperforms?**
Market commentary and macro finance. When I made a video about inflation or interest rate changes, views were half my average. My audience doesn't want to understand the economy — they want to fix their bank account. Also tried a series reviewing financial products (credit cards, savings accounts) and it felt too sponsored even though it wasn't. Audience didn't trust it.

**Q: Why do people watch your content?**
They feel behind and ashamed about it. They scroll past finance content that makes them feel worse — the "I saved $100K by 25" stuff. I don't do that. I talk about catching up, not showing off. A lot of my viewers are dealing with specific situations: student loan debt, first apartment expenses, figuring out a 401k for the first time, wanting to invest but being terrified of losing money.

**Q: What frustrates your audience?**
They feel talked down to by traditional finance content. Either it's too basic ("save 20% of your income" — they know that, they just can't figure out how) or it's aspirational in a way that feels disconnecting ("passive income" and "financial freedom" language). They also hate feeling judged for their spending — they know they spend too much on food delivery and don't need a lecture about lattes.

**Q: When and how do they consume your content?**
Sunday evenings and Monday mornings are my best posting times. I think they're doing "financial planning" for the week. Some watch on lunch breaks. Instagram carousels get saved a lot — I think they screenshot them for reference. YouTube videos are focused watching, not background.

**Q: What other creators do they follow?**
The Financial Diet, Broke Millennial (the book, not a creator), Ramit Sethi, some Caleb Hammer for the entertainment value. On Instagram, a lot of "money diary" type accounts. They also follow lifestyle and career creators — not just finance.

**Q: Does your audience spend money?**
They buy courses and books about money. Several people have mentioned buying Ramit's course or similar. They'll pay for tools that simplify something — budgeting apps, tax prep software. They're not impulse buyers for finance content, though. They research and think about it. A few have asked if I have a course or Patreon.

**Q: How does your audience talk about you?**
"She makes me feel like I'm not failing." "First finance channel that doesn't make me feel broke." "You explain things like a friend, not a lecturer." A recurring theme in DMs is people saying they finally opened an investment account or finally made a budget after watching my content. The word "finally" comes up a lot.

**Q: What would they share and why?**
They share my content by tagging friends or sending it in group chats. The framing is usually "this is us" or "we need to do this." It's relational — they share because the content reflects their own situation and they want their friends to experience the same relief of "oh, I'm not the only one."

**Q: Anything they ask for that you haven't made?**
A budgeting template or spreadsheet they can actually use. People ask for this constantly. Also want more "real number" breakdowns for different salary levels and cities. And several people have asked for a community or group where they can talk about money without judgment.
