# Trend Research and Content Planning

Identify timely content opportunities using observed sources and the creator's audience. Deliver an evidence table, prioritized candidates and practical content angles. Do not imply live research from memory or fabricate a virality score.

## Intake

Collect niche, audience, platform, available production time, normal format and relevant competitors. Ask whether current source access or supplied observations are available. A URL must be opened and relevant content inspected before it supports a claim.

## Gather signals

For each candidate record source URL, observed claim, access date, publication/event date, locale and time window. Distinguish official announcements, normalized search-interest data, social examples and audience questions. Public view counts are snapshots, not growth rates without earlier observations. Autocomplete is not a measured volume estimate.

When sources are unavailable, produce candidate questions and a research checklist, labelled unverified. Do not claim a topic is trending now or cite invented Google Trends percentages, clips, comments or competitor gaps.

## Assess candidates

Evaluate audience relevance, observed momentum, source reliability, existing coverage and feasible differentiation. Check whether the sample actually supports the proposed gap. Different platforms and geography may represent different audiences. Agreement among signals strengthens a hypothesis but does not prove demand or a future result.

Use qualitative priority and an explanation. If numeric scoring is requested, define a subjective rubric and show the evidence for each factor. Leave missing factors unknown; do not calculate a confident score from invented values. A priority score is not a probability of virality.

## Develop angles

For each selected candidate propose a working title, viewer question, source-backed outline, required original material, freshness check and production effort. Product tests and personal stories require actual creator evidence. Suggest one or two feasible pieces rather than a full speculative slate.

Timing is a planning judgment tied to an observed event or deadline. Do not promise a 48-hour opportunity or a fixed shelf life. Distinguish evergreen questions, scheduled events and uncertain emerging topics.

## Review and follow-up

Explain what could invalidate the recommendation. Track published content only after the creator confirms it exists, and compare results with appropriate baselines and exposure windows. Separate observations from causality. Do not schedule routines, publish content or monitor indefinitely without explicit authorization.

Return research coverage, source table, ranked candidates, content drafts and the next evidence-gathering action. Keep stale evidence visibly dated.
