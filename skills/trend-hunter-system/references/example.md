# Worked Example: Trend Candidates With Source Requirements

## Creator input

A tech creator has 42,000 subscribers, typical views of 12,000–35,000 and two weekly uploads. They cover consumer technology and ask what is trending now. No live trend observations are supplied or available in this example.

## Response

Current trend status is unverified. I can prepare research candidates and a collection plan, but cannot report Google Trends growth, social view counts or a time-sensitive opportunity as observed.

| Candidate to investigate | Relevance hypothesis | Evidence to collect |
|---|---|---|
| Budget-device accessibility features | Fits consumer-tech audience | Current official feature release and real audience questions |
| Repairability of devices the creator owns | Practical buyer question | Verified product documentation and available test material |
| Privacy settings after a software update | Useful if a relevant update occurred | Official dated change notes and actual settings |

These are topic hypotheses, not claims that an update or trend exists today. Avoid announcing product capabilities until verified.

## After evidence is available

For each observation record source URL, observation date, geography, query, time window, normalized versus absolute measure and sample limits. Search interest is not search volume or expected views. A percentage change from a small base can be misleading.

Use a qualitative priority such as investigate now, monitor or insufficient evidence. If the creator requests a numeric prioritization score, define its weights, mark it subjective and leave unobserved components unscored. Never manufacture a composite virality score from missing data.

## Production decision

Choose a candidate only after checking audience fit, factual source material and production capacity. Draft a title and outline grounded in that evidence. State what might change the recommendation. No guaranteed window, viral result or current competitor gap is claimed.
