# Worked Example: Five Posts From Supplied Material

## Synthetic creator input

Priya is a freelance B2B SaaS copywriter with four years of independent work and 8,000 LinkedIn followers. Her supplied notes say a client's homepage conversion metric rose 38% during two weeks after a rewrite, a client ghosted after a proposal, and she wants to discuss clearer messaging. No raw conversion counts, experiment design, client quote, additional closed deals or external research is supplied.

## Weekly plan

| Day | Format | Grounded subject |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Short post | What the homepage result does and does not show |
| Tuesday | Carousel draft | A practical homepage-message review |
| Wednesday | Question | How readers check whether copy is clear |
| Thursday | Reflection | A question to test before sending a proposal |
| Friday | Checklist | Evidence to collect for a useful copy case study |

## Monday draft

A client reported a 38% rise in their homepage conversion metric during the two weeks after my rewrite. I want to separate that observation from the explanation.

Before calling it a result of the copy alone, I'd check traffic mix, conversion definitions, sample size and other changes. The useful next step is to review the actual counts and test the message with comparable visitors.

What do you check before turning a before/after number into a case study?

## Tuesday carousel outline

1. Name the intended reader and decision.
2. Check whether the headline matches the actual product.
3. Replace vague benefits with supported specifics.
4. Show relevant evidence without inventing customer outcomes.
5. Make the next action clear.
6. Check the draft with real readers and measure a defined outcome.

Examples of product copy remain placeholders until actual product and customer evidence is supplied. No $400K ARR loss or four-to-seven-month delay is asserted.

## Wednesday question

When reviewing a homepage, what helps you spot unclear language first: reader questions, sales-call notes, usability feedback or analytics? Share the observation that changed your mind.

No 30-post experiment or 2.3x performance claim is invented to encourage comments.

## Thursday reflection

A prospective client went quiet after my proposal. I don't know why. Before the next proposal, I want to test one question: "What will you need to decide, and who else is involved?" That may make the process clearer, but it is not evidence of a higher close rate yet.

## Friday checklist

For the next case study I want the reporting window, numerator and denominator, change date, concurrent changes and permission to share the result. A clear uncertainty statement is more useful than filling missing evidence with a plausible story.

## Review

Confirm confidentiality and wording with the creator before publishing. The 38% figure remains creator-reported and needs a definition; no baseline rates are derived from it. This output is a set of drafts, not scheduled posts.
