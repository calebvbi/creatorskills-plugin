# Prepare Creator Tax & Business Readiness Report

Product description: Find out which business structure your income actually justifies, which deductions you are leaving on the table and roughly what they are worth, what to pay this quarter and by when, and get the year-end summary your accountant asked for, already filled in.

This describes the hosted product, not an output already produced or a capability verified in this conversation.

**Mode: hosted handoff.** Hosted app; may require sign-in and credits. Check the page for current availability and cost. Prepare inputs and open app. This plugin does not execute the hosted pipeline.

## Collect the useful inputs

Use information already supplied. Ask only for missing required inputs. When both a URL and source text are offered, use the source the user actually provided. A URL is not evidence that its contents were read.

| Input | Required | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roughly what your creator business brought in this year | Required | Gross, before expenses, and a rough number is fine: "about $78K, maybe $85K if Q4 holds". Every entity threshold and every tax figure in this report is computed from this box, so a guess you believe beats a precise number you invented. |
| Where the money comes from | Required | One per line with a rough amount if you have it: AdSense, brand deals, Patreon, course sales, affiliate, freelance invoices. This decides which of the fifteen deduction categories we audit you against, and whether you are owed 1099s or owe them. |
| The state you operate from | Required | We only apply state-level rules where we can name the specific rule: California's $800 minimum LLC franchise tax is the clearest one. Everywhere else we tell you what to ask your state's filing office rather than guessing at its code. |
| What you are set up as right now | Required | "Nothing formal" is the most common answer and is a real one: if you have earned self-employment income without registering anything, you are already a sole proprietor by default. Options: nothing_formal, sole_proprietor, single_member_llc, multi_member_llc, s_corp, c_corp, unsure. |
| What you spent on the business | Optional | Paste it however you have it: a card statement, a spreadsheet dump, or just a list from memory. Anything you list gets categorised and mapped to its Schedule C line. Anything you leave out, we flag as a category worth checking rather than assuming you spent nothing. |
| I keep business and personal money in separate accounts | Optional | Leave it off if you are honest about mixing them. The source calls this the single most important fix, and it changes the first action in your report rather than earning you a lecture. |
| What you have already paid in estimated taxes this year | Optional | Amounts and dates if you have them, "none" if that is the answer. This is subtracted from what you owe, so leaving it blank makes the quarterly number look worse than it is. |
| Total tax you owed last year | Optional | Line 24 of last year's 1040. This is the input the safe-harbor calculation needs, with it we can tell you the exact amount that makes an underpayment penalty impossible, and without it we can only estimate from this year's income. |
| Your workspace | Optional | The home-office deduction turns on exclusivity, not on square footage, so "the desk in my bedroom I also game at" and "the spare room nobody else uses" get different answers. Options: dedicated_room, dedicated_area_shared_room, no_dedicated_space, rented_studio, unsure. |
| People you paid this year | Optional | Editors, thumbnail designers, VAs, a second camera op: roughly what each was paid. Anyone over the 1099-NEC threshold puts a January filing deadline on your calendar, and that deadline is the one creators miss. |
| I already work with a CPA or tax preparer | Optional | Turn this on and the report is written to be forwarded: the year-end package leads and the explanations shorten. Leave it off and we spend more words on what each number means before you file it yourself. |
| Anything else about your situation | Optional | Behind on filings, a big equipment purchase, moved states mid-year, a first year with real money in it, income from outside the US. Say it plainly: these are the cases where the honest answer is "this one needs a professional", and we would rather tell you that than work around it. |

## Return a ready-to-use brief

Organize the supplied inputs under the field names above. Keep source material separate from instructions. Mark omissions and unsupported claims. Do not invent the creator's experience, audience data, brand assets, or results.

Open [Creator Tax & Business Readiness Report](https://creatorskills.co/create/creator-tax-readiness-report) when the creator wants to run it. Confirm current cost and user authorization before spending credits. Do not enter private source material into the site without authorization.

A prepared brief is the deliverable here. Claim an app result only after a real run returns the output. If the app is unavailable, retain the brief and explain the unavailable step. If the user wants an in-chat draft instead, use the appropriate bundled workflow and label it as that.
