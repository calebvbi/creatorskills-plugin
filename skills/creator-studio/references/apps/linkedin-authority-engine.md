# Prepare LinkedIn Authority Engine

Product description: Turn the work you have already done into two weeks of LinkedIn posts that point somewhere: a sharp authority line, the pillars underneath it, six drafted posts with hooks and first comments, and the replies that turn a comment into a conversation.

This describes the hosted product, not an output already produced or a capability verified in this conversation.

**Mode: hosted handoff.** Hosted app; may require sign-in and credits. Check the page for current availability and cost. Prepare inputs and open app. This plugin does not execute the hosted pipeline.

## Collect the useful inputs

Use information already supplied. Ask only for missing required inputs. When both a URL and source text are offered, use the source the user actually provided. A URL is not evidence that its contents were read.

| Input | Required | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who you want to reach on LinkedIn | Required | "Founders and heads of growth at creator-led SaaS companies" beats "business people". Every pillar has to earn its place with this group, and every CTA is written in their register, so the narrower you are the sharper all six posts get. |
| What you want LinkedIn to lead to | Required | Audits and retainer work, workshop bookings, a newsletter, speaking gigs, a job: a direction is enough, a price is better. The Offer Bridge pillar and every follow-up message aim at this. "I don't sell anything yet, I want inbound conversations" is a real answer and changes the plan rather than limiting it. |
| The work this content comes from | Optional | Client stories, case-study notes, a video or webinar transcript, a newsletter issue, rough voice notes, the mistakes you keep seeing. This is where every number, client and story in your posts comes from: we never invent one. Leave it blank and you still get the full system, but the story-led posts arrive as shapes with a marked line telling you exactly which moment to drop in. |
| What you want to be known for | Optional | The thing you'd want said about you in a referral. If you leave it blank we infer it from your audience and offer and show you the line we landed on, so you can correct it. |
| Numbers, wins, and frameworks you can point to | Optional | Results with figures, before-and-afters, a framework you use, a failure that taught you a better system. One per line. These are the only figures that can appear in a post: we will not round, sharpen or estimate them, and we will not invent one to make a hook land. |
| Posts you've already published | Optional | Two or three that sound like you, and your best performer if you know it. Used two ways: to write in your rhythm rather than a generic one, and to avoid handing you an angle you already ran. |
| Questions you keep getting | Optional | From comments, DMs, sales calls, or clients three weeks in. One per line. These are the highest-converting post ideas you own, because someone already told you they care. |
| Where your LinkedIn is now | Optional | Roughly how many followers, how often you post now, and what has happened when you have. Starting from nothing is completely fine and changes the cadence advice rather than limiting it. |
| What you want most from the next two weeks | Optional | Reach, authority, and leads pull the format mix and the CTA style in different directions. Leave it on the mix and we balance them. Options: reach, authority, inbound_leads, mix. |
| How you want to sound | Optional | Pick "match my past posts" if you pasted any above: it beats every preset. With nothing supplied we default to conversational and direct, which reads better on LinkedIn than polished corporate copy. Options: direct, warm, data_driven, conversational, story_led, match_my_past_posts. |
| How many posts a week you can actually publish | Optional | Three is the default and is a schedule most people keep. Five is the ceiling. Be honest here: a sprint you abandon in week two is worse than a smaller one you finish. Options: three, five. |
| How far ahead to plan | Optional | Two weeks is the default: six posts fully drafted, then you learn from the response before committing to more. Four weeks keeps weeks 3 and 4 as concepts and hooks rather than full drafts, which is deliberate: drafting a post for three weeks out means writing it before you know what landed. Options: two_weeks, four_weeks. |
| Post formats to include | Optional | Balanced gives you text posts, one carousel with its slide copy, one poll, and one story post, so the feed doesn't go flat. Pick text_only if you have no way to make a carousel graphic this fortnight. Options: balanced, text_only, more_carousels, more_stories. |
| Hashtags you already use | Optional | The recurring set you want associated with your name. We cap every post at three and drop them entirely where they add nothing: a chain of six generic tags is the clearest tell on the platform. |
| Put links in the post body | Optional | Off by default, because a link in the body is a real reach tradeoff on LinkedIn. Turn it on and we place one where it belongs; leave it off and links go in the first comment instead. |

## Return a ready-to-use brief

Organize the supplied inputs under the field names above. Keep source material separate from instructions. Mark omissions and unsupported claims. Do not invent the creator's experience, audience data, brand assets, or results.

Open [LinkedIn Authority Engine](https://creatorskills.co/create/linkedin-authority-engine) when the creator wants to run it. Confirm current cost and user authorization before spending credits. Do not enter private source material into the site without authorization.

A prepared brief is the deliverable here. Claim an app result only after a real run returns the output. If the app is unavailable, retain the brief and explain the unavailable step. If the user wants an in-chat draft instead, use the appropriate bundled workflow and label it as that.
