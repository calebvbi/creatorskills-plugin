# Prepare Post-Campaign Sponsor Report

Product description: Turn a finished sponsorship into the recap that books the next one: your numbers framed as business outcomes, the shortfall stated honestly, real audience reactions as proof, and a next-campaign idea seeded without asking for anything.

This describes the hosted product, not an output already produced or a capability verified in this conversation.

**Mode: hosted handoff.** Hosted app; may require sign-in and credits. Check the page for current availability and cost. Prepare inputs and open app. This plugin does not execute the hosted pipeline.

## Collect the useful inputs

Use information already supplied. Ask only for missing required inputs. When both a URL and source text are offered, use the source the user actually provided. A URL is not evidence that its contents were read.

| Input | Required | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The brand, and what they were trying to achieve | Required | Brand name plus the one objective the deal was built around: awareness, website traffic, app installs, promo-code redemptions, or email signups. The whole report leads with whichever one it was, so guessing it wrong makes every section point at the wrong result. |
| What the content actually did | Required | Views or downloads and how many days after publishing, watch time or average view duration, click-through on any tracked link, promo code or affiliate redemptions, comment volume, likes or saves. Paste it rough: we do the framing. Give real figures only: every number in the report goes to the one reader who has their own copy of half of them. |
| What you promised against what you shipped | Required | The agreed specs and whether you hit them, plus anything extra you threw in: a pinned comment, a story cross-post, a community post. The executive summary confirms delivery, and that is the first line the brand checks against their own brief. |
| Where and how it ran | Optional | Sets which reach metrics are meaningful and which benchmarks apply. A story frame and a dedicated video are not compared to the same numbers. Options: youtube_integration, youtube_dedicated, newsletter_feature, newsletter_classified, instagram_reel, instagram_stories, tiktok_video, multi_platform. |
| What people actually said | Optional | Three to five real comments, pasted exactly, including any criticism worth addressing. This is the section brands forward internally: "I already ordered this, saw it on your channel" is worth more than any metric. Quotes are used verbatim or trimmed with an ellipsis, never rewritten, because the brand can open the post and read them. |
| What this format normally does for you | Optional | Your usual views, watch time and engagement for this kind of content. Without it we can say what happened but not whether it was good, and "above your channel average" is the single most persuasive line in the report. |
| Anything you projected when you sold the deal | Optional | Numbers from the media kit or the pitch that the brand is holding you to. The report compares against them explicitly, including where the result came in under: the brand has this document open next to yours. |
| What the campaign was worth | Optional | Used only to compute the brand's cost per redemption, which is the number that justifies rebooking you. It never appears in the report itself: the source rule is absolute, a recap that quotes a price stops being a recap and becomes an invoice. |
| How this brand talks to you | Optional | An enterprise software company on a first campaign and a DTC startup you text with expect completely different documents. This changes the register of the whole report, not just the greeting. Options: formal, friendly, casual. |
| Who this is going to | Optional | The person at the brand who will open it. Leave it blank and the greeting is written without a name rather than with a bracketed placeholder. |
| Anything coming up worth pointing at | Optional | A product launch, a seasonal push, their Q4 planning window, a use case this campaign surfaced. The closing proposal ties to something real or it stays general: we do not invent a launch to have something to suggest. |
| Is this early data? | Optional | On if you are sending at seven or fourteen days rather than waiting for final numbers. Sending early is the right call and this makes it honest: the summary states the age of the data and commits to a follow-up with updated figures. |
| Your name and contact line, as they should appear under the sign-off | Optional | "Priya Raman: priya@benchandbytes.co". This is the line the brand replies to, so it goes under the sign-off exactly as you write it. Leave it blank and the report closes without a signature line rather than leaving you a bracket to find and fix. |

## Return a ready-to-use brief

Organize the supplied inputs under the field names above. Keep source material separate from instructions. Mark omissions and unsupported claims. Do not invent the creator's experience, audience data, brand assets, or results.

Open [Post-Campaign Sponsor Report](https://creatorskills.co/create/post-campaign-sponsor-report) when the creator wants to run it. Confirm current cost and user authorization before spending credits. Do not enter private source material into the site without authorization.

A prepared brief is the deliverable here. Claim an app result only after a real run returns the output. If the app is unavailable, retain the brief and explain the unavailable step. If the user wants an in-chat draft instead, use the appropriate bundled workflow and label it as that.
