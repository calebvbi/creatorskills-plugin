# Prepare Community Comment Reply Engine

Product description: Clear a full comment section in the time it used to take to answer five: every reply written in your voice, sorted so the questions get answered first, and the bad-faith ones left alone on purpose.

This describes the hosted product, not an output already produced or a capability verified in this conversation.

**Mode: hosted handoff.** Hosted app; may require sign-in and credits. Check the page for current availability and cost. Prepare inputs and open app. This plugin does not execute the hosted pipeline.

## Collect the useful inputs

Use information already supplied. Ask only for missing required inputs. When both a URL and source text are offered, use the source the user actually provided. A URL is not evidence that its contents were read.

| Input | Required | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paste the comments | Required | One per line, or straight out of YouTube Studio with the usernames attached: either works. Include the spam and the nasty ones; sorting those out and leaving them alone is part of the job, and hiding them from us just means they come back unlabelled. Up to forty at a time. |
| What the video was about | Optional | Title and two or three sentences on what you actually said or showed. This is what turns "great question" into an answer: a reply that references the thing they watched reads as though you watched it too, because you did. |
| Answers to the questions you get asked constantly | Optional | Your gear, your software, your settings, your prices, where you got the thing: one per line as "question: answer". This is the ONLY source we will answer a factual question from. Leave it out and "what mic is that?" gets a warm acknowledgement and a flag for you, rather than a confident guess at a microphone you do not own. |
| A few replies you have written yourself | Optional | Three to five, pasted exactly as you sent them: typos, lowercase, emoji and all. This is worth more than any description of your tone, because how you reply to a comment is not how you write a script. We read the register off these and reuse it. |
| How you come across in replies | Optional | Only used when you have not pasted samples above. Casual is the default because that is what a comment section is; a reply in full sentences with proper capitalisation reads as a brand account, not a person. Options: casual_lowercase, warm_professional, dry_deadpan, playful_banter, educational_generous, match_my_samples. |
| Emoji | Optional | Sparing is the default: roughly one where it genuinely lands. Getting this wrong is one of the two things that makes a batch of replies read as written by somebody else, the other being length. Options: none, sparing, freely. |
| How long your replies usually run | Optional | match_the_comment is the default and is the source's own rule: a three-word compliment gets a short reply, a four-paragraph story gets a real one. A long reply under a short comment reads as strange, not as generous. Options: very_short, short, medium, match_the_comment. |
| What you want left alone | Optional | The default skips both spam and bad-faith comments and tells you why each one was skipped. draft_everything_and_mark_it still refuses to draft an argument: it gives you a single de-escalating line and the note that not replying is usually the better move. Options: skip_spam_and_bad_faith, skip_spam_only, draft_everything_and_mark_it. |
| Anything you will not discuss in comments | Optional | Politics, a competitor, an ongoing dispute, your income, a family matter. One per line. Comments touching these go to the skip list with the reason, whatever else they say. |
| One thing you would like people to do | Optional | A poll running on your Community tab, a video you would like more eyes on, a question you want answers to. It gets woven into a couple of replies where it genuinely fits and nowhere else: a call to action under every reply is an advert, not a conversation. |

## Return a ready-to-use brief

Organize the supplied inputs under the field names above. Keep source material separate from instructions. Mark omissions and unsupported claims. Do not invent the creator's experience, audience data, brand assets, or results.

Open [Community Comment Reply Engine](https://creatorskills.co/create/community-comment-engine) when the creator wants to run it. Confirm current cost and user authorization before spending credits. Do not enter private source material into the site without authorization.

A prepared brief is the deliverable here. Claim an app result only after a real run returns the output. If the app is unavailable, retain the brief and explain the unavailable step. If the user wants an in-chat draft instead, use the appropriate bundled workflow and label it as that.
