# Prepare Membership Tier Refresh

Product description: Find out why your patrons are actually leaving and fix the structure that is losing them: a diagnostic on your real numbers, a rebuilt set of tiers with the arithmetic shown, the delivery rhythm that makes people stay, and the relaunch that changes it all without a walkout.

This describes the hosted product, not an output already produced or a capability verified in this conversation.

**Mode: hosted handoff.** Hosted app; may require sign-in and credits. Check the page for current availability and cost. Prepare inputs and open app. This plugin does not execute the hosted pipeline.

## Collect the useful inputs

Use information already supplied. Ask only for missing required inputs. When both a URL and source text are offered, use the source the user actually provided. A URL is not evidence that its contents were read.

| Input | Required | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your tiers as they stand | Required | One per line: name, price, and exactly what a member at that level gets. Copy it off your own page rather than summarising it; the value-overlap check reads the benefit lists word for word, and "behind-the-scenes photos" appearing in two tiers is the kind of thing a summary smooths over and a diagnostic needs to see. |
| How many members are in each tier, and what that earns | Required | "$3 tier: 45 people, $135/mo" per line, or just the counts if you would rather we do the multiplication. Every figure in the audit comes from this and nowhere else: we will not estimate a patron count, because the projection is computed from it and a made-up starting number makes every number after it wrong. |
| Where the membership runs | Optional | Patreon is the default. This is a real constraint, not a label: YouTube caps you at five tiers and rewards members-only videos, Substack's paid tier needs a different format rather than more posts, Ko-fi audiences expect something simpler. We will not recommend a structure your platform cannot run. Options: patreon, ko_fi, youtube_memberships, substack, memberful, other. |
| What you know about people leaving | Optional | How many leave a month, which tiers they leave from, anything they told you on the way out, and whether it spiked after something. Rough is fine. Leave it blank and the audit reasons from your tier distribution instead and says which parts of the diagnosis are inference rather than data, which is the honest version, not a worse one. |
| Which way the numbers have moved | Optional | Over the last six months or so. Flat and slowly_declining need different fixes: flat is usually a conversion or a top-tier problem, declining is usually a delivery problem: so this changes the diagnosis more than it looks like it should. Options: growing, flat, slowly_declining, falling_fast, brand_new. |
| How big your audience is outside the membership | Optional | Subscribers, downloads a month, list size, followers. Used for the conversion-rate check against the 1-5% band that most creators land in. Being under it is a marketing problem and being over it is a pricing one, and they have opposite fixes: so without this the audit cannot tell you which one you have. |
| What members actually get, and how often | Optional | Be honest about the frequency, including the months you missed. The most common cause of steady drip churn is not the tiers at all. It is that nobody can see what they are paying for, and an audit built on your intended schedule instead of your real one diagnoses the wrong thing. |
| Hours a week you can give member-only work | Optional | Hard cap on what the new top tier is allowed to promise. Every benefit in the restructure gets a time cost attached and the total has to fit inside this: a premium tier you cannot deliver churns worse than the one you have. Options: under_two, two_to_five, five_to_ten, ten_plus. |
| What you actually want to change | Optional | These pull in different directions: a lower entry price wins members and lowers your average, a stronger top tier does the reverse. Picking one gets you a sharper restructure; all_three gets you a sequenced plan that says which to fix first and why. Options: more_members, higher_average_revenue, less_churn, all_three. |
| Whether you offer annual | Optional | Worth asking because the source's strongest single retention lever is here: annual members churn far less, and because recommending it on a platform that does not offer it wastes the recommendation. Options: yes_already, no, platform_does_not_support. |
| Anything you are not willing to change | Optional | A price you promised would never rise, a benefit people joined for, a tier named after an in-joke. One per line. The restructure works around these rather than quietly dropping them, and where one is genuinely the problem we say so and leave the decision with you. |
| When you would announce the change | Optional | A date or a rough window. Used for the grandfathering period and the "new pricing goes live on" line. Leave it blank and the plan runs in relative weeks: we will not put a deadline in your announcement that you did not set. |

## Return a ready-to-use brief

Organize the supplied inputs under the field names above. Keep source material separate from instructions. Mark omissions and unsupported claims. Do not invent the creator's experience, audience data, brand assets, or results.

Open [Membership Tier Refresh](https://creatorskills.co/create/membership-tier-refresh) when the creator wants to run it. Confirm current cost and user authorization before spending credits. Do not enter private source material into the site without authorization.

A prepared brief is the deliverable here. Claim an app result only after a real run returns the output. If the app is unavailable, retain the brief and explain the unavailable step. If the user wants an in-chat draft instead, use the appropriate bundled workflow and label it as that.
