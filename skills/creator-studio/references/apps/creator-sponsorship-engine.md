# Prepare Creator Sponsorship Engine

Product description: Everything between having an audience and having a brand deal: what to charge with the arithmetic shown and a floor you can hold, which brands to approach and how to check they buy, the pitch that opens on something real, and the placement copy that runs when they say yes.

This describes the hosted product, not an output already produced or a capability verified in this conversation.

**Mode: hosted handoff.** Hosted app; may require sign-in and credits. Check the page for current availability and cost. Prepare inputs and open app. This plugin does not execute the hosted pipeline.

## Collect the useful inputs

Use information already supplied. Ask only for missing required inputs. When both a URL and source text are offered, use the source the user actually provided. A URL is not evidence that its contents were read.

| Input | Required | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where you publish and what you actually cover | Required | Be specific: "tech" is not a niche. "Budget Android phone reviews" and "daily vlogs" sit at opposite ends of the multiplier table, and a rate built on the wrong one is wrong by a factor of two. If your show is a podcast, use the Podcast Sponsorship Engine instead: it prices downloads rather than views. |
| Followers, and what a piece of content actually does | Required | Subscriber or follower count AND average views per post: these are different numbers and only the second one prices anything. A 100K channel averaging 20K views prices on 20K. Add your engagement rate if you know it, or recent like and comment counts and we will calculate it. Round numbers are fine; guesses are not, because every rate here is computed from this. |
| What you would actually sell | Optional | All by default, priced as separate line items plus a package. Each format has its own CPM band because a story frame and a dedicated video are not the same product. Options: all, youtube_integration, youtube_dedicated, instagram_reel_or_post, instagram_stories, tiktok_video, newsletter_placement, multi_platform_package. |
| Newsletter list and open rate, if you have one | Optional | Subscriber count and average open rate. Under twenty thousand subscribers a list is priced flat per placement rather than on CPM, and the open rate is the number that moves it: a 1,500-person list at 55% open outprices a 6,000-person list at 12%. Leave blank if you do not have one and no newsletter line gets priced. |
| Anything that shows people actually pay attention | Optional | Average likes and comments, saves, watch time, DMs, a community, past click-through. This is how a smaller channel outbids a bigger one, and it is the only input that can move the rate upward by 40%. Only what you can point at: we never estimate an engagement number. |
| Who your audience is | Optional | Top age brackets, gender split, top countries, and what they buy because of your niche. A brand manager needs this to justify the spend internally, and it is what turns a pitch from a stats dump into a business case. |
| Sponsorships you have already done | Optional | Brand, what they paid, what they got, whether they renewed. Renewals are the strongest single line in a pitch, and past rates stop the new card contradicting a number a buyer has already seen. |
| The brand you want to pitch, if you have one | Optional | Brand name and what they sell, in plain terms. Leave it blank and you get the rate card, the prospect list and a pitch skeleton keyed to a category rather than a company: we do not invent a brand to have something to write to. |
| What that brand is actually running right now | Optional | A launch, a sale, a hashtag push, a rebrand, a cause they are backing. Two minutes on their Instagram or homepage is worth more than any amount of polish: this one detail is the whole reason the email does not read like the other fifty in the inbox. If you leave it blank we say so in the draft and tell you exactly what to go and look for; we never guess at a campaign, because a wrong guess reads worse than a generic email. |
| What you would actually make for them | Optional | "A 90-second unboxing segment in my Friday gear video" gets a reply. "I'd love to feature your product" does not. One line is enough. |
| Where you are with this brand | Optional | Changes the whole email, not just the opening. A reply to an inbound enquiry never quotes a rate first; a counter-offer leads with arithmetic; a renewal leads with what the last campaign did. Options: cold, warm_prior_contact, they_reached_out_first, renewing_a_past_deal, countering_a_low_offer. |
| The offer you have been made, if there is one | Optional | What they offered, for what deliverables, and any strings: exclusivity, whitelisting, usage rights, revisions, timeline. We check it against your own floor and tell you plainly whether it is worth taking, worth countering, or worth walking away from. |
| What a signed sponsor requires you to say | Optional | Paste their brief exactly: mandatory claims, the offer, the promo code, the URL and any legal or disclosure language. Every one of these survives the rewrite word for word, and anything that clashes with how you talk gets flagged back to you rather than quietly dropped. |
| Products you genuinely use and would recommend | Optional | One per line. These are your warmest prospects and the honest source for the one line in a pitch that actually converts. With no sponsor signed, the placement copy is written for these or for your own offer instead. |
| How you actually write | Optional | Two or three sentences in your normal register, straight off a caption or a newsletter issue. This beats any tone preset for the placement copy, which is the artifact where sounding like a media buyer is the whole failure. |
| Anything you will not take money from | Optional | Categories, claim types, competitor comparisons, a register that does not fit your channel. These are honoured absolutely, they are excluded from the prospect list with a note, and where one clashes with something a signed sponsor requires we say so rather than choosing for you. |

## Return a ready-to-use brief

Organize the supplied inputs under the field names above. Keep source material separate from instructions. Mark omissions and unsupported claims. Do not invent the creator's experience, audience data, brand assets, or results.

Open [Creator Sponsorship Engine](https://creatorskills.co/create/creator-sponsorship-engine) when the creator wants to run it. Confirm current cost and user authorization before spending credits. Do not enter private source material into the site without authorization.

A prepared brief is the deliverable here. Claim an app result only after a real run returns the output. If the app is unavailable, retain the brief and explain the unavailable step. If the user wants an in-chat draft instead, use the appropriate bundled workflow and label it as that.
