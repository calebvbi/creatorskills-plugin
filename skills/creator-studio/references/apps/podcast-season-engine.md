# Prepare Podcast Season Engine

Product description: Plan a whole podcast season in one sitting: a throughline every episode has to serve, 8-12 mapped episodes with real format variety, a calendar built on the time you actually have, and the trailer and promo arc that launch it.

This describes the hosted product, not an output already produced or a capability verified in this conversation.

**Mode: hosted handoff.** Hosted app; may require sign-in and credits. Check the page for current availability and cost. Prepare inputs and open app. This plugin does not execute the hosted pipeline.

## Collect the useful inputs

Use information already supplied. Ask only for missing required inputs. When both a URL and source text are offered, use the source the user actually provided. A URL is not evidence that its contents were read.

| Input | Required | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your show, in a couple of sentences | Required | What the show is about, who listens, and your usual format (solo, interview, co-hosted, mixed). "A weekly solo-and-occasional-interview show about freelance consulting, for people two to five years in" beats "a business podcast": every episode on the map has to earn its place with that listener. |
| Why a season, and why this one | Required | The topic, question, or shift in your own thinking that made you want to plan a season instead of picking a topic each week. This becomes the throughline every episode has to serve. Half-formed is fine: "something about what breaks when you try to hire" is enough to work from. |
| Which season this is | Optional | "Season 4", "the first real season", or leave it blank. Only used for naming and for the callback beats in the promo plan. |
| How many episodes | Optional | Ten is the default and the shape most solo shows finish. Eight is the honest answer if you have never completed a season. Twelve needs either a co-host or batch recording. Options: eight, ten, twelve. |
| Formats you can actually produce | Optional | Solo, interview, co-host, listener Q&A, panel, narrative, live. One per line. We never put a format on the map you did not name: twelve interview slots on a show that cannot book guests is the failure this question exists to prevent. |
| Guests you can realistically book this season | Optional | Be honest rather than optimistic. This number caps how many guest-dependent episodes go on the map, and guest-dependent episodes are the ones that get flagged as at-risk. Options: none, one_or_two, three_to_five, six_plus. |
| Topics or guests you already know you want in | Optional | One per line. These get placed where they serve the arc rather than where they land alphabetically, and anything that fights the throughline gets flagged rather than quietly dropped. |
| What earlier seasons or episodes already covered | Optional | Titles, themes, or a rough list. Used to keep the new season off ground you have already worked, and to write the "what this season is NOT about" line that stops episodes drifting later. |
| How often you publish | Optional | Weekly is the default. Biweekly is what most solo hosts can actually hold for ten straight episodes, and a calendar you keep beats a cadence you announce. Options: weekly, biweekly, monthly, seasonal_batch. |
| How long one episode really takes you, start to finish | Optional | Recording time, editing time, and how long guests take to get on your calendar: your real numbers, not the good week. "Record 90 min, edit 4 hours over 3 days, guests take 3 weeks to schedule" is exactly right. Leave it blank and we state the model we assumed at the top of the calendar so you can correct it in one line. |
| When the season starts (or has to finish) | Optional | A start date, or a finale date if you are working backward from something fixed. Blank means we build the calendar in relative weeks from your first recording block. |
| Weeks you already know you lose | Optional | Travel, holidays, a launch, anything that will eat a production week. These are where seasons slip, so the calendar routes around them instead of pretending they are not coming. |
| Who does the work | Optional | Solo means the lead times stay long and the calendar recommends batch recording. An editor changes the edit-complete column and nothing else. Options: solo, editor_only, small_team. |
| Where the show is | Optional | A new show gets the launch checklist: trailer, directory submissions, the first-three-episodes plan. An ongoing show gets a promo arc that assumes an audience already exists, which is a different plan, not a shorter one. Options: launching_new_show, restarting_after_a_gap, ongoing_show. |
| Where you actually post | Optional | Instagram, X, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, LinkedIn, a newsletter: only the ones you genuinely use. We would rather write four good beats for two platforms than a plan across five you will abandon in week two. |
| Promo posts a week you can sustain | Optional | Through the whole season, not launch week. The plan is sized to this number and includes a repeatable weekly template so the middle stretch takes under ten minutes. Options: one, two_or_three, four_plus. |
| Where your numbers are now | Optional | Roughly what an episode does and how big your following is per platform. Starting small is completely fine and changes how aggressive the launch push is. These are the only audience figures that can appear anywhere in the output: we never invent one. |
| How the show sounds | Optional | Mostly matters for the trailer script, which is the one artifact here you read aloud. Pick match_my_show if you described the show's voice above. Options: conversational, warm_reflective, high_energy, deadpan_dry, journalistic, match_my_show. |

## Return a ready-to-use brief

Organize the supplied inputs under the field names above. Keep source material separate from instructions. Mark omissions and unsupported claims. Do not invent the creator's experience, audience data, brand assets, or results.

Open [Podcast Season Engine](https://creatorskills.co/create/podcast-season-engine) when the creator wants to run it. Confirm current cost and user authorization before spending credits. Do not enter private source material into the site without authorization.

A prepared brief is the deliverable here. Claim an app result only after a real run returns the output. If the app is unavailable, retain the brief and explain the unavailable step. If the user wants an in-chat draft instead, use the appropriate bundled workflow and label it as that.
