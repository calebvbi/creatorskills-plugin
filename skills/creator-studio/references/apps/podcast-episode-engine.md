# Prepare Podcast Episode & Guest Engine

Product description: Everything between an idea and hitting record: a timed segment plan with the opening line written out, questions that track your own segments, and the pitch, follow-ups and briefing email that get the guest into the chair.

This describes the hosted product, not an output already produced or a capability verified in this conversation.

**Mode: hosted handoff.** Hosted app; may require sign-in and credits. Check the page for current availability and cost. Prepare inputs and open app. This plugin does not execute the hosted pipeline.

## Collect the useful inputs

Use information already supplied. Ask only for missing required inputs. When both a URL and source text are offered, use the source the user actually provided. A URL is not evidence that its contents were read.

| Input | Required | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What this episode is about | Required | A working title or a sentence. "Getting your first hundred reviews without begging your audience" beats "podcast growth": the segment questions are cut from this, so the sharper it is the less generic the plan. |
| Your show and who listens | Required | The show in a line, then who listens and what they already know. "Indie podcasters, three to twelve months in, past the setup questions" changes every talking point: a plan written for a beginner audience and a plan written for a working one share no sentences. |
| Format | Optional | Defaults to interview. Solo and co-host skip the guest questions and get an opening line written as a direct address instead; roundtable gets a moderator framework with rotation and tension prompts. Options: solo, interview, co_host, roundtable. |
| Target length | Optional | Defaults to forty-five minutes. This sets the segment count directly: twenty gets three segments, forty-five gets five: because a 45-minute episode split three ways is a fifteen-minute segment nobody can hold. Options: twenty, thirty, forty_five, sixty, ninety. |
| Guest name and role | Optional | Leave it blank for a solo episode and you still get a full run: the guest step builds the scoring rubric for who you should be targeting, and the outreach kit writes the pitch you send the next one. |
| What you know about the guest | Optional | Their bio, recent work, a talk or article, past podcast appearances, anything they have said that you want to press on. Paste it rather than summarising it. This is the only source we will quote them from: we do not look anything up, and we will never invent a book, a job or an appearance to make a pitch land. Leave it blank and you get a short list of exactly what to go and find, with the pitch written around a marked gap. |
| What a listener can do after this episode | Optional | One thing, stated as an action. Every segment gets checked against it, and the segments that do not move a listener toward it get cut or flagged. |
| What makes this different from the other fifty episodes on this topic | Optional | A disagreement, a specific case, a number you have that nobody else does. Leave it blank and we propose the angle and show our reasoning so you can overrule it. |
| Segments or subtopics you already have | Optional | One per line. If you give us more than the length allows we say so and recommend what to cut or combine rather than cramming them all in. |
| What the last episode covered | Optional | Used to open with a short callback and close with a forward tease, which is the cheapest retention move on a series and the one most often skipped. |
| What you want listeners to do at the end | Optional | Exactly one gets written out verbatim. A list of options at the end of an episode converts on none of them. Options: subscribe, join_email_list, buy_or_book, join_community, listen_to_related_episode, leave_a_review. |
| Where you are with this guest | Optional | Not contacted gets the cold pitch and the three follow-ups. Confirmed gets the booking confirmation and the pre-recording briefing email instead. No guest gets the templates for the next one. Options: not_contacted, pitched_no_reply, confirmed, no_guest. |
| How much of a public figure they are | Optional | Sets the formality and length of the follow-ups. A major public figure's inbox is managed by someone else, so every touch gets shorter and the ask gets easier to say yes to. Options: peer_or_micro_creator, industry_expert_or_author, major_public_figure. |
| Numbers or names you can honestly cite | Optional | Downloads an episode, subscriber count, a guest people would recognise, a milestone. These are the only figures that can appear in a pitch: we will not round them up or invent one. A small show described honestly outperforms an inflated number that a guest can check. |
| Anything off limits | Optional | Controversies, work they have moved on from, subjects they have asked not to cover. These get a handle-with-care note with a redirect line, rather than being silently omitted where you might wander into them live. |
| How you sound on air | Optional | Defaults to conversational. This governs the opening line and the intro script most of all, since those are the two you read word for word. Options: conversational, warm_reflective, high_energy, deadpan_dry, journalistic, no_nonsense. |

## Return a ready-to-use brief

Organize the supplied inputs under the field names above. Keep source material separate from instructions. Mark omissions and unsupported claims. Do not invent the creator's experience, audience data, brand assets, or results.

Open [Podcast Episode & Guest Engine](https://creatorskills.co/create/podcast-episode-engine) when the creator wants to run it. Confirm current cost and user authorization before spending credits. Do not enter private source material into the site without authorization.

A prepared brief is the deliverable here. Claim an app result only after a real run returns the output. If the app is unavailable, retain the brief and explain the unavailable step. If the user wants an in-chat draft instead, use the appropriate bundled workflow and label it as that.
