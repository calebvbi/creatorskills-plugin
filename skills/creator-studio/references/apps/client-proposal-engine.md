# Prepare Client Proposal Engine

Product description: Turn one client conversation into the priced proposal you send today: their problem in their words, a scoped deliverable list with the exclusions written in, a rate you can defend, and the follow-up and objection replies for everything that happens after you hit send.

This describes the hosted product, not an output already produced or a capability verified in this conversation.

**Mode: hosted handoff.** Hosted app; may require sign-in and credits. Check the page for current availability and cost. Prepare inputs and open app. This plugin does not execute the hosted pipeline.

## Collect the useful inputs

Use information already supplied. Ask only for missing required inputs. When both a URL and source text are offered, use the source the user actually provided. A URL is not evidence that its contents were read.

| Input | Required | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What the client needs, in their words | Required | Paste their inquiry, your call notes, the Slack message: whatever they actually wrote or said. Do not tidy it up. The opening of every proposal that wins restates the client's problem in the client's own language, and this box is the only place that language exists. |
| What you are selling | Required | Sets the market-rate band we price against and the deliverable conventions we write to. Pick "other" and we price from your own stated rate instead of a band, and say so in the document rather than inventing a benchmark. Options: video_production, social_media_management, newsletter_writing, content_strategy, brand_ghostwriting, podcast_production, other. |
| Project or monthly retainer | Optional | "Project" writes a scoped one-off with a deposit structure. "Retainer" scores the relationship first, then writes a monthly offer with a minimum term and a cancellation clause. Leave it blank and we read it off your brief: a defined endpoint reads as project, a recurring need reads as retainer. Options: project, retainer, decide_for_me. |
| Your rate, or what you want to charge | Optional | A number, a range, or what you charged them last time. Leave it blank and we price from the market band for your service and show you the arithmetic: we would rather show the maths than quote a figure you cannot defend on a call. |
| Your history with this client | Optional | How long, how many projects, what they paid, whether they pay on time, how their briefs and feedback run, and anything they have said about future work. This is the whole input to the retainer candidate score: eight signals for, five against, and it is what decides whether we tell you to pitch monthly or stay on projects. |
| What you would actually produce | Optional | Counts, formats, turnaround, revision rounds. Exact numbers, not ranges: "10-15 posts" is the single most common way a freelance scope gets eaten. Leave it blank and we propose a scope from the service conventions and mark it for you to check. |
| Timeline and any deadline driving it | Optional | A launch, a season, an event, or just when they want to start. A three-line timeline in a proposal removes more hesitation than another paragraph of approach. |
| Your relevant experience and results | Optional | Client types, platforms, years, and any result you can point at with a real number. This box is the ONLY source for the credibility section: we will not invent a past client or a growth figure, and if you leave it blank we write the short honest version instead. |
| What the client's business does | Optional | Their product, audience, and what they are trying to grow. This is what turns a list of services into an offer aimed at their actual goal. |
| Who decides, and what you know about budget | Optional | Who signs off, whether they are comparing quotes, anything said about money. It changes the close and it decides which objection scripts we write for you. |
| What they have already pushed back on | Optional | "Higher than expected", "can you do less", "we are talking to a few people", "we do not have consistent work". Paste it and we write the reply to the objection you actually got instead of the generic five. |
| How you want to send it | Optional | A full document for a scoped engagement, a short email for a small job, or a packaged rate card when they have asked "what do you charge?". Leave it blank and we pick from the size of the engagement. Options: full_proposal, quick_email, rate_card, decide_for_me. |
| Give them tiered options | Optional | Builds Starter / Core / Premium with a comparison card, Core marked as recommended. Use it when they have pushed back on price or scope, for a first pitch, one clear option closes faster than three. |
| Contract terms you already work under | Optional | Dispute resolution, IP and usage ownership, confidentiality, late-payment interest, priority access: whatever your contract or your last engagement already says, one per line. Anything you name here is written into the terms list as your term. Anything you leave out is listed as a decision for you to make, because a clause we invented is one a client could hold you to. |

## Return a ready-to-use brief

Organize the supplied inputs under the field names above. Keep source material separate from instructions. Mark omissions and unsupported claims. Do not invent the creator's experience, audience data, brand assets, or results.

Open [Client Proposal Engine](https://creatorskills.co/create/client-proposal-engine) when the creator wants to run it. Confirm current cost and user authorization before spending credits. Do not enter private source material into the site without authorization.

A prepared brief is the deliverable here. Claim an app result only after a real run returns the output. If the app is unavailable, retain the brief and explain the unavailable step. If the user wants an in-chat draft instead, use the appropriate bundled workflow and label it as that.
