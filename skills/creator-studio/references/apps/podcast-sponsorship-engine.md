# Prepare Podcast Sponsorship Engine

Product description: Everything between having listeners and having income: what to charge with the arithmetic shown, which brands to approach and how to check they buy, the pitch that states a rate, and host-read scripts that keep every claim the sponsor requires.

This describes the hosted product, not an output already produced or a capability verified in this conversation.

**Mode: hosted handoff.** Hosted app; may require sign-in and credits. Check the page for current availability and cost. Prepare inputs and open app. This plugin does not execute the hosted pipeline.

## Collect the useful inputs

Use information already supplied. Ask only for missing required inputs. When both a URL and source text are offered, use the source the user actually provided. A URL is not evidence that its contents were read.

| Input | Required | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your show and its niche | Required | Be specific: "business" is not a niche. "Tax and bookkeeping for freelance designers" and "comedy interviews with musicians" sit at opposite ends of the CPM range, and a rate card built on the wrong one is wrong by a factor of three. |
| What an episode actually does | Required | Downloads per episode in the first thirty days, and whether that number is growing, flat, or falling. Round numbers are fine, guesses are not: every rate on your card is computed from this and it is the first thing a buyer asks for. Trend matters as much as size; a growing show prices better than a plateaued one twice its size. |
| How often you publish | Optional | Sets episodes per month, which is the multiplier on every package price. Weekly by default. Options: weekly, biweekly, monthly, daily, seasonal. |
| Newsletter list, if you have one | Optional | A list is a separate line item priced at its own CPM and it is the cheapest thing to add to a package. Leave it blank if you do not have one. |
| Anything that shows people actually listen | Optional | Reviews, average completion, a community, social following, replies. Buyers weigh this third after alignment and volume, and it is how a small show outbids a bigger one. Only what you can point at: we never estimate an engagement number. |
| Sponsorships you have already done | Optional | Brand, what they paid, what they got, whether they renewed. Renewals are the single strongest thing in a pitch, and past rates stop the new card contradicting a number a buyer has already seen. |
| What you want this to earn | Optional | Changes which streams get recommended and in what order, not what your show is worth. The rate card is the same either way. Options: side_revenue, replace_part_of_my_income, replace_my_income, scale_to_a_media_business. |
| Products you genuinely use and would recommend | Optional | One per line. These are the affiliate and sponsor candidates worth chasing, and if you have no sponsor yet they are what the ad scripts are written for, so you have a house read ready. |
| The sponsor, if you already have one | Optional | Brand name and what they do, in plain terms. Leave it blank and the ad scripts are written for your own offer or an affiliate instead: we do not invent a sponsor to have something to write. |
| What the sponsor requires you to say | Optional | Paste their brief exactly: mandatory claims, the offer, the promo code, the URL, and any legal or disclosure language. Every one of these survives the rewrite word for word, and anything that clashes with how you talk gets flagged back to you rather than quietly dropped. |
| Your own experience with the product | Optional | What you actually use it for, what it replaced, what it fixed, how long you have had it. This is the line that converts, and it is the one line we will not write for you: leave it blank and the script marks the spot and tells you exactly what to say from your own use. We will never put an experience you did not report into your mouth. |
| Which placements you sell | Optional | All by default. Each placement gets its own opening and closing move rather than one script trimmed three ways, because a pre-roll and a post-roll are talking to listeners in completely different states. Options: all, pre_roll_only, mid_roll_only, pre_and_mid_roll. |
| How ads go into your episodes | Optional | Baked-in ads stay in the file forever and carry a premium over dynamic ones for exactly that reason. Not sure is a fine answer and the card prices both. Options: baked_in, dynamic_insertion, both, not_sure. |
| How you actually talk | Optional | Two or three sentences in your normal register, straight off a transcript if you have one. This beats any tone preset for the ad scripts, which are the artifacts where sounding like a voiceover artist is the whole failure. |
| Anything you will not say on air | Optional | Overclaims, competitor comparisons, categories you will not take money from, a register that does not fit the show. These are honoured absolutely, and where one clashes with something the sponsor requires we say so rather than choosing for you. |

## Return a ready-to-use brief

Organize the supplied inputs under the field names above. Keep source material separate from instructions. Mark omissions and unsupported claims. Do not invent the creator's experience, audience data, brand assets, or results.

Open [Podcast Sponsorship Engine](https://creatorskills.co/create/podcast-sponsorship-engine) when the creator wants to run it. Confirm current cost and user authorization before spending credits. Do not enter private source material into the site without authorization.

A prepared brief is the deliverable here. Claim an app result only after a real run returns the output. If the app is unavailable, retain the brief and explain the unavailable step. If the user wants an in-chat draft instead, use the appropriate bundled workflow and label it as that.
