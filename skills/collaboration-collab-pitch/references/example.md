# Worked Examples — Collaboration & Collab Pitch in Action

## Example 1: Fitness YouTuber Pitching a Nutrition Creator

### Creator Context

- **Platform:** YouTube
- **Channel:** Home workout routines for people who hate the gym
- **Subscribers:** 34,000
- **Average views per video:** 11,200
- **Engagement rate:** 6.1% (average likes: 510, average comments: 175, per video)
- **Upload frequency:** Twice per week (Tuesday and Friday)
- **Past collabs:** One — appeared on a friend's fitness podcast (small, ~2K listeners). It was informal and fun but didn't bring noticeable growth.
- **Goal:** Collaborate with a nutrition/meal prep creator to cross-pollinate audiences and add variety to his content.

---

### Target Partner Analysis

**Target creator:** Sarah — runs a meal prep channel focused on high-protein recipes for busy people.

- **Platform:** YouTube
- **Subscribers:** 52,000
- **Average views per video:** 16,800
- **Niche:** Meal prep for active people — batch cooking, macros, grocery hauls
- **Content style:** Practical, no-nonsense, lots of kitchen footage with voiceover. Clean editing, not overly produced.
- **Recent video of note:** "5 Meals I Eat Every Week to Hit 150g Protein" (41K views — well above her average)

#### Compatibility Score

| Factor | Score | Notes |
|--------|-------|-------|
| Audience relevance | 9/10 | Her viewers work out. His viewers need to eat. The overlap is natural — people interested in home fitness care about what to eat around their workouts. |
| Size compatibility | 8/10 | She's about 1.5x his size. Well within the 3x rule. She's big enough that the collab is aspirational but not unrealistic. |
| Content style match | 7/10 | Both are practical and straightforward. His energy is a bit higher (he's on camera doing exercises), but the tones aren't clashing. A collab would feel natural, not forced. |
| Engagement quality | 8/10 | Her comment sections are active with real questions about recipes and macros. Not just "great video" comments. Her audience is engaged and asks for recommendations. |
| Collab history | 7/10 | She's done 2 visible collabs in the past year — a guest spot on another food channel and a joint live stream. She's open to it but not doing collabs constantly. |

**Overall: 78% — strong match. Worth pitching.**

The key selling point: her audience already works out (they're searching for protein-focused meal prep because they train). His content is the other half of their routine. This isn't a stretch — it's filling a gap her audience already has.

---

### Outreach — Cold DM (Instagram)

```
Hey Sarah — your "5 Meals for 150g Protein" video is the reason
I stopped eating sad chicken and rice every day. The turkey
meatball meal prep is genuinely in my weekly rotation now.

I run a home workout channel (34K subs, 11K avg views) and my
audience constantly asks me what I eat. I'd love to do a collab
where you build a week of meals around one of my workout
programs — "what to eat if you're doing this 4-day split." We'd
each post our own version to both channels.

Would you be up for chatting about it?
```

**Why this works:**
- Opens with a specific video and a specific recipe — proves he's actually watched her content
- States his credentials in one parenthetical, doesn't dwell on it
- Proposes a concept that benefits her (her audience wants workout programs to pair with their meal plans) not just him
- 97 words — she can read it in 30 seconds and decide

---

### Collab Plan

**Concept:** "What I Eat in a Week on the [Program Name] Workout Plan" (Sarah's video) + "The Complete Home Training Plan for [Meal Plan Name]" (Marcus's video)

**Format:** Joint video — each creator films their own portion and publishes to their own channel. They don't need to be in the same room.

| Detail | Sarah's Video | Marcus's Video |
|--------|--------------|----------------|
| **Content** | Full week of meals designed for his 4-day split: training day meals, rest day meals, macros for each | The 4-day workout split filmed start to finish, with callouts to Sarah's meals at each session |
| **Length** | 12-15 minutes (her standard) | 15-18 minutes (his standard) |
| **CTA to partner** | "Marcus designed this workout program — full video on his channel, link in description" | "Sarah built every meal you just saw me eat — full recipes on her channel, link in description" |
| **Thumbnail** | Split screen: meals on left, Marcus mid-workout on right. Both faces visible. | Split screen: Marcus mid-workout on left, meals on right. Both faces visible. |

**Timeline:**
- Week 1: Agree on the workout split and meal plan framework over a video call (30 min max)
- Week 2: Each films their portion independently
- Week 3: Edit. Share rough cuts with each other for approval by Wednesday.
- Week 4: Both publish on the same Tuesday. Each posts an Instagram Story cross-promoting the other's video on publish day.

**Promotion plan:**
- Both pin a comment on their video linking to the partner's video
- Both post one Instagram Story on publish day tagging the other
- Both mention the partner's video verbally in the first 60 seconds of their own video
- Neither has to post on platforms they're not already active on

---

### Deal Structure

| Term | Agreement |
|------|-----------|
| **Revenue** | Each keeps their own ad revenue. No split needed — both publish to their own channel. |
| **Credit** | Both creators named in both titles and thumbnails. |
| **Promotion** | Pinned comment, one IG Story each, verbal mention in video. |
| **Content approval** | Each reviews the other's rough cut before publish. Either can request edits to how they're represented. |
| **Exclusivity** | None. Both are free to collab with anyone else. |
| **If it doesn't work out** | If either creator isn't happy with the final content, they can choose not to publish. No hard feelings. |

---

### Why This Works

The pitch succeeds because it's built on a genuine audience gap. Marcus's viewers literally ask him about nutrition in his comments — this isn't a forced connection. Sarah's audience trains at home but she doesn't cover workout programming. The collab fills a real need on both sides.

The format is low-friction: nobody has to travel, nobody has to edit the other person's footage, and both videos stand alone as strong content even if viewers only watch one. That's the mark of a good collab — each video is genuinely useful by itself, but watching both gives you the complete picture.

## Example 2: Tech Reviewer Pitching a Software Company's YouTube Channel

### Creator Context

- **Platform:** YouTube (primary), Twitter/X (secondary)
- **Channel:** Reviews productivity apps, browser extensions, and workflow tools for remote workers
- **Subscribers:** 18,500
- **Average views per video:** 6,800
- **Engagement rate:** 7.2% (average likes: 340, average comments: 150, per video)
- **Upload frequency:** Weekly (every Monday)
- **Past collabs:** None with other creators. Has done two sponsored videos with SaaS companies.
- **Goal:** Collaborate with a larger tech channel that covers a broader range of tools to reach a wider productivity audience.

---

### Target Partner Analysis

**Target creator:** Dev & Deploy — a channel run by two developers who review dev tools, project management software, and tech stacks for small teams.

- **Platform:** YouTube
- **Subscribers:** 47,000
- **Average views per video:** 14,300
- **Niche:** Developer tools and team productivity software — reviews, tutorials, comparisons
- **Content style:** Casual pair format (two hosts bantering). Mid-level production. Good pacing, natural delivery.
- **Recent video of note:** "We Tried 7 Project Management Tools — Here's What Actually Worked" (68K views, way above their average)

#### Compatibility Score

| Factor | Score | Notes |
|--------|-------|-------|
| Audience relevance | 8/10 | Their audience uses dev tools daily and cares about productivity workflows. His audience is remote workers using similar tools from a different angle. Strong overlap in the "people who care about working efficiently" bucket. |
| Size compatibility | 8/10 | They're about 2.5x his size. Within the 3x rule. They're established but not so big that a collab pitch would be unrealistic. |
| Content style match | 6/10 | His style is solo presenter, methodical reviews. Their style is two-host, looser format. The tones could mesh but they'd need to plan around the format difference. A guest appearance on their show would work better than trying to merge styles. |
| Engagement quality | 9/10 | Their comment sections are full of specific tool recommendations and workflow debates. Real engagement from real users. The kind of audience that actively shares links they find useful. |
| Collab history | 7/10 | They've had guests on their channel twice before — both times as a "third chair" in their duo format. They're familiar with the format and seem to enjoy it. |

**Overall: 76% — good match, especially for a guest appearance format.**

The style mismatch lowers the score slightly, but a guest appearance (him joining their duo format for an episode) avoids the problem entirely. He doesn't need to match their energy — he just needs to bring expertise they don't have (deep knowledge of individual productivity apps for non-developers).

---

### Outreach — Cold Email

**Subject line:** The app stack video that got 68K views — guest idea

```

Idea: I come on as a guest for an episode where we build the

my audience gets introduced to the dev tool perspective.

I can prep my tool picks in advance and send them over so we're
not overlapping. Happy to work around your recording schedule.

Worth a chat?

Jake
[Channel Name] | 18.5K subscribers
jake@[channel].com
```

**Why this works:**
- Opens by referencing their most successful recent video with a specific, genuine reaction (bookmarked it, shared it with real people)
- Positions himself as bringing a complementary angle, not competing with their expertise
- The concept is specific and immediately filmable — "build the ultimate tool stack" is a natural fit for their format
- Offers to do prep work (sends picks in advance to avoid overlap) — shows he's thinking about making their job easier
- 162 words — quick read, clear pitch, easy to say yes to

### Collab Plan

**Concept:** "Building the Ultimate Remote Worker Tool Stack" — a guest episode on Dev & Deploy's channel where all three hosts contribute their picks across categories.

**Format:** Guest appearance on their channel. Jake joins their existing duo format as a third chair for one episode.

| Detail | Plan |
|--------|------|
| **Recording** | Remote via Riverside or Zencastr (their usual setup). 60-90 minute recording session, edited to 25-35 minutes. |
| **Structure** | 5 categories: Communication, Project Management, Note-Taking, Focus/Deep Work, Automation. Each host picks their top tool per category, brief debate, audience poll in comments. |
| **Prep** | Jake sends his 5 picks to the hosts 3 days before recording. They flag any overlaps and swap so each person has unique recommendations. |
| **Publish** | Publishes to Dev & Deploy's channel as a main episode. Jake shares it on his channel as a Community post and links it in his next video's description. |
| **CTA** | Dev & Deploy verbally intro Jake and his channel at the top. Jake's channel link in the description and pinned comment. |

**Timeline:**
- Day 1-3: Jake sends his 5 tool picks. Hosts compare and finalize the lineup.
- Day 5-7: Record the episode. Aim for 60-75 minutes of raw footage.
- Day 8-14: Dev & Deploy edits (it's their channel, their format, their editing style). Jake reviews the final cut for anything he wants adjusted.
- Day 15-17: Publish. Jake posts a Community tab teaser on his channel on the same day.

**If it goes well:** Discuss a Part 2 on Jake's channel where the Dev & Deploy hosts guest for him, covering "The Dev Tools Every Non-Developer Should Know." This turns a one-off into a two-part series that benefits both channels.

---

### Deal Structure

| Term | Agreement |
|------|-----------|
| **Revenue** | Dev & Deploy keeps all ad revenue (it's their channel and their edit). Jake gets audience exposure and a backlink. This is standard for guest appearances. |
| **Credit** | Jake named in the title (e.g., "Building the Ultimate Tool Stack ft. [Jake's Channel Name]"). His face in the thumbnail alongside the hosts. |
| **Promotion** | Dev & Deploy: pinned comment with Jake's channel link, verbal intro at the top of the video. Jake: Community post on publish day, link in his next video's description, tweet thread about the episode. |
| **Content approval** | Jake reviews the final edit before publish. Can request cuts to any segment where he misspoke or wants a correction. |
| **Exclusivity** | None. |
| **Follow-up collab** | If the episode performs well (above their average views), they'll discuss Part 2 on Jake's channel within 30 days. No obligation. |

---

### Why This Works

Jake is pitching up — Dev & Deploy is 2.5x his size. But the pitch works because he's not asking for equal billing. He's proposing a guest appearance on their show, in their format, that adds value they can't provide themselves (deep individual app reviews vs. their broader comparison style).

The format respects their workflow: they edit, they publish, they control the output. Jake's job is to show up prepared, be a good guest, and make the episode better than it would've been without him. That's the right posture when pitching someone bigger — make it easy to say yes, hard to say no.

The follow-up plan (Part 2 on Jake's channel) is smart but not presumptuous. He doesn't pitch it upfront — that would feel like he's angling for something bigger than what he's offering. He lets the first collab prove itself, then proposes the reverse when they've seen the results.
