# Caption Chain Generator: two source-grounded weekly plans

## Sample 1: beginner home baker

**Supplied facts:** The creator made three loaves. The first was dense in a cold kitchen; temperature was not measured. For the next two they moved dough near a warm, switched-off oven and changed flour. Both rose more. They cannot isolate the cause. Their tip is to record room temperature, flour and proofing time, changing one thing at a time. No sales offer or posting-time analytics supplied.

### Monday: observation

Three loaves, one weekend, and a reminder to keep better notes.

My first loaf stayed dense in a cold kitchen. The next two rose more after I moved the dough near a warm, switched-off oven. But I changed the flour too, so I can't tell which change mattered.

Next time: one change at a time, with notes.

**Visual:** Use actual loaf photos if available; otherwise a text card. Do not fabricate comparison photographs. **Timing:** Choose an available posting slot; no optimal-time claim.

### Wednesday: practical checklist

A bread experiment is easier to learn from when you write down what changed.

For my next batch, I'm tracking:
- Room temperature.
- Flour used.
- Proofing time.

Changing one thing at a time should make the comparison easier to interpret. It doesn't mean warmth alone fixes every dense loaf.

**CTA:** Save the checklist for your next bake.

### Friday: invitation

What do you record when you bake bread?

After changing both the flour and the dough's location in one weekend, I realized my notes couldn't tell me which difference mattered. I'm starting with temperature, flour and proofing time.

**CTA:** Share a detail you've found useful to track.

**Chain logic:** Observation → checklist → discussion. All personal statements come from supplied facts; no outcome multiplier is promised.

## Sample 2: new business coach without testimonials

**Supplied brief:** Help first-time founders talk to potential customers. Voice: direct and practical. Goal: introduce a free email course. No clients, results, personal failure story or course URL supplied.

### Monday: useful starting question

Before describing your product idea, ask someone who has the problem how they handle it today.

Try: “Walk me through the last time this happened.” Then listen for the steps, workarounds and frustrations. Their answer can help you decide what to investigate next.

**CTA:** Write down one question for your next conversation.

### Tuesday: explicitly fictional teaching example

Fictional example: a founder wants to build a booking app. Instead of asking “Would you buy this?”, they ask a salon owner how a booking changed yesterday.

That question can reveal the existing process. It does not establish demand by itself; the founder still needs to test the idea with real people.

**Visual:** Two question cards, not a fabricated customer portrait or testimonial.

### Wednesday: checklist

For your next customer conversation:
1. Ask about a recent event.
2. Separate their words from your interpretation.
3. Record what you still don't know.

One conversation is a starting point, not proof that a market exists.

### Thursday: practice prompt instead of a testimonial

Take your current pitch and turn it into a question about the person's experience.

“Would you use my invoicing tool?” could become “What happened the last time an invoice was paid late?”

Keep the conversation about their situation before you propose a solution.

### Friday: offer introduction

Want a structured way to prepare for a customer conversation? My free email course introduces the process of planning questions and reviewing what you learn.

[Confirm that this description matches the course and add its actual signup URL before publishing.]

**Scheduling note:** These are draft weekday slots. Choose dates and times using the creator's capacity and audience information. No posts were scheduled, and no messages were sent.

## Evidence check

Sample 1 uses the supplied baking observations without removing their uncertainty. Sample 2 contains no invented client, revenue result, personal history or endorsement. Its teaching scenario is clearly fictional in the publishable copy itself.
