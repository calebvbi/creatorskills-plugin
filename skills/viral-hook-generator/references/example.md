# Viral Hook Generator — Worked Examples

Two complete examples showing the system in action across different niches and content styles.

---

## Example 1: Fitness Creator — YouTube Shorts About Workout Tips

### Creator Input

```
I'm a fitness creator making YouTube Shorts for beginners. My audience is mostly
guys aged 18-30 who want to build muscle but don't have much gym experience.
Give me hooks for a Short about the 3 exercises that build the most muscle.
```

### Generated Output

## Your Hooks: 3 Best Muscle-Building Exercises for Beginners

**Hook 1 — The Controversy Hook**
"The exercise every gym bro tells you to start with is actually the worst one for beginners."

**Hook 2 — The Authority Hook**
"After training over 200 beginners, these are the only 3 exercises I have all of them start with."

**Hook 3 — The List Hook**
"3 exercises that build more muscle than everything else in the gym combined — and you probably aren't doing any of them."

**Hook 4 — The Fear Hook**
"If you're spending hours at the gym and not doing these 3 exercises, you're basically wasting your time."

**Hook 5 — The Tutorial Hook**
"Here's how to build the most muscle possible in the gym with just 3 exercises — takes 30 minutes."

---

### Best for Your Content
Go with Hook 2 (Authority Hook). Your beginner audience needs to trust you before they'll change their workout, and "trained over 200 beginners" gives you instant credibility. It also creates curiosity — they want to know which 3 exercises a real trainer picks when everything else is stripped away. Hook 3 is your runner-up if you want something more punchy and less credential-dependent.

---

### Why This Works

**Archetype selection was tailored to the audience.** Beginners respond to authority and clear structure more than shock or controversy. Someone new to the gym doesn't yet have strong opinions to challenge, but they DO want to trust that their time in the gym counts. That's why the Authority Hook and the Fear Hook (wasting gym time) both resonate with this specific audience.

**The hooks are spoken-word ready.** Each one sounds natural when said out loud. Notice there's no complex sentence structure or written-language phrasing — these are designed to be delivered on camera in 2-3 seconds.

**Specificity drives credibility.** "200 beginners" is more compelling than "many clients." "30 minutes" is more compelling than "not much time." "3 exercises" is more compelling than "a few exercises." Every number in these hooks serves a purpose: it makes the promise feel real and measurable.

**Each hook creates a different flavor of curiosity:**
- Hook 1: "Wait, which exercise is wrong? Am I doing it?"
- Hook 2: "What does an actual expert choose when forced to pick only 3?"
- Hook 3: "Am I missing the most effective exercises?"
- Hook 4: "Am I wasting my gym time without knowing it?"
- Hook 5: "I can do this in just 30 minutes? Show me how."

All five keep the viewer watching, but through different psychological entry points.

## Example 2: Comedy/Entertainment Creator — TikToks About Daily Life

### Creator Input

```
I make funny TikToks about the struggles of working from home. My audience
is mostly millennials and Gen Z. Give me hooks for a video about pretending
to work during meetings.
```

### Generated Output

## Your Hooks: Pretending to Work During Meetings

**Hook 1 — The Relatable Hook**
"POV: You've been on mute for 45 minutes and someone just said your name."

**Hook 2 — The Story Hook**
"So yesterday I accidentally unmuted during a meeting and my boss heard me placing a DoorDash order."

**Hook 3 — The Question Hook**
"What's the most unhinged thing you've done during a work meeting? Because I just hit a new personal record."

**Hook 4 — The Callout Hook**
"This is for anyone who has mastered the art of nodding on camera while doing absolutely nothing."

**Hook 5 — The Shock Hook**
"I just realized my camera was on for the last 20 minutes and I was lying on my couch the entire time."

---

### Best for Your Content
Hook 1 (Relatable Hook) is your winner. Comedy content lives and dies on the "that's so me" reaction, and every remote worker has experienced the terror of hearing their name after zoning out. It also works perfectly as text-on-screen with a reaction clip underneath — the TikTok format practically builds itself around this hook. Hook 4 is a strong backup if you want to build community ("the nodding on camera" crowd will tag their coworkers).

---

### Why This Works

**The hooks match the comedic tone.** None of these feel like a business tutorial or a productivity video — they all carry a slightly chaotic, self-deprecating energy that matches "funny TikToks about WFH struggles." The language is casual: "unhinged," "absolutely nothing," "personal record." This is how the target audience actually talks.

**Platform awareness shaped the style.** These hooks are designed for TikTok specifically — short, punchy, and optimized for the scroll speed of the For You Page. Hook 1 uses the "POV:" format that TikTok audiences are already trained to engage with. Hook 3 invites comments, which signals engagement to the algorithm.

**Relatability scales through specificity.** "Pretending to work during meetings" is relatable. But "nodding on camera while doing absolutely nothing" is viscerally relatable — the reader can see themselves doing it. "Placing a DoorDash order on mute" is a hyper-specific scenario that feels like it actually happened, which makes it funnier.

**Each hook opens a different content structure:**
- Hook 1: Reaction/skit format — show the panic moment
- Hook 2: Storytime with a punchline — narrative comedy
- Hook 3: Comment-bait with a personal reveal — engagement driver
- Hook 4: Sketch/montage of meeting avoidance techniques — community builder
- Hook 5: Dramatic re-enactment of the horror — physical comedy

The same video topic produces completely different videos depending on which hook you choose. This is why generating 5 hooks matters — you're not just picking an opening line, you're choosing the structure and energy of the entire video.

---

## Key Takeaways From Both Examples

1. **Different niches need different archetypes.** Fitness beginners responded to Authority and Tutorial hooks. Comedy audiences responded to Relatable and Story hooks. The same 12 archetypes work everywhere, but the best archetype for any given video depends on the audience and the content.

2. **The recommendation matters as much as the hooks.** Five hooks is useful. Five hooks plus a clear explanation of which one to use and why — that's what saves creators from paralysis and helps them learn which patterns work for their content over time.

3. **Hooks shape the entire video.** A Story Hook leads to a different video than a List Hook, even if the topic is identical. The hook you choose determines the structure, pacing, and energy of everything that follows.
