## Example: "Sawdust & Spreadsheets" — Home Renovation Podcast, Season 3

### Input

```
I need to plan Season 3 of my podcast.

Show: Sawdust & Spreadsheets — a podcast about DIY home renovation for people who
manage their own projects (not full DIY, not full contractor-run). Usually a mix
of solo breakdowns and interviews with contractors/designers.

Usual format: mixed — about 60% solo, 40% interview
Target episode count: 10
What's on my mind: I keep getting the same question in my inbox — "how do I know
when to DIY something vs. hire it out, and how do I manage a contractor without
getting ripped off." I think that's actually the season.
Target listener for this season: people mid-renovation right now, not just
planning-stage dreamers
```

### Output: Season Premise Brief

**Season Premise: Season 3 — "The Line"**

**Show:** Sawdust & Spreadsheets | **Target episode count:** 10 | **Target listener for this season:** People currently mid-renovation, managing a mix of DIY work and hired contractors

---

**The Throughline (one sentence)**
This season answers: how do you know where the line is between "I should do this myself" and "I need to hire this out," and how do you manage the hired-out side without getting burned?

**Why This Season, Why Now**
This is the single most common question in the inbox — not "how do I tile a backsplash" but "should I even be the one tiling it." Listeners aren't stuck on skills, they're stuck on judgment calls, and nobody's making content about the judgment call itself. Fall is also peak "started a project over summer, now facing the parts I'm not sure about" season.

**The Season Arc**
This season moves thematically, not chronologically — each episode tackles a different category of the same decision (structural work, electrical, plumbing, finish carpentry, design decisions) rather than following one project start to finish. It opens by naming the actual decision framework, spends the middle stretch applying it category by category (often via interviews with tradespeople who see DIY-gone-wrong constantly), and closes with a synthesis episode on managing a contractor relationship once you've decided to hire out. A listener should end the season with an actual framework they can apply to their own house, not just more renovation trivia.

**What This Season Is NOT About**
- Not a full-project walkthrough of any single renovation — that's what standalone episodes outside the season already do
- Not about budgeting/financing a renovation — that was Season 1's focus and this season assumes budget is a separate, already-solved question

**Season Title Options**
1. **"The Line"** — Short, ownable, works as a recurring phrase in every episode ("today we're talking about the line on electrical work")
2. **"Should You Even Be Doing This"** — Direct, provocative, matches the show's slightly blunt voice
3. **"Hire It or DIY It"** — Clear and searchable, less distinctive as a season brand

**The One-Line Pitch**
"Season 3 is about the one renovation question nobody actually answers: not how to do the work, but whether you should be the one doing it."

### Output: Episode-by-Episode Topic Map

**Episode Map: Season 3 — "The Line"**

**Throughline:** How do you know where the line is between DIY and hiring out, and how do you manage the hired-out side without getting burned?
**Format mix for this season:** 6 solo, 3 interview, 1 co-host/finale synthesis (10 total)

| # | Working Title | Format | Core Question This Episode Answers | Where It Sits in the Arc |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "The Decision Framework" | Solo | What are the actual factors (skill, risk, code, time, resale) that should decide DIY vs. hire? | Premiere — introduces the framework every later episode applies |
| 2 | "Structural: The Line Nobody Should Cross Alone" | Interview (structural engineer) | What structural work is genuinely never a DIY call, and why do people misjudge it? | Applies the framework to the highest-stakes category first |
| 3 | "Electrical: Where the Line Actually Is" | Interview (licensed electrician) | What electrical work is legally/safely DIY-able vs. not, state by state? | Second high-stakes category, contrasts with structural |
| 4 | "Plumbing: The Skill Gap Is Smaller Than You Think" | Solo | Which plumbing tasks are realistically DIY-able with the right prep, and which aren't? | Introduces a category where the line moves further toward DIY |
| 5 | "The $400 Mistake I Almost Made" | Solo (narrative) | What does a near-miss DIY decision actually look like in real time? | Midpoint — personal story that makes the framework concrete |
| 6 | "Finish Carpentry: Why 'Easy' Work Still Goes Wrong" | Solo | Why do low-risk-seeming tasks (trim, doors, cabinetry) still get botched, and what's the real skill floor? | Shows the framework applies even to "safe" categories |
| 7 | "Design Decisions: The Line Isn't About Skill" | Interview (interior designer) | When should you hire out a decision (not labor) — layout, material choice, spatial planning? | Introduces a whole new axis: judgment work, not just manual work |
| 8 | "Vetting a Contractor Without Getting Burned" | Solo | What should you actually check before hiring — licensing, references, contract terms? | Turn — season shifts from "should you hire" to "how to hire well" |
| 9 | "Managing the Job Without Micromanaging" | Solo | Once you've hired someone, how do you stay involved without becoming the client everyone dreads? | Sets up the finale by resolving the practical relationship question |
| 10 | "The Line, Revisited" | Co-host synthesis | Looking back across the season, what's the actual one-sentence rule listeners should walk away with? | Finale — resolves the throughline directly |

**Format Variety Check**
No format repeats more than twice in a row (solo runs at Eps 4/5/6 are the longest stretch, broken up by narrative framing on Ep 5 so it doesn't feel repetitive). The finale deliberately shifts to a co-host format to signal "season closer" distinctly from the regular solo cadence.

**Episodes Most at Risk of Falling Through**
- **Episode 2 (structural engineer interview)** — Structural engineers are harder to book than contractors and typically need the most lead time. Start outreach for this one first.
- **Episode 7 (interior designer interview)** — Depends on finding someone willing to talk candidly about when clients shouldn't DIY design decisions, which is a slightly unusual ask; have a backup solo episode ready in case this doesn't land.

**Backup Episode Ideas**
1. "HVAC: The Category Everyone Underestimates" — solo, fits anywhere in the middle stretch if a guest falls through
2. "Permits: The Paperwork Line" — solo, could replace Ep 7 if the designer interview doesn't come together
3. Listener mailbag on DIY regrets — flexible, can slot in anywhere and doubles as light content if the host falls behind on research-heavy episodes

### Output: Publishing Calendar

**Publishing Calendar: Season 3 — "The Line"**

**Cadence:** Weekly | **Season runs:** Sept 8 → Nov 10 | **Total episodes:** 10

---

**Lead-Time Model Used**
Interview episodes need a guest booked at least 3 weeks before publish (tradespeople and specialists have unpredictable schedules). Recording happens 10 days before publish for all episodes. Editing takes 4 days. That leaves a 2-day buffer before publish for final listen-through and show notes.

**Recording Block Recommendation**
Batch-record the four solo episodes without narrative elements (Eps 1, 4, 6, 8) in two sessions of two, two weeks before the season starts — this takes the weekly grind off the calendar for nearly half the season. Record interviews closer to their individual publish dates since they depend on guest availability, and record Ep 5 (narrative) and Ep 9 close to their slots since both benefit from being written fresh.

**Pressure Points to Watch**
The week of Oct 10-17 stacks Episode 7's guest booking deadline (Sept 8, already passed by this point but the interview itself records Oct 10) directly against Episode 6's edit-complete deadline (Oct 9). If Ep 7 booking slipped, this is the week it would collide with editing crunch. Also watch late September — Ep 5's recording (Sept 26) lands right after Ep 4 publishes (Sept 29), leaving little slack if Ep 5's narrative writing takes longer than expected.

**Buffer Episode Rule**
If the season falls behind, Episode 6 (finish carpentry, standalone solo topic) is the safest to push a week — it doesn't set up or depend on any other episode in the arc, unlike Episodes 8 and 9 which are sequentially linked.
