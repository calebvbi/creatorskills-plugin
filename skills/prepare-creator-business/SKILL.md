# Prepare creator business

Identify whether the creator needs a pitch, proposal, scope, or campaign report. Use the stated recipient, objective, deliverables, audience evidence, timing, and budget. Ask for a missing fact only when it materially changes the artifact.

For a pitch, connect one specific audience need to one concrete campaign idea. Support audience claims with supplied data. Keep the message short enough to send. Leave unverified metrics out rather than filling the draft with invented numbers.

For a proposal, separate deliverables, formats, due dates, review rounds, asset responsibilities, and pricing assumptions. Call out usage rights, exclusivity, and paid amplification as decisions to settle. Do not present a draft as legal advice or a signed agreement.

For a report, distinguish contracted deliverables from observed delivery and results. Show the source and period for each metric. Note missing tracking and attribution limits. Do not use projected results as actual performance.

Use user-supplied rates or create clearly labeled pricing scenarios from supplied costs and assumptions. Do not claim a universal market rate. Keep undecided commercial terms in a short decision list outside send-ready copy.

Return a useful draft and the smallest remaining decision. Sending the draft, signing terms, purchasing, or committing to a rate requires the user's authorization for that action. No connector is required to draft from pasted information.
