# AI Title A/B Testing Framework — Worked Examples

Two examples showing how the framework works before a title goes live and after test data comes back.

---

## Example 1: Pre-publish YouTube title test

### Creator input

```text
I need help testing titles for a YouTube video.

Topic: I batch filmed YouTube Shorts for 30 days
Audience: creators trying to post more consistently
Current title: I Tried Batch Filming Shorts for 30 Days
Goal: better CTR without losing clarity
Keyword: batch filming shorts
```

### Generated output

## Title Test Framework: Batch Filming Shorts for 30 Days

**Platform:** YouTube
**Primary goal:** Higher CTR without losing clarity
**Audience:** creators trying to post more consistently

### Candidate Titles

| Variant | Angle | Title | Curiosity | Clarity | SEO | Click Potential | Weighted Score | Why It Could Work | Main Risk |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| A | Control / outcome-first | I Tried Batch Filming Shorts for 30 Days | 7 | 9 | 8 | 7 | 7.55 | Clear experiment framing with natural keyword use | Slightly flat emotionally |
| B | Curiosity-first | Batch Filming Shorts Changed My Channel Faster Than I Expected | 8 | 7 | 6 | 8 | 7.40 | Stronger curiosity and implied payoff | Keyword is weaker |
| C | Specificity-first | 30 Days of Batch Filming Shorts: What Actually Happened | 8 | 8 | 7 | 8 | 7.80 | Keeps the time frame while adding a clearer reveal | Slightly longer |
| D | Clarity-first | How I Batch Film a Month of Shorts in One Day | 6 | 10 | 8 | 7 | 7.65 | Very clear promise for practical viewers | Less intrigue |
| E | SEO-first | Batch Filming Shorts: How I Made 30 Days of Content Fast | 6 | 9 | 9 | 7 | 7.55 | Strong search fit with useful specificity | Feels a little mechanical |
| F | Tension-driven | Posting Daily Was Killing Me, So I Tried This Instead | 9 | 6 | 4 | 8 | 6.95 | High emotional pull for burnt-out creators | Too indirect for search |
| G | Outcome-first | I Batch Filmed 30 Days of Shorts and It Fixed My Workflow | 8 | 8 | 7 | 8 | 7.80 | Strong result framing and clear payoff | "Fixed" may overstate if results were mixed |
| H | Audience callout | If You Cannot Post Consistently, Try Batch Filming Shorts Like This | 7 | 8 | 8 | 7 | 7.35 | Speaks directly to the pain point | Longer and slightly instructional |

### Recommended Test Shortlist

- **Control:** Variant A — it already has solid clarity and good keyword placement.
- **Challenger 1:** Variant C — tests whether "What Actually Happened" adds useful curiosity without hurting clarity.
- **Challenger 2:** Variant D — tests whether a more practical, tutorial-style promise beats the experiment framing.

### Test Plan

- **Hypothesis:** A title that adds either a stronger reveal or a clearer practical payoff will outperform the current experiment-framed title.
- **What changed:** curiosity framing in Variant C and practical outcome framing in Variant D.
- **Primary metric:** CTR
- **Guardrail metric:** first 30-second retention
- **Run window:** at least 7 days, or until each variant reaches meaningful impression volume
- **Success criteria:** pick a winner only if CTR lift is meaningful and retention does not drop in a major way

### Recommendation

Start with Variant C as your first challenger. It keeps the same core idea as the control, which means the test teaches you something useful if it wins. If the audience responds to the stronger reveal language, you can push further on curiosity in future uploads without losing the keyword and experiment framing that already fit the topic.

## Example 2: Post-test title analysis

### Creator input

```text
Analyze these title test results for my article about creator burnout.

Control title:
"How to Avoid Creator Burnout When You Work From Home"

Variant B:
"The Work-From-Home Habit That Caused My Creator Burnout"

Variant C:
"Creator Burnout While Working From Home: What Finally Helped"

Results after 12 days:
- Control: 18,420 impressions, 812 clicks
- Variant B: 17,980 impressions, 1,006 clicks
- Variant C: 18,105 impressions, 905 clicks

Primary goal: better search CTR
Guardrail metric: average engagement time
Engagement time was stable across all three versions.
```

### Generated output

### Results Analysis

- **Control CTR:** 4.41%
- **Variant B CTR:** 5.60%
- **Variant C CTR:** 5.00%
- **Verdict:** Highest observed CTR; experiment comparability needs confirmation
- **Candidate to validate:** Variant B

### Hypothesis to test about Variant B

Variant B had roughly **27% higher observed CTR than the control**. Similar impression counts alone do not establish comparable groups. The supplied engagement time was stable, but that does not prove the copy is accurate or explain why people clicked. Assignment, traffic mix and exposure dates were not supplied, so this is an observed association rather than a causal conclusion.

One hypothesis is that the specific problem and personal framing affected response. The counts do not measure motivation or establish that explanation. Content differences, exposure, timing and audience composition remain alternative explanations.

### What to test next

Confirm that the underlying article supports every claim in each title. Document the assignment method and comparison window. Then test one wording change with the same content and suitable guardrails. Do not claim that this aggregate result proves the audience prefers personal proof or that a format will win on a new topic.
